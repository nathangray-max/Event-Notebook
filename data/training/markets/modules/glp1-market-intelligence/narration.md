# GLP-1 Market Intelligence: The GLP-1 Revolution. Narration

## Slide 1

Source: draft

This is our GLP-1 market intelligence report, "The GLP-1 Revolution", a training and intelligence report from March 2026. The deck calls GLP-1 the fastest-growing category in supplement history. It is marked confidential, so keep it inside the company. Over the next slides it covers the opportunity, the landscape, three market segments and a playbook for us.

## Slide 2

Source: draft

The report runs in four acts. Act one is the opportunity: market size, growth and the consumer shift behind GLP-1 demand. Act two is the landscape: the competition, the key players and the white space. Act three is three segments with three strategies: who the consumers are and how to position for them. Act four is the playbook: innovation, channels and go-to-market recommendations.

## Slide 3

Source: draft

Act one: the opportunity. The next few slides size the market, explain what the drugs do to the body, show how users behave, and look at the UK, which the deck treats as the most advanced retail market outside the US.

## Slide 4

Source: draft

The market in numbers. The deck gives 137 million Americans potentially eligible for GLP-1 therapy, and a GLP-1 supplement market projected at 2.5 billion dollars by 2030, up from about 680 million in 2024. 30% of users already take supplements, 80% are willing to pay a premium, and 23% of buyers are new to supplements. Europe is following: 9.1% of Danish households and 4.1% of UK households are on a GLP-1.

## Slide 5

Source: draft

The deck starts with what the drugs do, benefits first. They mimic the hormone GLP-1, suppress appetite and slow stomach emptying. Weight loss runs from 15 to 24% depending on drug and duration. Then the costs: 25 to 45% of weight lost is lean mass, 40 to 85% of users have GI side effects, 22.4% have vitamin deficiencies at twelve months, and only 8.1% remain on therapy at three years. Each cost, the deck says, creates a supplement need.

## Slide 6

Source: draft

What do GLP-1 users actually do? According to Innova's 2025 data in the deck, 30% take supplements while on the drugs, and 80% will pay more for GLP-1-specific products: 28% a premium above 50%, and 52% a premium of 25 to 50%. 25 million US consumers have used compounded semaglutide. Self-prescribers have no clinical guidance on nutrition or side effects, so the deck sees them as needing reliable products most.

## Slide 7

Source: draft

The UK, the deck says, is the most advanced GLP-1 supplement retail market outside the US, and every major retailer responded within weeks. Morrisons launched 53 products with Applied Nutrition in January 2026 under a three-year exclusive deal. M&S, Sainsbury's, Iceland, Co-op, Asda, Aldi and Lidl all have ranges. Holland & Barrett is reformulating its whole range for GLP-1 users; its top searches in 2025 were magnesium, collagen and vitamin D.

## Slide 8

Source: draft

UK brands are pivoting. SlimFast's new owner bought it from Glanbia for 20.1 million pounds, citing GLP-1. Huel launched a 190-calorie meal, Naturecan a GLP-1 collection, and Pura Collagen a collagen product making its own GLP-1 claims. The demographics explain it: 1.6 million UK users in the prior year and 3.3 million more interested, 77.6% of them women, average age 42.3: the core wellness-supplement shopper. The ASA is enforcing on positioning.

## Slide 9

Source: draft

What does the UK wave mean for ingredient suppliers? The deck sees five categories, all accelerating. Protein is the baseline: every retail range leads with it. Fibre is emerging: M&S bet on fibre over protein. Micronutrients are the premium play: B12, D, iron, folate and collagen. Botanicals are an opportunity, such as ginger and adaptogens. And muscle is growing fast, because 25 to 45% of weight lost is lean mass.

## Slide 10

Source: draft

Act two: the landscape. The next slides cover who is already buying ingredients, how the rules differ between the US, the EU and the UK, what the new oral pills change, and what the WHO says about nutrition alongside the drugs.

## Slide 11

Source: draft

The brand wave is already under way. Arrae, a native GLP-1 brand, is berberine-based. Pendulum sells a GLP-1 probiotic at 165 dollars a month and has raised over 100 million. Huel and AG1 target nutrient gaps. Lemme's GLP-1 product faces a class action. Nestlé Health Science has repositioned a high-protein product, and Isagenix and Plexus are adapting their ranges. Every channel is responding: direct to consumer, retail, multi-level marketing and big consumer brands.

## Slide 12

Source: draft

This is the most important slide for what we say. Three markets, three claim regimes. The US allows structure-function claims. The EU has zero approved GLP-1 claims, and any GLP-1 pathway reference is prohibited. In the UK, any GLP-1 support reference is prohibited, and the ASA banned five supplement brands in August 2025. The safest path, the deck says, is approved nutrient claims, such as glucomannan for weight loss or protein for muscle mass, kept outside medicinal territory.

## Slide 13

Source: draft

The oral pill changes the scale. Oral Wegovy was approved in December 2025 at 149 dollars a month, against 349 for the injection. The semaglutide patent expires in March 2026, with generics in 87 countries. The deck projects users growing from 6.5 million today to 30 million or more by 2027 to 2028. It also reports new data on SNAC, the absorption enhancer in oral semaglutide, and says that if confirmed, the microbiome need may grow.

## Slide 14

Source: draft

The deck reports that the WHO agrees drugs alone are not enough. It summarises Francesco Branca, WHO Director of Nutrition, as saying the drugs create a risk of nutritional deficiency that needs a supplementation strategy. A 2025 joint advisory from ACLM, ASN and OMA calls for protein at 1.2 to 2.0 grams per kilo per day, structured exercise and micronutrient monitoring. The deck lists the needs: protein, vitamin D with calcium, iron and B12, the microbiome, and lean mass.

## Slide 15

Source: draft

Act three: three segments, three strategies. The GLP-1 supplement market is not one market. The next slides take each segment in turn: natural alternatives to the drugs, support for people taking them, and support for people coming off them. Each has its own consumers, evidence and timeline.

## Slide 16

Source: draft

Here are the three segments side by side. Segment one, natural GLP-1 alternatives: the highest consumer demand, mixed evidence, and high regulatory risk, with no approved GLP-1 claims in the EU or UK. Segment two, adjunct support for people on the drugs: growing demand, strong evidence from the 2025 joint advisory, and low regulatory risk. Segment three, the off-ramp after stopping: latent demand, minimal evidence, and moderate risk. Each needs its own go-to-market strategy.

## Slide 17

Source: draft

This slide explains the science segment one relies on: three pathways the deck describes linking the gut to GLP-1. First, fibre fermentation produces short-chain fatty acids that act on receptors on gut L-cells. Second, bitter compounds bind taste receptors on the same cells. Third, an enzyme, DPP-4, breaks GLP-1 down within about two minutes, and some compounds slow it. Remember that in the EU and UK no GLP-1 claim is allowed.

## Slide 18

Source: draft

The "nature's Ozempic" market. Consumers want to lose weight without a prescription, from a product that works on several fronts: appetite, blood sugar, fat metabolism and gut health. The deck counts about 600 Amazon products with GLP-1 in the name, most of them single-pathway, and says 27% of weight-loss users self-prescribe. Its point for us: brands need a partner who can supply across all five layers of the stack, with the rationale and the defensible claims.

## Slide 19

Source: draft

This is the deck's map of mechanisms. On the left, the GLP-1 axis in three parts: produce, protect and amplify, with the deck listing eight receptor systems and the ingredients it links to each. On the right, complementary pathways that do not involve GLP-1: AMPK activation, carbohydrate-enzyme inhibition, physical satiety, thermogenesis, serotonergic appetite and fat-cell formation. The deck's case is for formulations that combine several pathways. Treat this as mechanism, not as claims.

## Slide 20

Source: draft

Segment one's evidence tiers. Tier one, human trials that measured GLP-1, includes prebiotic fibres, berberine, curcumin and Pediococcus acidilactici. Tier two, plausible but with gaps, includes cinnamon, gymnema, fenugreek, L-glutamine and quercetin. The deck singles out berberine: the most effective segment-one ingredient, it says, but the riskiest, with over 60% of tested products failing, an active EFSA safety assessment and US tariff exposure. Akkermansia is not legal in the EU.

## Slide 21

Source: draft

This table lists the segment-one ingredients with pathway, tier, dose and EU status. Fibres lead: FOS at 5 to 15 grams a day, the deck's best GLP-1 evidence but the worst GI tolerance; inulin, with the EU bowel claim at 12 grams; and resistant starch, well tolerated. Note the EU column: most entries carry no GLP-1 claim, and glucomannan, at 3 grams a day, holds the EFSA weight-loss claim. Real formulations, the deck says, combine several pathways.

## Slide 22

Source: draft

Segment two, support for people on the drugs, is the structurally larger opportunity. The deck gives a 16 to 39% cut in calorie intake, 22.4% deficiency within twelve months, and over 60% below calcium and iron needs. Only 43% meet the protein minimum. GI symptoms are common: bloating 50%, diarrhoea 38%. Spending is shifting to greens, protein and bone health. That makes two product conversations: acute help on dosing days, and daily nutrition.

## Slide 23

Source: draft

The deck groups adjunct support into five pillars, each a product conversation. GI support, for bloating, with probiotics, ginger and PHGG. Micronutrient repletion, for deficiency, with D3 and K2, iron bisglycinate, calcium, magnesium and B vitamins. Muscle preservation, for lean mass, with protein, creatine and HMB. Skin, hair and nails, with collagen peptides. And emotional and cognitive support, with L-theanine, ashwagandha and saffron.

## Slide 24

Source: draft

This table summarises the adjunct ingredients with category, role, dose and tier. A few to remember. PHGG is the deck's GI fibre for this segment, at 5 to 6 grams a day, while FOS is marked to avoid here, because it has the worst tolerability. Vitamin D3 with K2, iron bisglycinate and calcium address the deficiencies. Protein at 1.2 to 2.0 grams per kilo, creatine at 3 to 5 grams, and HMB at 3 grams address muscle.

## Slide 25

Source: draft

Segment three is the off-ramp, and the deck says 92% will need one. Only 8.1% remain on therapy at three years. People stop because of side effects, with 28.2% citing adverse effects, because of cost, at 900 to 1,400 dollars a month, or because they feel done. When they stop, 82.5% regain at least a quarter within a year. The deck notes there are no published trial protocols for supplement support here: total white space.

## Slide 26

Source: draft

What happens when people stop? The deck calls it the metabolic trap. In the STEP 1 extension, people regained much of what they lost, keeping a net loss of only 5.6%. The weight tends to return as fat rather than muscle, and because 25 to 45% of the weight lost was lean mass, metabolic rate drops and maintaining weight gets harder. The deck adds an average regain of 0.8 kilos a month, from a 2025 BMJ meta-analysis.

## Slide 27

Source: draft

The deck proposes five pillars of off-ramp support. A satiety bridge, using fermentable and gel-forming fibres. Metabolic rate, using thermogenic ingredients. Weight-regain prevention, through the microbiome, with Hafnia alvei in the EU or Akkermansia in the US, plus polyphenols. Rebuilding lean mass, with higher protein, creatine and HMB. And appetite and mood support through what the deck calls a psychologically difficult transition.

## Slide 28

Source: draft

This table gives the fourteen off-ramp ingredients across the five pillars, each with mechanism, tier and EU or UK status. The key regulatory point: glucomannan holds the only EFSA weight-loss claim, which the deck calls a unique regulatory moat. Protein has the EFSA muscle claim, and the fibres carry the inulin bowel claim. Note too that Akkermansia is marked not legal in the EU, while Hafnia alvei is marked EU legal.

Act four: the playbook. Having looked at the three segments, the deck now turns to what we do about them as a supplier: the premix opportunity, the quality crisis, and five strategic insights to take into customer conversations.

## Slide 30

Source: draft

The premix opportunity: ready-made blends for each segment, which the deck calls the highest-value play for ingredient suppliers. Examples are a GI support premix and a micronutrient repletion premix for segment two, a muscle preservation premix for segments two and three, and an off-ramp premix for segment three. Premixes win on margin, on switching cost once validated, and on lower formulation risk for the customer. They make us a formulation partner, not just a supplier.

## Slide 31

Source: draft

The deck turns the science into ten ready-to-brief premix concepts across the three segments, each with a format and its rationale. They include a metabolic fibre complex, a GI comfort capsule, a micronutrient repletion capsule, a lean mass powder with protein, 5 grams of creatine and 3 grams of HMB, and a two-phase off-ramp programme. Use them as conversation starters with buyers, adapted to each customer's needs.

## Slide 32

Source: draft

The quality crisis is our opportunity. The deck cites independent testing: over 60% of tested berberine products fail label claims or contain undisclosed ingredients; most creatine gummies deliver less than half their label claim; and probiotic counts at expiry are often below label. Quality suppliers can lead with a certificate of analysis for every shipment, third-party testing, format guidance, regulatory alerts and strain-verified probiotics. Quality turns a commodity supplier into a trusted partner.

## Slide 33

Source: draft

Five strategic insights. One: segment two is the immediate opportunity, with approved nutrient claims and the lowest regulatory risk. Two: segment three is the biggest long-term white space. Three: quality leadership is the moat. Four: the rules differ by market, so one formulation will not work everywhere. Five: the premix model captures the most value. Together: lead with science, quality and segment expertise.

Source: draft

The deck closes on three numbers: 30% of users already take supplements, 92% stop the drug within three years, and the market is projected at 2.5 billion dollars by 2030.

## Summary

This module is our market-intelligence briefing on GLP-1 drugs and the supplement demand they create. It sizes the opportunity
(a projected $2.5B GLP-1 supplement market by 2030, up from about $680M in 2024, per the deck), explains what the drugs do and
what they cost the body (GI side effects, lean-mass loss, nutrient deficiencies, and most users stopping within three years),
and uses the UK retail wave as a leading indicator for other markets. Its core idea is that GLP-1 is three supplement markets,
not one: natural alternatives for people without a prescription (highest demand, highest regulatory risk), adjunct support for
people on the drug (the immediate opportunity, with approved nutrient claims), and an off-ramp for people coming off it (the
biggest long-term white space, with no published protocols). For each segment the deck grades ingredients by evidence and EU
status, and it stresses that fibres are not interchangeable. It sets out what can and cannot be said in the US, EU and UK: in
the EU there are no approved GLP-1 claims. It closes with the playbook for us as a supplier: segment-specific premixes, quality
leadership where the supply chain is failing, and market-specific claims.

## Key learnings

- The deck sizes the market at 137M Americans potentially eligible for GLP-1 therapy (JAMA Cardiology via Innova Market
  Insights) and a GLP-1 supplement market projected at $2.5B by 2030, up from about $680M in 2024; 30% of users already take
  supplements, 80% will pay a premium and 23% of GLP-1 supplement buyers are new to supplements (slide 4).
- GLP-1 is three segments with three strategies: natural alternatives (demand highest, regulatory risk high), adjunct support
  (evidence strong, risk low) and off-ramp (demand latent, evidence minimal, total white space) (slide 16).
- The drugs work, and every benefit creates a need: 25–45% of weight lost is lean mass, 40–85% of users get GI side effects,
  22.4% have vitamin deficiencies at 12 months, and only 8.1% remain on therapy at 3 years (slide 5).
- In the EU there are zero EFSA-approved GLP-1 claims and any GLP-1 pathway reference is prohibited; the safest path is approved
  nutrient claims (for example glucomannan for weight loss at 3 g a day, chicory inulin for bowel function) placed outside
  medicinal territory (slide 12).
- Fibres are not interchangeable: FOS has the best GLP-1 evidence but the worst GI tolerance; PHGG is the best GI fibre but has
  no GLP-1 data (slides 24, 34).
- The deck's five strategic insights: Segment 2 is the immediate opportunity, Segment 3 the biggest long-term white space,
  quality leadership the moat, regulatory divergence needs market-specific strategies, and premixes capture the most value
  (slide 33).

## In practice

- Lead with adjunct support: it answers live retailer briefs, has approved nutrient claims and the lowest regulatory risk
  (slide 33).
- Frame two product conversations with an adjunct customer: acute support on dosing days (nausea, GI distress; sachets,
  effervescents) and ongoing daily support (deficiency, protein, microbiome; capsules, powders) (slide 22).
- Use the ten premix concepts as conversation starters with buyers, adapted to each customer's requirements (slide 31).
- Never use "nature's Ozempic", drug-alternative or drug-comparison language, and in the EU and UK no GLP-1 reference at all
  (slide 12).
- Match the fibre to the segment: PHGG for GI support in people on the drug, and FOS only at 3 g a day or less there, starting
  low (slide 24).
- Lead with quality: a certificate of analysis per shipment, third-party testing shared up front, format guidance (powder or
  capsule where gummies fail) and early warning on berberine's EFSA risk (slide 32).

## Check your understanding

- Q: Name the three GLP-1 supplement segments. Which does the deck call the immediate commercial opportunity?
  A: Natural GLP-1 alternatives, adjunct support and off-ramp or maintenance; Segment 2, adjunct support, is the immediate opportunity (slides 16, 33)
- Q: Can a supplement sold in the EU refer to GLP-1?
  A: No. There are zero EFSA-approved GLP-1 claims and any GLP-1 pathway reference is prohibited; 55 of 58 hunger and satiety claims were rejected (slide 12)
- Q: A customer making a product for people on a GLP-1 drug asks for a fibre. Which does the deck point to, and which should be kept low?
  A: PHGG (Sunfiber, 5–6 g a day) for GI support; FOS is flagged to avoid for GI-sensitive users, at 3 g a day or less, starting low (slide 24)
- Q: Why does the deck call berberine the highest commercial risk in Segment 1?
  A: A high quality failure rate, an active EFSA safety assessment that could lead to an EU ban, and US tariff exposure from China sourcing (slide 20)
- Q: What share of patients remain on GLP-1 therapy at 3 years, and why does that matter to us?
  A: 8.1%; every new prescription is a future off-ramp customer, and no published protocols for supplement-based off-ramp support exist (slide 25)

## Detail

The deck is marked "Confidential" on its cover. It is our own internal material (Vita Actives Limited, March 2026), fine for
the staff training site, not for customers or anyone outside the company as it stands.

**Act 1: the opportunity.** $23.3B GLP-1 pharma revenue in 2024 (US); in Europe a €7.1B EU pharma market, 9.1% of Danish
households on GLP-1 and 4.1% of UK households (doubled in 12 months); oral formulations and generics expand the eligible
population from 6.5M current users toward 30–40% of US adults (slide 4; sources Innova Market Insights 2025, JAMA Cardiology,
H&B Wellness Report 2026). Weight loss of 15–24% depending on drug and duration (semaglutide 14.9% in STEP 1, tirzepatide 20.9%
in SURMOUNT-1, CagriSema 22.7%, retatrutide 24.2%), −20% MACE in SELECT, HbA1c −1.0 to −2.0% in type 2 diabetes; side effects
include nausea 44%, constipation 24%, 28.2% discontinuing for adverse effects, vitamin D deficiency 13.6%, ferritin 26–30%
lower, and 3–5.7% hair loss, with women disproportionately affected (slide 5). Users' top actions: added exercise 63%, drink
more water 46%, adjusted diet 40%, take supplements 30%; 28% will pay a premium over 50% and 52% a 25–50% premium; 25M US
consumers have used compounded semaglutide (slide 6).

**The UK as leading indicator.** Morrisons and Applied Nutrition launched 53 SKUs on 2 January 2026 across about 400 stores
(3-year exclusive deal, £3.75 per meal); M&S, Sainsbury's, Iceland, Co-op, Asda, Aldi and Lidl followed; Holland & Barrett is
reformulating its range for GLP-1 users, with food sales up 34% and top 2025 searches magnesium (8.5M), collagen (6.2M) and
vitamin D (5.1M) (slide 7). 1.6M UK GLP-1 users in the prior year and 3.3M more interested (UCL, Jan 2026), NHS prescriptions
+900% since 2020, Mounjaro on the NHS with a 580K target over 5 years; users 77.6% women, average age 42.3 (slide 8). Brand
moves: SlimFast (Supreme bought the UK/EU assets from Glanbia for £20.1M), Huel Lite, Naturecan's GLP-1 collection, Pura
Collagen GLPure+ (slide 8); Arrae, Lemme (class action filed), Pendulum, Nestlé Health Science, Huel and AG1, Isagenix and
Plexus (slide 11). Ingredient signals: protein (£491M UK protein market in 2026 to £637M by 2031, the deck's wording keeps
"isolates, concentrates, collagen peptides, plant blends"), fibre (M&S bet on fibre over protein; Sainsbury's trademarked "Full
On Fibre"), micronutrients, botanicals and muscle (slide 9).

**Regulation (slide 12).** US: DSHEA structure/function claims such as "supports natural GLP-1 levels"; FDA issued 50+ warning
letters (Sep 2025). EU: viable claims glucomannan for weight loss (3 g a day), chromium for blood glucose, protein for muscle
mass, chicory inulin for bowel function; no GLP-1 reference, no berberine health claim; berberine under active EFSA safety
assessment, flagged by France and Belgium. UK: GB NHC Register; no GLP-1 support reference or drug comparison; ASA banned 5
supplement brands (Aug 2025) and uses AI scanning.

**The pill and the pipeline (slide 13).** Oral Wegovy approved December 2025 at $149 a month against $349 injectable;
semaglutide patent expiry March 2026 with generics across 87 countries; orforglipron (Eli Lilly); Medicare Part D coverage in
2026. On SNAC, the absorption enhancer in oral semaglutide, the deck cites Feb 2026 data of a 77% reduction in faecal butyrate
and a 70% increase in plasma TNF-alpha, and says "if confirmed" the oral drug may also create greater need for microbiome
support. Slide 14 reports the WHO (Francesco Branca) and a 2025 ACLM/ASN/OMA joint advisory on protein of 1.2–2.0 g/kg a day,
exercise and micronutrient monitoring.

**Segment 1, natural alternatives.** Three gut pathways to GLP-1: SCFAs from prebiotic fibres via FFAR2/FFAR3, bitter-taste
receptors (berberine, curcumin, quercetin) and DPP-4 inhibition (curcumin, quercetin, collagen peptides; "measurable but modest")
(slide 17). About 600 Amazon SKUs carry "GLP-1" in their names, 27% of weight-loss users self-prescribe, and the berberine market
is $0.9–1.4B (slide 18). The deck maps 8 receptor systems that stimulate GLP-1 plus protect and amplify routes and six
complementary pathways (slide 19). Tier 1 (human RCT with GLP-1 measurement): prebiotic fibres (inulin, FOS, GOS), berberine,
Pediococcus acidilactici, Akkermansia muciniphila (not legal in the EU) and L-glutamine; Tier 2: Ceylon cinnamon, curcumin,
gymnema, fenugreek and quercetin (slide 20). The ingredient table gives doses and EU status, for example FOS 5–15 g a day,
inulin 8–12 g, resistant starch 15–30 g, and in the deck's wording "whey protein" 20–40 g a day for amino-acid sensing
(slide 21).

**Segment 2, adjunct support.** Caloric intake falls 16–39%, 60%+ are below calcium and iron requirements and only 43% meet the
protein minimum; GI burden from Lumina: bloating 50%, diarrhoea 38%, acid reflux 34%; spending among GLP-1 users: superfoods and
greens +58%, protein shakes and bars +38%, bone health +23% (slide 22). Five pillars: GI support, micronutrient repletion, muscle
preservation, skin, hair and nails, emotional and cognitive (slide 23), with the ingredient table on slide 24 (the deck lists
"Protein (whey/coll.)" at 25–40 g a day).

**Segment 3, off-ramp.** 52.3% remain at month 12, 20% at month 24 and 8.1% at month 36; about 50% cite cost ($900–1,400 a
month) as the primary reason; 82.5% regain at least 25% within 12 months, 78.5% have appetite rebound; Rick Polito (Nutrition
Business Journal) calls it "the biggest market for all of us" (slide 25). Weight returns as fat rather than muscle; average
regain 0.8 kg a month after stopping (BMJ 2025 meta-analysis) (slide 26). Five pillars of maintenance: satiety bridge,
metabolic rate, weight-regain prevention, lean-mass rebuilding, appetite and emotional support (slide 27), with 14 ingredients
on slide 28; the deck notes that glucomannan holds the only EFSA weight-loss claim.

**Playbook.** Four premixes (GI support, micronutrient repletion, muscle preservation, off-ramp phase 1) and why they win:
margin, lock-in, lower formulation risk for the customer, and positioning as a formulation partner (slide 30); ten concepts
across the three segments (slide 31). The quality crisis: berberine products failing label claims, most creatine gummies
delivering under 50% of label claim, probiotic viability below label at expiry (slide 32).

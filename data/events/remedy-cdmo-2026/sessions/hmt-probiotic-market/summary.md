# HMT: beyond probiotics, four new rules for the category

## Summary
Marit Weinstra of HMT, a branding and innovation agency for food and supplements, opened the probiotic programme. Her question was how to win in a biotics market that has grown into a jungle of products, where consumers say they do not feel informed.

She traced four eras. Yakult made good bacteria a daily ritual. Danone Activia made digestion benefits tangible, which led brands to compete on CFU count. Then technology and survival became the story. Now the shift is from adding bacteria to nourishing the gut ecosystem, with the gut as the gateway to the rest of the body.

From that she drew four rules for winning today: quality and proof over "more is better", feeding the whole ecosystem, benefits consumers can measure and feel, and everyday food formats. Her examples came from the market: a US brand built on one Akkermansia strain, fibre brands selling plant diversity, brands setting expectations by week and month, and Poppi as a format consumers like.

Her conclusion: most of these claims cannot be made in Europe, so the brand has to carry them. A strong probiotic needs a "double IP", rational science plus an emotional brand.

## Key takeaways
- Consumers say they do not feel informed about probiotics; the category has too many options and not enough clarity [00:24].
- Rule 1: 50, 80 or 100 billion CFU on shelf means nothing to the consumer; numbers alone no longer build trust, so be the proven expert in a niche, as a US Akkermansia brand does for metabolic health and weight management [03:59].
- Rule 2: talk about feeding what the consumer already has, fibres and plant diversity, which they can see and act on; fibre brands lead this language [03:59].
- Rule 3: early adopters measure with microbiome tests and apps; mass-market consumers keep buying only when they feel a difference, so brands now set expectations for one week and one month [03:59].
- Rule 4: consumers stick with formats they like; Poppi is her example of a product designed around the consumer rather than the ingredient [03:59].
- In Europe most of these claims cannot be made, so the brand must carry them: brand the strain and build a "double IP", rational and emotional [03:59].

## For Vita Actives

- The shift from "what is in the product" to "what does it do for me" is the same shift sports and active nutrition brands face with protein and creatine, and it favours ingredients whose effect a consumer notices. My reading: that is how a CreaWin or IsoClr: Pea pitch to a brand should be framed.

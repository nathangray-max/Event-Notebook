# HMT: beyond probiotics, four new rules for the category

## Summary
Marit Weinstra of HMT, a branding and innovation agency for food and supplements, opened the probiotic programme by arguing that the biotics market (probiotics, prebiotics, fibres, postbiotics) has grown into "a jungle of products" and that consumers say they do not feel informed about probiotics [00:24]. She traced four eras: Yakult's daily ritual and the idea of good bacteria; Danone Activia making digestion benefits tangible, which led to competition on CFU count; a survival era where technology became the story; and the current shift from adding bacteria to nourishing the gut ecosystem, with the gut as the gateway to the rest of the body [00:24] [03:59]. From that she gave four rules for winning today: quality and proof over "more is better", feeding the whole ecosystem, measurable and felt benefits, and everyday food formats [03:59]. Her evidence was market examples: a US brand built on an Akkermansia strain for metabolic health, fibre brands selling plant diversity, brands setting expectations by week and month, and Poppi as a format consumers like [03:59]. Because most of these claims cannot be made in Europe, she said the brand has to carry them: a "double IP" of rational science plus an emotional brand [03:59].

## Key takeaways
- Consumers say they do not feel informed about probiotics; the category has too many options and not enough clarity [00:24].
- The category was born as a daily ritual in a convenient format (Yakult), then competed on CFU count (after Activia made digestion tangible), then on survival and technology, and now on nourishing the gut ecosystem [00:24] [03:59].
- Rule 1: 50, 80 or 100 billion CFU on shelf means nothing to the consumer; numbers alone no longer build trust, so be the proven expert in a niche, as a US Akkermansia brand does for metabolic health and weight management [03:59].
- Rule 2: talk about feeding what the consumer already has, fibres and plant diversity, which they can see and act on; fibre brands lead this language [03:59].
- Rule 3: early adopters measure with microbiome tests and apps; mass-market consumers keep buying only when they feel a difference, so brands now set expectations for one week and one month [03:59].
- Rule 4: consumers stick with formats they like; Poppi is her example of a product designed around the consumer rather than the ingredient [03:59].
- In Europe most of these claims cannot be made, so the brand must carry them: brand the strain and build a "double IP", rational and emotional [03:59].
- The chair placed HMT as helping companies connect what they can develop with what consumers understand and value [00:00].

## For Vita Actives
Medium relevance: nothing was said about protein, creatine or sports nutrition, but the branding argument transfers to our customers' categories. My reading: the shift from "what is in the product" to "what does it do for me" is the same shift sports and active nutrition brands face with protein and creatine, and it favours ingredients whose effect a consumer notices, which is how a CreaWin or IsoClr: Pea pitch to a brand should be framed. My reading: "science opens the doors, but it's a brand that keeps you in the room" is an argument for branded ingredients with a story a European brand can tell within claim rules. HMT is an agency, not a buyer; it could be a partner for a customer's launch.

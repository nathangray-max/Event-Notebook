# Longevity - from a trend to a product

## Summary
Mateusz Korgul and Victoria Lesiovski of Tantus, a Polish CDMO, asked how a longevity trend becomes a product people actually take. A trend changes nothing on its own. It matters only once a product reaches the market and becomes part of a daily routine.

Consumers want healthspan, not only lifespan: years spent active, capable and independent. The industry answers with ever more complex formulas, but they argued the real question is what makes biological sense in a product, not what else can be added. Longevity is a marathon, so format, dose and ease of use decide whether people keep taking it.

Their process has four steps: trend, ingredient, formula, market. Suppliers are partners who bring research, safety dossiers and formulation know-how, not vendors filling separate orders.

They showed three concepts. The first, made with Solabia, is a chewable tablet of 3 g creatine and 400 mg of Solabia's citrus extract WATTS'UP, sold on physical function with age rather than sport: "Chew it, don't shake it." The second is a daily healthy-ageing capsule of Kaneka Ubiquinol, VitaCholine and active B vitamins. The third is a tablet of Nuritas's PeptiStrong plant peptides for muscle with age. Their close: keep it simple, make it last.

## Key takeaways
- A trend does not change anything on its own; longevity becomes a product "when it fits real life - and stays there" [00:31] (slide 13).
- Complexity is not the answer: the question is never what else to add, but what "genuinely makes biological sense in this specific product" [01:05] (slide 4).
- Concept one: 3 g creatine plus 400 mg WATTS'UP in a chewable tablet, formulated by Tantus and Solabia, for mobility and muscle resilience "without requiring athletic sports positioning" (slide 7). Spoken as "400 grams" of a hesperidin-based ingredient [04:45]; the slide's 400 mg differs from what was said. The slides do not name the creatine's source.
- The slide's image gives four reasons people stop taking creatine powder: water needed, a shaker to carry, grit and aftertaste, and it will not dissolve ("Half of it sits at the bottom") (slide 8).
- Concepts two and three, from the slides only: Anti-Age by Tantus, one capsule a day of 100 mg Kaneka Ubiquinol, 300 mg VitaCholine and an active B-complex; and a PeptiStrong tablet co-developed with Nuritas and Vivatis Pharma (slide 9) (slide 10) (slide 12).
- Tantus wants long-term suppliers who bring clinical research, safety dossiers, technology know-how and new concepts, not transactional orders [03:09] (slide 6).

- The four steps on the slide: trend (read the market and consumer need with partners), ingredient (evidence-backed, not just strong marketing), formula (combine around one concrete product function), market (from first concept to a manufacturable product) (slide 5).

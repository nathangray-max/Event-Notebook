# Tantus: longevity in action, from a trend to a product

## Summary
Victoria and Mateus of Tantus, a Polish CDMO, asked how a longevity trend becomes a product people actually take. Their starting point was that a trend changes nothing on its own; it matters only once a product reaches the market and becomes part of a daily routine.

Consumers want to live better, not only longer, so healthspan drives the conversation. The industry answers with new ingredients and ever more complex formulas, but they argued the right question is what makes sense in a formula, not what else can be added. Longevity is "a marathon", so format, dose and ease of use matter as much as the ingredient: a formula people will not take every day fails.

Their process runs in four steps: trend, ingredient, formula, market. Suppliers are partners who bring science and formulation knowledge, not vendors filling separate orders.

The one concept captured before the recording ended was creatine seen through a longevity lens: a chewable tablet developed with Solabia, combining three grams of creatine with a hesperidin-based ingredient and aimed at keeping physical function with age. Their line: "Chew it, don't shake it."

## Key takeaways
- A trend does not change anything on its own; the product has to reach the market and become part of a daily routine [00:31].
- The question is not what else to add to a formula but what makes sense and why [01:05].
- Simplicity first: format, dosage and ease of use matter because consistent use is the point [02:09].
- Tantus wants long-term supplier relationships rather than transactional orders, for access to research, technical information, formulation expertise and new ideas [03:09].
- Concept one: creatine reframed for longevity, a chewable tablet with Solabia, three grams of creatine with "400 grams" of a hesperidin-based ingredient (Otter's summary gives 400 mg), tried at Solabia's stand [04:45].

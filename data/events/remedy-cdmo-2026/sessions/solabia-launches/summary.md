# Solabia Nutrition: three things your brief forgot to tell you

## Summary
Thomas, presenting for Solabia Nutrition, opened with the brief every brand writes and every CDMO receives: clinically proven, clean label, cost-effective, innovative, ready in Q3, two-year shelf life. He called it impossible and replaced it with three questions: will they remember it, can you make it, can you prove it.

On memory, the name decides. A flavonol with a prebiotic effect went nowhere as a "flavorbiotic" and was rebranded, while an aronia berry extract renamed Brainberry is now Solabia's second biggest and fastest growing product.

On making it, briefs now ask for several grams of creatine and collagen plus botanicals in one soluble format. Creatine adds its own problem: brands want it in gummies, but heat turns it into creatinine. Solabia and the Polish CDMO Tantus avoided this with a cold-pressed tablet of creatine and a citrus extract.

On proof, he said "science-backed" means nothing on its own. What counts is the dose, the population, the endpoints and the change against placebo. Solabia has run randomised placebo-controlled trials on all its ingredients and put each study on its website. He closed by inviting brands to bring their briefs to Solabia.

## Key takeaways
- The standard brief is "impossible"; judge a launch on whether they will remember it, whether you can make it and whether you can prove it [00:01].
- Naming decides memory: the aronia extract renamed Brainberry is now Solabia's second biggest and fastest growing product [00:01].
- Briefs now ask for three to five grams of creatine and five to ten grams of collagen plus a botanical extract and a vitamin complex in one format that is soluble and mixes well [00:01].
- Creatine in gummies: heat applied over time gives creatinine, which "doesn't really do much"; Tantus's answer was a cold-pressed tablet, no heat, of creatine plus a citrus extract [00:01].
- "Science-backed" is one of the most used and most dangerous phrases in the industry; look at dose, population, endpoints and the change against placebo [00:01].

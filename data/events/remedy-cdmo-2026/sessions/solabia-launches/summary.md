# Solabia Nutrition: three things your brief forgot to tell you

## Summary
Thomas, presenting for Solabia Nutrition, read out the standard brand brief ("clinically proven, clean label, cost-effective, innovative, ready in Q3, two-year shelf life") and called it impossible [00:01]. He replaced it with three questions: will they remember it, can you make it, can you prove it. For memory he gave two renamings from Solabia's portfolio: a flavonol with a prebiotic effect that failed as a "flavorbiotic" and was rebranded, and an aronia berry extract renamed Brainberry, now the portfolio's second biggest and fastest growing product. For making it he described briefs asking for three to five grams of creatine plus five to ten grams of collagen plus botanicals in one soluble format, and a creatine problem: heat in gummy production turns creatine into creatinine, which Solabia and the Polish CDMO Tantus avoided with a cold-pressed tablet of creatine and a citrus extract. For proving it he said "science-backed" means nothing on its own; dose, population, endpoints and the change against placebo are what count, and Solabia has run randomised placebo-controlled trials on all its ingredients and put the studies on its website.

## Key takeaways
- The standard brief (clinically proven, clean label, cost-effective, innovative, Q3, two-year shelf life) is "impossible"; judge a launch on whether they will remember it, whether you can make it and whether you can prove it [00:01].
- Naming decides memory: "flavorbiotic" did not fly and the industry does not need another "biotic"; the aronia extract renamed Brainberry is now Solabia's second biggest and fastest growing product [00:01].
- Briefs now ask for three to five grams of creatine and five to ten grams of collagen plus a botanical extract and a vitamin complex in one format that is soluble and mixes well, which gets complicated fast [00:01].
- Creatine in gummies: heat applied over time gives creatinine, which "doesn't really do much"; Tantus's answer was a cold-pressed tablet, no heat, of creatine plus a citrus extract the speaker said helps nitric oxide and blood flow, tasted at Solabia's stand [00:01].
- "Science-backed" is one of the most used and most dangerous phrases in the industry; look at dose, population, endpoints and the change against placebo [00:01].
- Solabia has run randomised placebo-controlled trials on all its ingredients and rebuilt its website over the summer so each study can be read [00:01].
- The olive leaf extract was marketed for bone health and is now studied for skin health and menopause symptoms; all its studies were in women, by female-led research teams [00:01].
- The host introduced Tantus as a Polish contract manufacturer of dietary supplements working in tablets and hard capsules, with formulation, development and some packaging [08:23].
- The talk was one unbroken Otter turn, so every passage above sits in the same [00:01] block (my reading: the order of points follows the talk).

## For Vita Actives
- Creatine is being briefed at three to five grams a serving alongside collagen and botanicals, and brands want it in gummies and chewables, not only powder [00:01]. The heat problem in gummies is an opening for a stable creatine and for formats that avoid heat; my reading: a CreaWin pitch to CDMOs should carry stability data for cold-pressed and chewable formats.
- Tantus (Poland) is a CDMO already making a creatine chewable with a supplier's own ingredient [00:01]; my reading: a direct CreaWin prospect, and a sign that Polish CDMOs co-develop concepts with ingredient suppliers rather than only buying to spec.
- "Can you prove it" (dose, population, endpoint, placebo) is how an ingredient marketer like Solabia expects evidence to be shown [00:01]; my reading: our CreaWin and IsoClr: Pea materials should lead with those four items.
- A benefit in the name beat a mechanism word [00:01]; my reading: relevant to how we name concepts for customers.
- Relevance high: creatine dose and format constraints in sports and active nutrition, and a CDMO that makes creatine products.

# Healthy Ageing Reimagined

## Summary
Nick Morgan of Nutrition Integrated talked through how longevity is sold, from his own database of products. The recording catches only his last six minutes; his slides give the whole talk in five parts.

Healthy ageing, healthspan and longevity are different things: function and wellbeing, years in good health, and years of life. Brands either try to alter the biology of ageing, optimise how people feel as they age, or fit longevity to one group such as ageing athletes or women through menopause. On the page they sell energy and vitality, not science: the hallmarks of ageing appear on only one product page in eleven.

Brands sell a system, not a product: Cellaro's three products for morning, evening and a monthly reset. And longevity is one of the most expensive categories per serving. Creatine sells at a median of €0.47 a serving in capsules and tablets, while MitoQ's longevity creatine sells at €1.60, more than three times as much.

He closed on where the category is going: new formats, longevity claims moving into hydration, and longevity told as a performance story through sports nutrition.

## Key takeaways
- On 265 product pages assessed, energy and vitality is the most common message (83.8%), while the hallmarks of ageing are named on 9.1% and placed in lead copy on 4.2% (slide 5); spoken as about 300 products, 9% and 4% [00:00].
- Price: median price per serving in capsules and tablets is €0.47 for creatine (518 products) against €1.35 for longevity (194) and €1.36 for NAD (28), read from the chart (slide 7); spoken as "around about 50 cents" for creatine [00:00].
- MitoQ Longevity Creatine Complex, 5 g creatine with citicoline, sells at €1.60 a serving, 3.41 times the creatine median, positioned for "muscle, mind and cellular energy" (slide 7).
- "You don't sell one product in 2026": Cellaro Core is three products, one system (morning, evening, a monthly two-day reset) (slide 6) [00:00].
- Longevity 2.0: precision and convenient formats (a spermidine pen, an NAD+ liquid, longevity chocolate), hydration with a cellular-health story (DryWater), and longevity as a performance story (Centenarian Road) (slide 8) [00:00].

## Detail
- The deck is dated Remedy, Gdansk, 22 Oct 2026, and its data come from Nutrition Integrated's own product dataset covering longevity and adjacent categories such as protein, creatine, HMB and beauty (slide 1) (slide 2).
- Five parts: grounding the conversation; to alter or optimise ageing; selling support, not products; the cost to be healthy; Longevity 2.0 (slide 2).
- Definitions: healthy ageing is developing and maintaining functional ability; healthspan the period free from chronic disease, disability or substantial functional impairment; longevity the number of life years (slide 3).
- Three approaches: altering the ageing process (cellular and mitochondrial health, senescence); optimising the ageing experience (muscle and mobility, cognition, energy, sleep, gut, metabolic health, beauty, immunity); personalising the age focus (ageing athletes, women through menopause and later life, beauty longevity) (slide 4).
- Messages on the 265 pages, mentioned and in lead copy: longevity 72.5% and 44.9%; cellular health 65.7% and 34.3%; NAD+ 57.4% and 40.8%; cognition 57.0% and 21.9%; skin and beauty 35.8% and 9.1%; all hallmarks covered 2.3% and 1.1%. 44.5% use both "longevity" and "healthy ageing" (slide 5).
- Top ingredients in longevity blends (336 products), read from the chart: curcumin 38.4%, vitamin B1 26.8%, NAD 25.6%, vitamin C 22.0%, nicotinamide riboside 19.9%, fisetin 19.3%, black pepper 18.2%, vitamin B12 16.7%; NMN, zinc and calcium 14.6% each; spermidine 14.0%; quercetin and CoQ10 13.7%; resveratrol 13.4% (slide 6).
- Other medians per serving, read from the chart: lactoferrin €1.05, NMN €1.01, beauty €0.98, brain €0.91, multivitamin €0.49, collagen €0.44, magnesium €0.31, zinc €0.13 (slide 7). MitoQ is US$54 for 30 servings, converted at €1 = US$1.1234 (ECB, 21 Oct 2026); the slide notes a powder blend benchmarked against capsules and tablets (slide 7).

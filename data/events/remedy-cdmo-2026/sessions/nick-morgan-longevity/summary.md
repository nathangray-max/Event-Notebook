# Nick Morgan: how longevity is sold, from hallmarks to habits

## Summary
The recording catches the last six minutes of Nick Morgan's talk on the longevity consumer. His question was how brands actually sell longevity, and his answer was: not through the science.

Longevity now sits inside women's health, menopause and sports nutrition. Consumers connect with "energy and vitality" and "cellular health", not the hallmarks of ageing; in about 300 products he coded, only 9% refer to the hallmarks at all. Brands sell a routine of about three products, not one, so an ingredient is sold on how it fits the customer's product system.

Longevity is also one of the most expensive categories per serve. His example was creatine: a median of about 50 cents a serve on the sports shelf, and far more with longevity on the front of the pack. A good story can turn an existing ingredient into a premium one.

He closed on where the category is going. Capsules dominate today, but formats are widening into liquids, chocolate, gummies and drinks, cellular claims are entering hydration, and longevity is moving into sports nutrition. His advice was to keep up, because what brands say today will change within months.

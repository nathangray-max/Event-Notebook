Nick Morgan discusses the evolving consumer landscape, communication strategies, and product formats within the longevity and healthy aging market.

## Market Communication
**Nick** notes that while "longevity" is a technical term, consumers connect more with concepts like "energy," "vitality," and "cellular health." Most brands avoid technical "hallmarks of aging" in lead copy, as only 4% to 9% of products utilize them effectively for a general audience.
**Consumer Trends**
*   Longevity is increasingly linked to women's health (menopause) and sports nutrition.
*   "Cellular health" has become a widely used surrogate for longevity.

## Product Strategy
**Nick** emphasizes that success in 2026 requires selling routines rather than single products. Brands should focus on multifaceted approaches that integrate into daily habits and rituals.
**Pricing and Ingredients**
*   Common ingredients include **resveratrol**, **CoQ10**, **ubiquinol**, and **spermidine**.
*   Longevity products command premium pricing; for example, even traditional ingredients like **creatine** can be repositioned as high-end longevity products through better storytelling.
*   The brand **Solero** is cited as an example of a successful routine-based model using AM, PM, and monthly "reset" products.

## Future Formats
The longevity category is moving beyond the standard capsule format, which currently accounts for 70% of the market. New delivery methods are increasing accessibility and consumer engagement.
**Emerging Delivery Methods**
*   Liquids, gummies, chocolates, and energy drinks (e.g., drinks containing **NAD**).
*   Precision delivery methods such as injection pens/cartridges.
*   Integration into broader categories like hydration and sports nutrition, exemplified by the brand **Centenarian**.

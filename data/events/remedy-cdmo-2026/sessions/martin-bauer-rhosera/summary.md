# Martin Bauer: Rhosera, cultivated Rhodiola rosea against anabolic resistance

## Summary
Ralph of Martin Bauer Nutraceuticals, a new unit of the family-owned Nature Network (Martin Bauer, Finzelberg, PhytoLab; 4,300 people, over 30 sites, several in Poland), introduced Rhosera, an extract of cultivated Rhodiola rosea aimed at the ageing muscle. His case: by 2050 probably over 2 billion people will be 60 or older; with age the muscle cell uses protein less efficiently (anabolic resistance), which is why protein advice rises from 0.8 to 1.2 g, and hardly any ingredient addresses that gap [00:00]. Wild rhodiola is overharvested, adulterated and, he said, listed as endangered in 2023, so Martin Bauer has cultivated it for 25 years: over 240 accessions reduced over ten years to three elite lines, grown under controlled integrated cultivation in Central Europe, with the root taking three to four years [00:00]. The cultivated material has four to five times the precursor compounds of wild material and, in muscle cells, switches on Pax7, MyoD and polyamine pathways that wild material does not unless concentrated 2.5-fold [00:00]. A double-blind, placebo-controlled trial in 140 untrained adults aged 55 to 75, all training and all given protein, with 400 mg of Rhosera each morning, reduced delayed-onset muscle soreness by 46% in men and 52% in women against 18% and 11% on placebo, and improved chair stand, single-leg balance, bench press, leg press, fatigue and quality of life, "statistically highly significant" by his account [00:00]. Preclinical work is published in Antioxidants and the human paper is in progress; he recommends 250 to 350 mg, and because rhodiola has no EFSA claim, brands borrow magnesium or vitamin B fatigue claims [00:00].

## Key takeaways
- The gap he names is anabolic resistance: ageing muscle uses protein less well, hence the move from 0.8 to 1.2 g of protein, and "hardly any ingredient" addresses it [00:00].
- Wild Rhodiola rosea is overharvested, adulterated with other species and, he said, listed as endangered in 2023: a supply risk for any brand built on it [00:00].
- Martin Bauer has cultivated rhodiola for over 25 years: over 240 accessions to three elite lines in ten years; controlled integrated cultivation with no pesticides and manual weeding; three to four years to harvest [00:00].
- Cultivated material has the same compounds as wild but four to five times the precursors; in muscle cells it upregulates Pax7 and MyoD and polyamine pathways, so spermidine is made in the cell; wild material does the same only at 2.5-fold concentration [00:00].
- Trial: double-blind, placebo-controlled, 140 untrained adults aged 55 to 75 with slight muscle loss but no sarcopenia; both arms trained and were given protein; 400 mg once a day in the morning [00:00].
- Delayed-onset muscle soreness fell 46% in men and 52% in women, against 18% and 11% on placebo [00:00].
- Chair stand, single-leg balance with eyes closed, bench press and leg press improved "statistically highly significant"; hand grip was tested; fatigue fell; quality of life and social engagement rose [00:00].
- Preclinical results are published in Antioxidants; the human publication is "in progress" [00:00].
- Formulation: 250 to 350 mg; tablets, capsules, bars and V caps work; beverages are hard because rhodiola is very bitter; 100% plant-based, European grown [00:00].
- No EFSA claim on rhodiola; brands "borrow" magnesium or vitamin B claims on fatigue [00:00].
- The Nature Network is in Poland (Martin Bauer Polska and other units, as Otter heard them) and offers "science to shelf" [00:00].

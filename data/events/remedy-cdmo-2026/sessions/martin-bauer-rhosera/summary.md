# Martin Bauer: RhoSera, cultivated Rhodiola rosea against anabolic resistance

## Summary
Dr Ralf Spreemann of MartinBauer Nutraceuticals, a new unit of the family-owned Nature Network, introduced RhoSera, an extract of cultivated Rhodiola rosea for the ageing muscle. His question was what goes "beyond protein" for older people.

His case started with anabolic resistance. Ageing muscle responds less to protein and training, and he said hardly any ingredient addresses that gap. Wild rhodiola is overharvested, adulterated and now needs trade permits. So the group has spent decades breeding its own elite lines, grown in Germany, Denmark and the Netherlands. The cultivated root carries several times more of some compounds than wild material. In human muscle cells it switched on two stem-cell markers that wild material did not.

The human trial put 140 inactive adults aged 55 to 75 on training and protein, with RhoSera or placebo on top. Muscle soreness fell by about half on RhoSera, far more than on placebo. Strength, balance and fatigue also improved. He called them the best results of his career with a botanical. The trial is not yet published.

He closed with a typical dose of 250 to 350 mg and a regulatory note. Rhodiola has no authorised EFSA claim, so brands pair it with magnesium or B vitamins for fatigue claims.

## Key takeaways
- The gap he names is anabolic resistance: ageing muscle "responds less to protein and training", so protein advice rises from 0.8 to 1.2 g, and "hardly any ingredient" addresses it (slide 3) [00:00].
- Wild Rhodiola rosea is overharvested and adulterated with non-rosea species. Rhodiola spp. has been in CITES Appendix II since February 2023, so roots and extracts traded across borders need CITES documents (slide 4). He said it was listed "as an endangered species"; the slide says CITES Appendix II, which differs [00:00].
- In primary human muscle cells only the cultivated extract raised Pax7 and MyoD, the muscle stem-cell markers; wild harvest had no effect. Published as Iannuzzo et al., Antioxidants 2024;13:1000 (slide 8) [00:00].
- Trial: 12 weeks, 140 inactive adults aged 55 to 75, RhoSera 400 mg a day or placebo, both arms on the same training programme plus 60 g protein a day (slide 9). He described it as double-blind and placebo-controlled [00:00].
- Muscle soreness (DOMS) fell 46.3% in men and 51.7% in women, against 18.1% and 10.6% on placebo (slide 9, read from the chart); p = 0.004 and p < 0.001. He rounded these to 46%, 52%, 18% and 11% [00:00]. The data are "on file" and the manuscript is "in preparation" (slide 9).
- No authorised EFSA claim for rhodiola. On-hold areas (stress, physical and mental performance) are usable if substantiated, per national rules; pair with magnesium or B vitamins for authorised fatigue claims (slide 11) [00:00].

## Detail
- The Nature Network: privately owned, fourth generation. MartinBauer (founded 1930, "the vanguard of botanical solutions": cultivation and sourcing, big in tea and RTD drinks), Finzelberg ("the experts in botanical efficacy": extracts for pharma and supplements; he said 1875 and that he came from there) and PhytoLab (third-party lab: reference substances, contaminants) (slide 2) [00:00].
- Group figures: 90+ years, 30+ manufacturing sites and sales units, 4,300+ employees, 650+ million euro consolidated revenue (slide 2). Otter heard "over 30 here in Poland"; the slide's 30+ is the group's sites and sales units, which differs [00:00]. The deck names no Polish sites.
- The ageing population: people aged 60+ rise from 1.0 billion in 2020 to 2.1 billion in 2050, and from 12% of the world's population in 2015 to 22% in 2050 (WHO/UN; slide 3, read from the chart). He said "probably over 2 billion" by 2050 [00:00].
- 10 to 27% of people aged 60+ live with sarcopenia, depending on the definition (Petermann-Rocha et al., J Cachexia Sarcopenia Muscle 2022) (slide 3).
- RhodioFarm cultivation: own fields in Germany, Denmark and the Netherlands; young plants raised in Germany; a 3-year cycle; K.I.A. standard, "no chemical pesticides as a rule, none in the 6 months before harvest"; a PA avoidance programme and full batch traceability (slide 5). He said "Central Europe", three to four years to harvest and that farmers may not use pesticides; the slide's 3 years and pesticide wording differ [00:00].
- Cultivation history: 242 accessions collected 25+ years ago; 210 were true R. rosea (32 were not) and 143 survived cultivation (67 failed in the field); only 6 to 11% met single targets (root biomass, rosavin and salidroside, reproducibility) and none met all; about 1% entered breeding; today 3 elite lines (slide 6). He said "over 10 years" to reach the three elite lines; the slide says "decades of selective breeding followed", which differs [00:00].
- Cultivated against wild, by HRMS peak area: rhodiolin 4.8 times, quercetin glycoside 3.6 times, rhodiocyanoside A 3.4 times (Iannuzzo et al. 2024) (slide 7). He said a "four to five-fold concentration" of the precursors; the slide's range is 3.4 to 4.8 times [00:00]. Otter's "rhodiolene, glycosyl, rhodiocyanide A" are these compounds.
- The "salidroside paradox": the wild extract had 1.4% salidroside and RhoSera 0.8%, yet only RhoSera switched on Pax7 and MyoD (slide 7).
- Cell work: primary human myoblasts, 48 hours, 30 µg/mL (slide 8). Pax7 rose about 1.5-fold and MyoD about 2.3-fold over untreated with the cultivated extract; myogenin did not change (slide 8, read from the chart). The clinical tests to date on rhodiola used wild material, he said [00:00].
- He said the cultivated extract also triggers folate and polyamine pathways, so spermidine, spermine and putrescine are made in the cell [00:00]. The slide gives an estimated 2.5-fold "dose advantage" on folate and polyamine pathways against wild extract, "calculated from metabolite ratios, in vitro" (slide 8). He said wild material concentrated 2.5 times shows similar effects in cells [00:00].
- Trial population: slight muscle loss but no sarcopenia, considered healthy [00:00]. Registered as CTRI/2025/04/083930. No serious adverse events (slide 9).
- Other results, as % change from baseline to week 12 in each arm (slide 9): chair stand +36 to +40% against +9 to +14% on placebo; single-leg balance with eyes closed +35 to +48% against +3 to +18%; handgrip in women +13% against +5%; bench press in men +13% against +6%; all significant against placebo. Fatigue fell and social engagement and quality of life rose, significant in men and women; sleep quality improved significantly in women. He also named leg press as improved "statistically highly significant" [00:00]; the slide does not show it.
- Formulation: 250 to 350 mg a day typical in finished products; the study used 400 mg (slide 11). Hydro-ethanolic (70%) extract on maltodextrin; vegan, non-GMO, caffeine-free, preservative-free (slide 11).
- Formats: the slide lists capsules, soft-gels, tablets, instant powders and beverages (slide 11). He named tablets, capsules, bars and V caps, and called beverages a challenge because rhodiola is very bitter [00:00].
- Supply: "100% EU grown", zero wild sourcing, "CITES-compliant by design" (slides 10, 11). Otter's "100% cytosy compliance" is CITES [00:00].
- Support offered: a clinical study summary (data on file), the RhoSera ingredient brand and logo, regulatory guidance and formulation support from their NPD team (slide 11).
- RhoSera is a trademark of MB-Holding GmbH & Co. KG, marketed by MartinBauer Nutraceuticals; contact details are on the last slide (slide 12). No slide is marked confidential.

# Martin Bauer: Rhosera, cultivated Rhodiola rosea against anabolic resistance

## Summary
Ralph of Martin Bauer Nutraceuticals, a new unit of the family-owned Nature Network, introduced Rhosera, an extract of cultivated Rhodiola rosea aimed at the ageing muscle. His question was what goes "beyond protein" for older people.

His case started with anabolic resistance. With age the muscle cell uses protein less efficiently, which is why protein advice rises from 0.8 to 1.2 g, and he said hardly any ingredient addresses that gap. Rhodiola is a well-known adaptogen, but wild supply is overharvested and adulterated, so Martin Bauer has spent 25 years cultivating its own elite lines. The cultivated root carries far more of the precursor compounds than wild material and, in muscle cells, switches on muscle-building and polyamine pathways that wild material does not unless concentrated.

The human trial put 140 untrained adults aged 55 to 75 on training and protein, with Rhosera or placebo on top. By his account soreness after exercise fell by about half on Rhosera, and strength, balance, fatigue and quality of life all improved. He called them the best results he had seen with a botanical in 31 years.

He closed with formulation advice, a dose of 250 to 350 mg, and a regulatory note: rhodiola has no EFSA claim, so brands borrow fatigue claims from magnesium or B vitamins.

## Key takeaways
- The gap he names is anabolic resistance: ageing muscle uses protein less well, hence the move from 0.8 to 1.2 g of protein, and "hardly any ingredient" addresses it [00:00].
- Wild Rhodiola rosea is overharvested, adulterated with other species and, he said, listed as endangered in 2023: a supply risk for any brand built on it [00:00].
- Trial: double-blind, placebo-controlled, 140 untrained adults aged 55 to 75; both arms trained and were given protein; 400 mg of Rhosera once a day in the morning [00:00].
- Delayed-onset muscle soreness fell 46% in men and 52% in women, against 18% and 11% on placebo; the human publication is "in progress" [00:00].
- No EFSA claim on rhodiola; brands "borrow" magnesium or vitamin B claims on fatigue [00:00].

## Detail
- The Nature Network: privately owned, fourth generation; Martin Bauer (1930, cultivation and sourcing, big in tea and RTD drinks), Finzelberg (1875, extracts for pharma and supplements) and PhytoLab (third-party lab, reference substances, contaminants); 4,300 people and over 30 manufacturing sites, several in Poland (Martin Bauer Polska and other units, as Otter heard them) [00:00].
- By 2050 probably over 2 billion people will be 60 or older; muscle loss starts unnoticed at 25 or 30 and becomes a real problem by 60 [00:00].
- Cultivation: over 25 years of experience; over 240 accessions reduced over ten years to three elite lines; seedlings raised and transplanted; controlled integrated cultivation in Central Europe with no pesticides and manual weeding, to keep out pyrrolizidine alkaloid weeds; three to four years to harvest [00:00].
- Cultivated material has the same compounds as wild, but four to five times the precursors; the clinical tests to date used wild material [00:00].
- In muscle cells the cultivated extract upregulates Pax7 (waking dormant stem cells) and MyoD (telling them how to differentiate), plus folic acid and polyamine pathways, so spermidine, spermine and putrescine are made in the cell; wild material does the same only at 2.5-fold concentration [00:00].
- Trial population: slight muscle loss but no sarcopenia, considered healthy [00:00].
- Chair stand, single-leg balance with eyes closed, bench press and leg press improved "statistically highly significant"; hand grip, which he called one of the most important biomarkers for longevity, was tested; fatigue fell significantly; quality of life, motivation and social engagement rose [00:00].
- Preclinical results are published in Antioxidants [00:00].
- Formulation: 250 to 350 mg; tablets, capsules, bars and V caps work; beverages are hard because rhodiola is very bitter; 100% plant-based, European grown, with "100% cytosy compliance" as transcribed (probably CITES; to check) [00:00].
- The group offers a service "from science to shelf" [00:00].

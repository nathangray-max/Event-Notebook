To Conclude
• Longevity science is rich in mechanisms, but consumers experience outcomes.
• Many supplements show biomarker changes, but translation to meaningful functional benefits is
inconsistent.
• The most compelling categories in retail are those where consumers can feel or measure the
outcome
 (mobility, cognition, vision).
• Ingredients like curcumin, UC-II, and anthocyanins illustrate how mechanistic plausibility can align
with real-world outcomes.
• Others, such as NAD boosters, show strong molecular rationale but limited evidence for tangible
consumer benefits so far.
• For retailers, the challenge is translating emerging science responsibly without over-promising
outcomes.
• The opportunity lies in prioritising ingredients with both biological rationale and human outcome
data.

# Holland & Barrett: how longevity science translates into consumer outcomes

## Summary
Alex Glover, who leads Holland & Barrett's global own-label product formulation team, argued that consumers buy outcomes they can feel, not mechanisms [00:25]. He used a tortoise to show that ageing badly is not biologically fixed, and said the category should be about living better, not longer. H&B's data on millions of customers shows repeat purchase goes to products with a clearly communicated benefit (joint function, joint comfort, brain function), and its customer panels talk about playing with grandchildren and getting out of a chair, not sirtuins or telomeres. His evidence against mechanism-led novelty: of over 1000 customers surveyed, not one knew what NAD was, and the outcome-level data for nicotinamide riboside and mononucleotide is "very very limited", while cheap interventions (fibre, vitamin D, calcium, protein) have strong evidence; he gave about 9% lower coronary heart disease risk per seven grams of fibre and vitamin D at about two cents per 10 microgram serving. Curcumin, H&B's second best-selling ingredient globally ("one bottle a minute"), was his example of evidence stronger than the permitted claim.

## Key takeaways
- Customers do not buy the mechanism; they buy how they feel and keep feeling, so the retailer's recurring questions are when will I feel it, what will I feel, can I measure it, and how much does it cost [00:25].
- H&B's biggest own-label sellers have a clearly communicated benefit (joint function, joint comfort, brain function); products that recite clinical literature do not sell as well [00:25].
- Ingredient suppliers get "stuck" in mechanism and biomarkers; the customer outcome is the gap (sleep example: the Pittsburgh Sleep Inventory means nothing to a shopper) [00:25].
- NAD: over 1000 customers surveyed and "not a single one knew what NAD was"; outcome-level data for NR and NMN is "very very limited" (he cited an April 2026 meta-analysis and one trial on walking speed in the over-60s) [00:25].
- US shelves in the last six months are "protein plus fiber"; he gave about 9% lower coronary heart disease risk for every seven grams of fibre, and called fibre one of the biggest nutritional predictors of longevity [00:25].
- Cheap, evidence-rich interventions (fibre, vitamin D, calcium, protein) beat novel technologies on evidence and cost; vitamin D about two cents per 10 microgram serving [00:25].
- Curcumin is H&B's second best-selling ingredient globally, about one bottle a minute; the on-hold claim is joint flexibility and comfort while the evidence he cited covers knee osteoarthritis, function and systemic inflammation [00:25].
- When a benefit shows in trials at four, six or eight weeks, H&B can sometimes put that timescale on pack [00:25].
- He believes about 80% of how we age can be influenced, with an 81-year-old sprinter (Kenton Brown, 14.4 seconds for 100 metres) as his closing example [00:25].

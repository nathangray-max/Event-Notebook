# Holland & Barrett: how longevity science translates into consumer outcomes

## Summary
Alex Glover leads Holland & Barrett's global own-label product formulation team. He asked how longevity science becomes something a customer will buy, and his answer was that consumers buy outcomes they can feel, not mechanisms.

He opened with a tortoise to argue that ageing badly is not biologically fixed, and said the category should be about living better, not longer. H&B's data on millions of customers showed the pattern: repeat purchase goes to products with one clearly communicated benefit, such as joint function or brain function. Its customer panels talk about playing with grandchildren and getting out of a chair, not sirtuins or telomeres.

He then turned on mechanism-led novelty. Of over 1000 customers surveyed, not one knew what NAD was, and the outcome data for the NAD boosters is very limited. Cheap interventions with strong evidence, such as fibre, vitamin D, calcium and protein, do better on evidence and on cost. Curcumin, H&B's second best-selling ingredient, was his example of evidence running ahead of the permitted claim.

His conclusion for suppliers: answer the shopper's questions. When will I feel it, what will I feel, can I measure it, and what does it cost? He believes about 80% of how we age can be influenced.

## Key takeaways
- Customers do not buy the mechanism; they buy how they feel and keep feeling. The retailer's recurring questions are when will I feel it, what will I feel, can I measure it, and how much does it cost [00:25].
- H&B's biggest own-label sellers have a clearly communicated benefit (joint function, joint comfort, brain function); products that recite clinical literature do not sell as well [00:25].
- NAD: over 1000 customers surveyed and "not a single one knew what NAD was"; outcome-level data for NR and NMN is "very very limited" [00:25].
- Cheap, evidence-rich interventions (fibre, vitamin D, calcium, protein) beat novel technologies on evidence and cost; vitamin D costs about two cents per 10 microgram serving [00:25].
- US shelves in the last six months are "protein plus fiber"; he gave about 9% lower coronary heart disease risk for every seven grams of fibre [00:25].
- When a benefit shows in trials at four, six or eight weeks, H&B can sometimes put that timescale on pack [00:25].

# How a novel food ingredient unlocks new solutions for the Health & Wellness market. What can you learn from successful US brands?

## Summary
Thijs Bosch, CEO of The Protein Brewery and formerly a seller of dairy and fava proteins, presented Fermotein, a whole-food mycelium powder grown by biomass fermentation in Breda, in the Netherlands. His pitch was that one ingredient can carry protein, fibre and longevity bioactives together.

Fermotein is 50% protein and 30% fibre, and is rich in spermidine and vitamins. EFSA gave its safety opinion in 2025 and EU authorisation followed in 2026; approval took five years, he said. While waiting, the company built its market in the US, in "all-in-one nutrition": powders between protein powder and meal replacement, priced against the whole supplement basket. Ka'Chava is the model, growing fast in measured retail without discounting. In Europe the brands still sell meal replacement, and he asked the CDMOs in the room to embrace the all-in-one concept.

His evidence so far is laboratory work: prebiotic effects in a gut model, GLP-1 release in human colon tissue, and longer lifespan in nematodes. The slides say plainly that this is not evidence of an effect in humans. Two human trials with Wageningen come next.

He closed on scale: 10 million servings sold in 2026, three active US customers and €70 million raised. The company is not yet profitable.

## Key takeaways
- Fermotein is 50% protein with a complete amino acid profile, 30% fibre ("unique fungal fibres"), spermidine at "7x more than wheat germ", vitamin B6, vitamin B4, calcium, iron, zinc, phosphorus and magnesium (slide 5). Spoken: chitin and chitosan fibre, spermidine at about 1,700 mg per kilo, seven times "beet germ" as transcribed, and choline [00:00].
- Approvals: self-affirmed GRAS in the US and Singapore (SFA) approval in 2024, EFSA safety approval in 2025, EU authorisation and market launch in 2026 (slide 3). He said "since the summer of this year" after five years, with Australia and Canada pending [00:00].
- All-in-one nutrition is worth $0.5 to 1bn in the US against $8.6bn for protein supplements and $4.9bn for meal replacement (2025), and is priced at about $5 to $7 a serving against $1 to $2 for protein powder and $4 to $6 for meal replacement (slide 6) [00:00].
- Ka'Chava: $47.2M measured retail sales excluding direct sales, up 24.4% in dollars and 39.9% in units over 52 weeks to 07/12/2026 (SPINS); single-serve multipacks grow fastest, up 254% to over 1,000%, at the highest price per serving (slide 8). He called it "super profitable" [00:00].
- Evidence is preclinical: in a TNO gut model Fermotein raised butyrate, propionate and acetate and microbial diversity; in TNO human colon tissue it raised GLP-1 against a blank; in NemaLife's C. elegans study median lifespan rose 21.7%, 28 against 23 days (slide 11) (slide 12). He said 22% [00:00].
- Next: a 16-week human RCT on biological ageing clocks in adults aged 40 to 60, led by Dr Pol Grootswagers of Wageningen; a human spermidine bioavailability trial; and a NemaLife follow-up on lifespan and muscle health (slide 13). He said the ageing-clock trial starts in quarter one [00:00].

## For Vita Actives
- Relevance high, unchanged: a non-animal protein ingredient for active nutrition, now authorised in the EU, competing for the same brief as IsoClr: Pea (slide 3) (slide 5) [00:00]. My reading: at 50% protein it sells protein plus fibre plus longevity, not protein content; it will meet us in all-in-one and superfood blends, not in clear protein drinks.
- All-in-one is priced against the whole basket, at about $5 to $7 a serving against $1 to $2 for protein powder (slide 6). My reading: a premium European format where IsoClr: Pea and CreaWin could be the protein and performance pillars; the European meal-shake brands on his slide, BOL and vybey, are worth approaching (slide 10).
- He says Europe has not yet adopted all-in-one messaging (slide 10) [00:00]. My reading: a CDMO pitch for a European all-in-one shake, built on our ingredients, is open.
- The longevity science is laboratory-only and the company labels it so (slide 11) (slide 12). My reading: a fair benchmark for how plainly to present preclinical data in our own collateral.

## Detail
- Title slide: "Remedy Gdańsk – October 2026 – Thijs Bosch, CEO" (slide 1). His 15+ years in proteins run through Fonterra, Cosun and The Protein Brewery (slide 2); he said he ran a European dairy protein business at Fonterra, then sold fava proteins at Cosun [00:00].
- Slide 8 is marked "Property of The Protein Brewery · Strictly Private and Confidential" (slide 8). Its Ka'Chava figures stay internal.
- History: founded in 2020 by Wim de Laat; a 3,000 L pilot plant in 2021; a commercial demo plant and nine patents granted in 2024; two US customers booked all capacity for 2026 (2025); 150 tonnes run-rate capacity in 2026; the commercial plant has two 85,000 L fermenters (slide 3) [00:00].
- Funding: Series A €22M, Series B €30M and a Series B extension €18M, €70M in all (slide 3). He named Novo Holdings (transcribed "Nova Holdings") as the biggest shareholder and said the company is not yet profitable [00:00]; the slides name no shareholders.
- Process: a fungal strain grown in bioreactors forms hyphae of protein, prebiotic fibre and bioactives, and downstream processing turns it into a powder for shakes [00:00].
- Fermotein won the Vitafoods Europe Startup Innovation Challenge 2026 category "Most Innovative Sustainable Solution", and carries allergen-free, certified gluten-free, kosher and non-GMO marks (slide 5).
- Segments: protein powder skews younger, male, urban and athletic and is priced per gram of protein; all-in-one buyers are 30+, slightly female and "health optimisers"; meal replacement serves the time-poor and patients through carers. Growth: protein supplements +11.0% a year to 2032, meal replacement +6.9% to 2033, Ka'Chava +24.4% in 2026 (slide 6).
- Ka'Chava price per serving: $5.20 for the 2 lb bag (14.6 servings), $4.88 for the 15.31 oz tub (7 servings), $6.20 to $6.80 for the single-serve 5-pack (slide 8). He said the price rises as the format gets smaller [00:00]; on the slide the tub is cheaper per serving than the bag and only the single-serve pack costs more.
- US examples: Shakeology (plant protein and superfoods, "over 1 billion servings sold") with its own comparison against Vega One, Orgain and Ka'Chava; Ka'Chava as "Whole Body Nutrition" with 25 g plant protein, 26 vitamins and minerals and 85+ superfoods (slide 7). Vita Hustle ONE Superfood Protein, 20 g plant protein, 86 superfoods, under 160 calories, pre- and probiotics and ashwagandha, set against typical protein and greens powders such as AG1 (slide 9) [00:00].
- European examples are meal replacements: BOL "A meal in a bottle" (25 g+ protein, 9 g+ fibre) and vybey Complete Meal Shake (plant based, 29 g protein, with lion's mane and Bacillus coagulans) (slide 10). He named recent sales of meal replacement brands to Danone and Nestlé [00:00].
- TNO studies, both on Fermotein predigested by the INFOGEST method: gut-model fermentation over 24 h (TNO 2026 15116) at 38 mg/mL, equal to 12 g Fermotein (~4 g fibre); colon tissue over 1 h (TNO 2026 15096) at 15 mg/mL, about 5 g (slide 11).
- Alpha-diversity rose from 2.728 to 2.969 with Fermotein, while inulin's fell from 3.089 to 2.004, as labelled on the chart; the slide says inulin was tested undigested against a different control, "a platform benchmark, not a like-for-like comparator" (slide 11). GLP-1 reached about 440 pM against near zero for the blank, read from the chart (slide 11).
- NemaLife, a Texas Tech University spin-off founded by Prof. Siva Vanapalli, fed nematodes the water-soluble fraction (229.6 mg/mL, n=137 against 102 on standard diet; p<0.0001, log-rank). The dose equals 14 mg spermidine or about 8 g Fermotein, which the slide calls "indicative only" and "not a health claim" (slide 12). Otter's "Nema Live" is NemaLife [00:00].
- Upcoming studies: NemaLife at 7 and 14 mg human-equivalent spermidine, measuring lifespan, muscle health and RNA expression; a human spermidine bioavailability trial measuring blood spermidine and spermine, GLP-1, glucose and insulin; the Wageningen ageing-clock RCT, Fermotein against control, measuring autophagy, DNA methylation and hair growth (slide 13). Otter's "Paul Gössfaifers" is Pol Grootswagers [00:00].
- Progress: 10M servings sold in 2026, 3 active US customers, 80+ prospects in the EU pipeline, €70M raised, factory at scale (slide 14). He said the US customers are mainly in active nutrition, also all-in-one [00:00].
- Slide 14 shows Clean Machine MycelPro-1, a "whole-food mycelium protein" with 20 g protein, 10 g prebiotic fibre and 69 mg spermidine, labelled "Best Protein 2026" by Plant Based News (slide 14). My reading: one of the US customers.
- Slide 15 shows a concept pack, "PRIME Foundation+", "for illustration purposes, not for sale", and a vote for the Protein Production Technology International shortlist (slide 15) [00:00]. Samples, neutral and iced coffee, were at the company's table [00:00].

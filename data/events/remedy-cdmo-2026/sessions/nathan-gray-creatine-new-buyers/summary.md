# The gym & beyond: Creatine’s new buyers … and what they want

## Summary
Nathan Gray of Vita Actives told Remedy's contract manufacturers who is now buying creatine and what they want. Creatine launches grew 61.9% in the first eight months of 2026 against the same months of 2025, three times the average ingredient's growth over 2022 to 2025.

His four lessons came from launch data. First, creatine's benefits are moving beyond performance: hydration, brain and mood, energy and women-specific claims are rising, and launches in the supplements aisle outside sports nutrition are growing fastest. Second, combinations now grow faster than creatine on its own, with electrolytes, HMB, collagen, magnesium and citicoline as partners. Third, consumers want flavours and formats they enjoy: two in three launches were flavoured by 2025, and bars and chewables grow fastest. Fourth, new buyers expect proof of quality, and independent testing found that 18 of 88 creatine gummies failed their label claim.

Price follows the move. A kilogram of creatine sells at a retail median of €73 as plain powder and €275 in formats such as gummies.

He closed on CreaWin: a specification inside EFSA's residue limits, a certificate with every batch, stock in Europe, and concepts in fast-melt sticks, chewable tablets and fruit chews. His message: plain creatine is a commodity, and benefits, combinations, flavour and format are where the growth is.

## Key takeaways
- Creatine launches rose 61.9% in January to August 2026 against January to August 2025; over 2022 to 2025 they grew +35.6% against +11.1% for the average ingredient (Innova Market Insights; Vita Actives analysis) (slide 2).
- Retail price per kg of creatine, medians: €73 plain unflavoured powder, €87 flavoured powder (1.19×), €222 combination powder (3.03×), €275 formats such as gummies (3.76×) (slide 2).
- Beyond performance: pack claims on creatine launches, 2025 to 2026, hydration +107.1%, health and wellness +78.2%, brain and mood +61.5%, energy and stamina +60.0%, women-specific +400.0% (base below 10); creatine launches in the supplements aisle outside sports nutrition +194.7%; the EU-authorised claim for strength with training in adults over 55 is rarely used on pack (slide 3).
- Combinations grew 134.0% in 2026 against 52.7% for creatine on its own; the fastest partners are electrolytes +101.2% (hydration), magnesium +138.9% (sleep and overnight recovery), collagen +80.0% (recovery, skin and joints), and HMB and citicoline from a base below 10 (slide 4).
- Flavoured launches rose from 38.4% to 66.6% of creatine launches from 2022 to 2025, yet 82% of products on the shelf are still a single unflavoured SKU; in 2026 bars grew +260.0%, chewables +250.0%, powder +62.4% and gummies +29.5% (slide 5).
- Quality: 18 of 88 creatine gummies failed their label claim in independent testing and 10 contained no creatine (ABENUTRI 2024); in the only published market survey, half of commercial creatine failed at least one EFSA limit (Moret et al., 2011) (slide 6) (slide 7).

## Detail
- Prices are retail shelf prices per kg of creatine in launched products, medians (Innova Market Insights; Vita Actives analysis of retail listings). The slide also cites Nutrition Integrated (August 2026): in Europe the shelf price of creatine powder has held at about £60 per kg of creatine for a year. The two figures differ in source and currency; both are kept (slide 2).
- Pack claims, average year-on-year growth 2022 to 2025: hydration +50.5%, health and wellness +94.7%, brain and mood +126.6%, energy and stamina +78.9%, women-specific +105.4% (base below 10). The slide notes these are positioning on pack, not authorised claims (slide 3).
- Almost half of all creatine blends on the shelf arrived in the last six months (Nutrition Integrated, August 2026) (slide 4).
- Fastest flavours in 2026: chocolate +133.3%, vanilla +104.3%, tropical +93.4%. Gummies and chewables rose from 0.5% of creatine launches in 2022 to 18.3% in 2025, then fell to 15.7% in 2026 (Innova Market Insights; Nutrition Integrated) (slide 5).
- Launches carrying each quality claim, average 2022 to 2025 and 2025 to 2026: dose stated +61.7% and +64.9%; purity or micronised +76.0% and +62.8%; third-party tested +49.9% and +77.2%; named creatine ingredient +23.4% and +38.3%. These are claims on pack, not laboratory results (slide 6).
- EFSA limits the residues that matter to 50 to 100 ppm (EFSA 2004), while a creatine sold as 99.95% pure can hold up to 500 ppm of something else (slide 6).
- EFSA limits against CreaWin's specification: creatine monohydrate ≥99.95% against ≥99.95% by HPLC; creatinine ≤100 mg/kg against ≤50 ppm; dicyandiamide ≤50 mg/kg against ≤30 ppm; dihydrotriazine not detectable against not detected (LOD 5 ppm) (slide 7).
- The market survey (Moret et al., Food Chemistry 2011): half failed at least one EFSA limit, 44% exceeded the creatinine limit, about 15% exceeded the dicyandiamide limit, and dihydrotriazine was detected in about 15% (slide 7).
- The comparison chart, read from the chart: generic creatine drawn level with EFSA's limits on all three residues; an unnamed "premium creatine brand" at about 30 mg/kg dicyandiamide and about 67 mg/kg creatinine; CreaWin at about 30 and 50; dihydrotriazine not typically detected in either (slide 7).
- The offer: a certificate of analysis with every batch, stock held in Europe, supply at scale, and co-formulation with CogniWin citicoline, HMB, electrolytes, magnesium and collagen (slide 7).
- Concepts, shown as examples of what can be made: fast-melt stick packs (3 g of CreaWin in a 6 g stick, melts on the tongue); chewable tablets (1.5 g per tablet, fruit flavoured, 2.7× plain powder per kg at retail); fruit chews (1.67 g or 2.5 g per chew, 3.8×); CreaWin powder in 80, 200 and 500 mesh, micronised and high-flow grades; premix and companion-formulation support (slide 8).
- Take-aways 02 to 04: the fastest-growing partners match the fastest-growing claims (hydration, cognition, muscle, sleep and recovery); the commodity shelf is still one unflavoured product while innovative launches moved to flavour and new formats; buyers should ask their supplier for the residues, batch certificates and stock position (slide 9).
- Close: samples of raw materials, finished concepts and co-formulation options, from Nathan and Natalia at Vita Actives (slide 10).

# Closing panel: evidence, regulation, supply and AI in nutraceuticals

## Summary
The day's closing panel brought together a retailer (Alex Glover, Holland & Barrett), a CDMO (Martina), an ingredient developer (Matt), a food lawyer (Joanna) and a pharmaceutical company (Alexandra). The moderator asked what each wants in the next two or three years, then turned to regulation, supply and AI.

They agreed that the consumer comes first. Alexandra wanted evidence-based claims and better communication, Martina a focus on what consumers actually need, and Alex a few claims done well, starting from the consumer's problem rather than piling on ingredients. Matt wanted a revolution in clinical trials: high-dose studies in people with real problems, published where consumers' chatbots will find them.

On regulation they differed in tone. Joanna wants food law to be a shield for innovators, though the claims system was written for vitamins. Matt was blunter: a three-year longer approval in Europe than in the US almost makes innovation impossible.

On supply, Matt warned of a fish oil shortage reaching shelves next summer. Alex described pre-bought stock and protecting the claim shoppers buy. Joanna said blaming the supplier is no defence.

On AI they wanted a human in the loop and peer-reviewed sources, and Matt warned that anyone who cannot use AI will not be able to work in two years.

## Key takeaways
- Matt: trials in this industry study healthy people for short periods, so effects look small; he would run high-dose studies in people with real problems and publish, because consumers will find it through chatbots [02:52].
- Alex: consumers want evidence but not the EFSA wording; "throwing the kitchen sink" (90 plus ingredients, 35 claims) muddles the message; do a few things well for a defined audience [08:33].
- Joanna: EU food law and the claims regulation were written for vitamin C and D; innovators must combine what is legally possible with showing what trials found; food law should be "a shield", not "a heartbreak" [05:36].
- Matt: same safety package, three years longer approval in Europe than the US; cost today, income later, so the delay "almost makes the innovation impossible" [14:53].
- Matt: fish oil supply is collapsing for aquaculture now and reaches supplement shelves next summer; aquaculture buys the alternatives with higher willingness to pay [18:48].
- Alex: H&B holds up to nine months of pre-purchased stock on core lines; shoppers give three or four seconds at shelf, so protect the claim they buy (heart, eye) and find the alternative [19:36].

## Detail
- Wants for the next two or three years: evidence-based claims and better communication to consumers (Alexandra [02:10]); a "revolution" in clinical trials, high-dose studies in people with real problems, published (Matt [02:52]); a focus on what consumers actually need (Martina [04:30]); food law as "a shield" for innovators (Joanna [05:36]).
- Matt, in his words: "I would do high dose studies, you know, much higher than what you sell, but do people that have actually real problems, and then publish it, and then the consumer is going to find it through their chatbots" [02:52].
- Matt: AI assistants rank credibility, peer-reviewed science and university pages, after LinkedIn and Reddit were gamed; the language in published studies matters [03:45], [03:59].
- Alex: H&B's own label has "customer missions" (joint, bone and muscle care; skin; weight management); where it wants to win it runs primary research with universities, clinical trials and standardised or branded ingredients; the lesson of seven years is start with the consumer problem [08:33].
- Martina: legislation is an obstacle for a CDMO; do not fill every gap, stick to one piece and explain it; the customer, not the CDMO, has the power to sell [11:13], [12:26].
- Alexandra: "what is on the box should be in the box during the shelf life"; brand means trust and quality [13:33].
- Matt: micro-influencer social selling arriving from China via the US is "almost impossible to regulate" [14:53].
- Matt on fish oil: "it's not going to hit us on the shelves, on your shelves, until next summer, because we have a much longer value chain" [18:48]. The risks are losing consumers to other ways of managing health, and shortcuts where traceability is absent [21:19].
- Alex: rhodiola's endangered listing brought about 400 customer emails [19:36].
- Joanna: liability stays with you; share responsibility by contract; "it's my supplier's fault" is not a defence [22:09].
- AI: a human in the loop for anything legally binding and for IP (Joanna [23:33]); peer-reviewed databases, not blogs (Alexandra [24:59]); a ChatGPT over factory sensors (Matt [25:42]).
- Panellists' own supplements: Alex takes creatine daily, omega-3, astaxanthin, vitamin D in winter and pre-workout; Matt protein, creatine, magnesium, zinc, vitamin D; Martina magnesium and krill oil [00:30], [00:51], [00:57].
- Closing asks: "speak the language of the consumer" (Alex [30:07]); bring lawyers in at the laboratory stage (Joanna [27:27], [28:09]); talk to people outside your company (Matt [28:55]); think what you want, not what the market says (Martina [29:34]).

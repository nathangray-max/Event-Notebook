# Kaneka: Ubiquinol, the active form of coenzyme Q10

## Summary
Oliver of Kaneka presented ubiquinol, the reduced and active form of coenzyme Q10, which Kaneka makes in Japan. He argued that from about 30 the enzyme diaphorase falls away, so the body converts less Q10 to ubiquinol and should be given the active form directly [00:00]. He cited a blood test in which 90 mg of ubiquinol gave the same blood level as 300 mg of Q10, and said Q10 above 300 mg a day is not converted [00:00]. Kaneka recommends 100 mg a day, up to 300 mg for people on statins, under stress or seeking to have children, and lists heart, energy, mitochondrial, reproductive, cognition and menopause benefits, resting on what he said are more than 100 studies [00:00]. The ingredient comes pure for softgels, gel pills and gummies or microencapsulated for other formats, with halal certification, a partnership with Borussia Dortmund's women's team and a warehouse in Belgium for short lead times [00:00].

## Key takeaways
- Ubiquinol is the active form of CoQ10; the speaker says the converting enzyme diaphorase is no longer available as you get older, from about 30 [00:00].
- 300 mg of Q10 gives the same blood ubiquinol as 90 mg of ubiquinol; at 300, 600 or 900 mg of Q10 the body cannot convert more than 300 mg [00:00].
- Kaneka's dose: 100 mg a day for a normal person; up to 300 mg for statin users, energy, fertility and stress [00:00].
- Claimed areas, the speaker's: immune support, energy, health span, mitochondrial health, heart, male and female reproductive health, cognition, menopause [00:00].
- Formats: pure ubiquinol for softgels, gel pills and gummies; a microencapsulated form for other dosage formats [00:00].
- Made in Japan, more than 100 studies, halal certified, served from a warehouse in Belgium; sponsor of Borussia Dortmund's women's team and "MTIC certified" in the UK [00:00].
- He opened on Nick Morgan's product coding, which placed CoQ10 second among longevity ingredients [00:00].

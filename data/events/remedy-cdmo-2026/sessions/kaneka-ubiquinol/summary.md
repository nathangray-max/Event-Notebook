# Kaneka: Ubiquinol, the active form of coenzyme Q10

## Summary
Oliver of Kaneka presented ubiquinol, the reduced and active form of coenzyme Q10, which Kaneka makes in Japan. His question was why a brand should use ubiquinol rather than ordinary Q10.

His argument began with conversion. From about 30, he said, the enzyme diaphorase falls away, so the body turns less Q10 into ubiquinol; giving the active form directly gets round that. He cited a blood test in which 90 mg of ubiquinol gave the same blood level as 300 mg of Q10, and said the body cannot convert Q10 doses above 300 mg. He then listed the benefits Kaneka claims, from heart and energy to fertility, cognition and menopause, which he said rest on more than 100 studies.

He closed on Kaneka as a supplier: 100 mg a day for most people and up to 300 mg for statin users, fertility and stress; grades for softgels, gummies and other formats; halal certification; and a Belgian warehouse for short lead times in Europe.

## Key takeaways
- Ubiquinol is the active form of CoQ10; the speaker says the converting enzyme diaphorase is no longer available as you get older, from about 30 [00:00].
- 300 mg of Q10 gives the same blood ubiquinol as 90 mg of ubiquinol; at 300, 600 or 900 mg of Q10 the body cannot convert more than 300 mg [00:00].
- Kaneka's dose: 100 mg a day for a normal person; up to 300 mg for statin users, energy, fertility and stress [00:00].
- Formats: pure ubiquinol for softgels, gel pills and gummies; a microencapsulated form for other dosage formats [00:00].
- Kaneka is pushing ubiquinol into active nutrition: "energy and sports" among its study areas, and a partnership with Borussia Dortmund's women's football team [00:00].

# Kaneka: Ubiquinol, the active form of coenzyme Q10

## Summary
Oliver Romanow of Kaneka presented Kaneka Ubiquinol, the reduced and active form of coenzyme Q10, which Kaneka makes in Japan by yeast fermentation. His question was why a brand should use ubiquinol rather than ordinary Q10.

His argument began with conversion. The enzymes that turn Q10 into ubiquinol decline with age, he said, so giving the active form directly gets round that. He showed blood levels after 28 days in which 90 mg of ubiquinol matched 300 mg of Q10, while higher Q10 doses added little. He then listed the benefits Kaneka claims, from heart and energy to fertility, cognition and menopause, which the deck says rest on more than 100 studies.

He closed on Kaneka as a supplier: 100 mg a day for most people and 300 mg to restore or boost energy and for fertility; a pure grade for softgels and gummies and a stabilised grade for tablets, bars, shots, sticks and gels; a long list of certifications; and a Belgian warehouse for short lead times in Europe.

The deck leans on sport more than the talk did. It sets out a loading protocol before marathons and triathlons, counts 16 energy and sports studies, and shows a partnership with Borussia Dortmund's women's team.

## Key takeaways
- Ubiquinol is the reduced, active form of CoQ10. The speaker said the converting enzyme diaphorase is no longer available from about 30 [00:00]; the slide shows NQO1 activity falling 88% between 15 and 60 years (slide 2).
- Blood levels after 28 days, read from the chart: 90 mg of ubiquinol about 2.8 µg/ml against about 2.7 for 300 mg of oxidised CoQ10; 300 mg of ubiquinol about 7.3 against about 3.2 for 900 mg of CoQ10 (slide 9). Spoken as equivalent at 90 against 300 mg [00:00].
- Dose: 100 mg a day (to 200 mg for one month) to maintain energy levels; 300 mg to restore or boost energy and for fertility support (slide 11). Spoken, 300 mg is also for statin users and people under stress [00:00]; the slide names neither.
- Sport: "Ubiquinol loading", every day for seven days before events such as marathons and triathlons and five days after, sits under the 300 mg dose (slide 11). The Energy and Sports block counts 16 studies, on energy and fatigue, oxidative stress, inflammation and muscle recovery (slide 13).
- Formats: pure ubiquinol for softgels, liquid-filled hard capsules, gummies and beadlets; "ultra stabilized" ubiquinol for tablets, capsules, bars, shots, sticks, gels, gums and coffee or tea capsules (slide 12). Spoken as "a microencapsule form" [00:00].
- Claimed evidence base: 100+ studies, 80+ patents, 45+ years of research; heart health 23 studies, healthy ageing and cognition 23, fertility 18, energy and sports 16, women and beauty from within 4 (slide 13).

## Detail
- He opened on Nick Morgan's product coding, which he said placed CoQ10 second among longevity ingredients [00:00].
- Conversion: Q10 is activated by DT-diaphorase (NQO1) and thioredoxin reductase, using NADPH (slide 2). NQO1 activity is 100% at 15 years, about 55% at 30 and about 12% at 60, read from the chart (Shih et al., 2007) (slide 2). The speaker said the enzyme is not available from maybe 30 [00:00]; the slide shows a decline, not an absence, so the two differ.
- Also on conversion: 30-50% of people have reduced capacity to convert Q10 due to genetic polymorphism (Kelsey et al., 1997); a high glycaemia level (fasting plasma glucose 5.6-6.9 mmol/L) reduces Q10 activation by 50% (Lim et al., 2006) (slide 2).
- Blood-level chart, all three pairs read from the chart: 90 mg ubiquinol about 2.8 against 300 mg CoQ10 about 2.7; 150 mg about 3.8 against 600 mg about 3.1; 300 mg about 7.3 against 900 mg about 3.2 µg/ml, plasma level after 28 days (slide 9). The ubiquinol bars cite Hosoe K. et al., 2007 and the CoQ10 bars Ikematsu H. et al., 2006 (slide 9). My reading: two studies set side by side, not one head-to-head trial.
- The speaker said the body cannot convert Q10 doses above 300 mg [00:00]; the chart shows CoQ10 plasma levels rising little from 300 to 900 mg (slide 9).
- In "mitochondrial and CoQ10 deficient subjects", mitochondrial total CoQ10 was about 95 ng/mg protein on placebo, about 85 on CoQ10 and about 225 on ubiquinol, read from the chart; the slide gives no source (slide 9).
- Antioxidant: the slide says ubiquinol exceeds vitamin E and C antioxidant activity, with a bar marked "+30%", in vitro (Cervellati et al., Helv. Chim. Acta, 2016) (slide 4). The speaker said it supports "the intake of vitamin C or vitamin E up to 30%" [00:00]; the two differ.
- "Triple action": a cofactor for cellular energy production, an antioxidant protecting mitochondria against reactive oxygen species, and a contributor to the biogenesis of mitochondria (slide 3).
- At the cellular level, Kaneka Ubiquinol increased SIRT-1 and PGC-1α ("the master regulator of mitochondrial biogenesis") mRNA against placebo, p<0.05 (Pérez-Sánchez et al., 2017) (slide 6). Read from the chart, on a broken axis: about +180 for SIRT-1 and about +700 for PGC-1α relative expression (slide 6).
- A slide on mitochondrial dysfunction lists conditions by organ, including skeletal muscle weakness, fatigue and myopathy (Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine); the slide says ubiquinol "helps to protect against mitochondrial dysfunction" (slide 7).
- Ageing: ubiquinol level at 80 against 20 years is 42.9% in the heart, 51.7% in the lungs, 65.3% in the kidneys and 83.0% in the liver; at 40 it is 68.2% in the heart, 72.6% in the kidneys and 93.5% in the liver (Kalén et al., Lipids, 1989) (slide 10). Spoken: the heart needs ubiquinol most, hence "the heart vitamin" [00:00].
- Consumer survey: over 100 consumers aged 50+ took 100 mg a day for 28 days; 7 in 10 would recommend it to a friend and 8 in 10 felt an improvement in overall health (slide 10).
- Food equivalent of 100 mg a day: 10 kg of broccoli, 1.6 kg of sardines or 3 kg of beef (slide 11). Spoken as 60 avocados or 3.5 kg of beef [00:00]; the beef figures differ.
- Claimed areas, the speaker's: immune support, energy performance, healthspan against lifespan, mitochondrial health, heart, male and female reproductive health, cognition, menopause (slide 5).
- Study sub-areas: heart (cardiac and endothelial function, glycaemic control, blood pressure); healthy ageing (brain health, cognitive function, fatigue, migraine); women (PMS, menopause, skin turnover and hydration); fertility (sperm quality, ovarian function, oocyte quality, oxidative stress) (slide 13). The five blocks add to 84 studies against the headline 100+ (slide 13).
- Supplier: maker of Kaneka Ubiquinol since 2007, by "ultrapure, natural yeast fermentation", with pharmaceutical-grade manufacturing practices; stability tested in tablets, softgels, capsules, liquid, powder and gummies (slide 15).
- Certifications shown: TGA, GMP, GRAS, HACCP, NDI, OSHMS, halal, kosher, non-GMO, non-animal origin, ISO 14001 (printed "140001") and ISO 22000 (slide 15). Spoken: halal is the latest [00:00].
- Awards shown: NutraIngredients Asia Awards winner 2026 (Ingredient of the Year: Healthspan) and 2024 (Healthy Ageing), finalist 2025 (women's health); Vitafoods Europe Innovation Awards 2026 (most innovative consumer-ready nutraceutical finished product); Natural Health Products NZ 2025 marketing winner; a 2025 Tmall Global marketing innovation award; CMA raw material supplier award 2024 (slide 16).
- "MTIC certified" in the recording is MTick® certified, "Menopause Friendly", with Kaneka an "Official GenM Partner, supporting women's health through the menopause transition" (slide 16). The speaker placed it in the UK and called it "female-friendly" [00:00]; the slide does not name a country.
- Kaneka Ubiquinol is a "Proud Partner" of BVB Frauen, Borussia Dortmund's women's team (slide 16).
- Europe is served from a warehouse in Belgium for short lead times [00:00]; the slides do not mention it.
- The deck carries no "Confidential" marking.

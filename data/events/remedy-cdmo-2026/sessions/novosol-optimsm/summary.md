# Aquanova NovaSol micellar delivery, then Balchem OptiMSM

## Summary
Two short talks in one recording. Aquanova's speaker (Otter: "Toshton Sack") presented NovaSol, a micellar encapsulation technology that makes lipophilic ingredients water-soluble: a water-free, homogeneous liquid concentrate in which the native ingredient sits in a micelle with a lipophilic core and a hydrophilic surface, protected from stomach acid, made by physical processing with no chemical change [00:18]. Each formulation is unique to its ingredient; ready products include NovaSol curcumin, astaxanthin and coenzyme Q10, for capsules, shots and combinations [00:18]. His examples were Favea's cap-in-cap product Nourreflex PD and the US longevity brand GVR, which built three products on the same micellar resveratrol plus different NovaSol actives [00:18]. Balchem's Lena Leder, senior manager nutrition science, then began on OptiMSM: Balchem has 33 active research projects across chelated minerals, choline, vitamin K2 and methylfolate [09:04], and MSM is 34% sulfur by weight with evidence, she said, for joint and skin health and exercise recovery [10:40]. The recording ends about three minutes into her talk, before the data.

## For Vita Actives
Neither technology touches our ingredients directly, but both show what our customers' manufacturers are buying. Micellar delivery is a format play: it lets a capsule or shot maker differentiate on bioavailability rather than on the ingredient, and the cap-in-cap and three-product GVR examples are the finished-product systems Nick Morgan described [00:18]. My reading: delivery technology is where a CDMO earns its margin, so when we pitch creatine or clear pea protein to a Polish contract manufacturer, "which format can you make it work in" will come before price. Balchem is a supplier to the same sports and active nutrition brands we serve, and OptiMSM is positioned on exercise recovery [10:40]. Relevance medium for that and for the format lesson.

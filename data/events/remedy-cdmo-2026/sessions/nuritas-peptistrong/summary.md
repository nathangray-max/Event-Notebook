# Nuritas: AI-discovered plant peptides and the PeptiStrong trials

## Summary
Miguel, business developer, and Brian Keogh, chief scientific officer, presented Nuritas, a Dublin R&D company that uses its own AI platform to find bioactive plant peptides. The AI starts from a mechanism of action or a consumer need and returns candidate peptides for lab and animal testing.

Keogh then made the case for PeptiStrong, a fava bean (Vicia faba) protein hydrolysate. The speaker says its peptides build muscle through mTOR, protect it from breakdown and calm inflammation through NF-κB. In mice it cut the muscle lost when the hind legs were unloaded by 54.3%. Three published human trials followed. After immobilisation, strength came back to baseline against a milk protein control. After a damaging exercise bout, strength recovered faster and fatigue was lower than with placebo. In untrained men and women, strength rose faster, with more bone mineral content as an unexpected finding.

His conclusion was that PeptiStrong boosts protein rather than competing with it. His slides add cell data with leucine and HMB, and ask whether it can augment creatine.

Miguel closed on the commercial side. EU on-pack claims rest on protein claims, with brand callouts beside them. PeptiSleep, a sleep peptide, is next. Vivatis Pharma and Tantus are partners, with a prototype PeptiStrong tablet shown.

## Key takeaways
- PeptiStrong is a Vicia faba (fava bean) protein hydrolysate; the speaker says it works three ways: building muscle (mTOR), protecting it from breakdown (ubiquitination) and calming inflammation (NF-κB) [03:56] (slide 8).
- Immobilisation trial (Weijzen et al. 2023, The Journal of Nutrition; men aged 18-35, 20 g a day): muscle size changed the same as with milk protein concentrate, which was the control, not a placebo. Leg press strength recovered to 103% of baseline against 68% with milk protein, and muscle protein synthesis in recovery was about four times higher (read from the chart) [03:56] (slide 10).
- Strength recovery trial (Kerr et al. 2023, Nutrients; men aged 30-45, 2.4 g a day for 17 days, muscle damage on day 14): peak torque was higher than placebo at 72 hours (p=0.025), with 36% less fatigue at 48 hours and 47% less at 72 hours (read from the chart) [03:56] (slide 11).
- Strength and endurance trial (Mohan et al., BMJ Nutrition, Prevention & Health; men and women aged 19-40, 2.4 g a day for 56 days): leg extension 1RM higher at day 28 and day 56, endurance higher at day 56, bone mineral content up ("a serendipitous finding"), and 51.85% against 14.29% on placebo rated their health above 95 [03:56] (slide 12).
- Nuritas positions PeptiStrong as a booster of protein and a partner for leucine, HMB and creatine: "we're not competing with protein". The leucine and HMB data are from muscle cells; the creatine case is in vitro data for each ingredient on its own [03:56] (slides 13, 14).
- EU claims are protein claims ("source of protein" needs at least 12% of energy from protein), with US structure and function claims and brand callouts beside them; Vivatis Pharma and Tantus show a prototype PeptiStrong tablet with Nuritas [13:50] (slides 15, 17).

## Detail
- The deck is titled "AI-Discovered Plant Peptides: The Next Frontier in Longevity & Healthy Aging", "Nuritas Company Overview | Nutrafood Poland", Gdańsk, 22 October 2026. Nuritas marks its title slide and the PeptiSleep slide "Confidential" (slides 1, 16).
- Nuritas is an R&D company in Dublin; its own AI goes from a mechanism, consumer need or pathway to peptide candidates, then in vitro and in vivo [00:20]. The name is Nutrition + Veritas (slide 3).
- Discovery time differs between the two sources. Miguel said about two years on average from first hypothesis to result [00:20]; the slide headline says "What Once Took Decades Now Happens in Months" (slide 2).
- Target areas on the slide: sleep support, muscle strength and recovery, cognitive performance, and metabolic and overall wellness (slide 2). A peptide is defined as a chain of 2 to 50 amino acids (slide 4).
- The three things Miguel asked the audience to remember: artificial intelligence, a plant-based source, and the bioactivity of peptides [00:20].
- Potential effects listed: better glucose uptake, lower cortisol, more muscle protein synthesis, less inflammation [03:56] (slide 5).
- The 17% figure is worded differently. Keogh said that if we keep our muscles healthy "we get a 17% decrease in all-cause mortality" [03:56]; the slide says the 17% decrease is "associated with increasing muscle mass", citing "Sarcopenia: an emerging geriatric giant" (amjournal.co.uk) (slide 7).
- The same slide says muscle loss starts at age 30, skeletal muscle is up to 35% of body weight, and the usual sports actives (dairy protein, BCAAs, creatine, HMB) "typically target one pathway" (slide 7).
- Mouse hind-limb unloading (C57BL/6 mice, 650 mg/kg/day of PeptiStrong or casein for 18 days): the control lost 33.4% of soleus weight and PeptiStrong reduced that loss by 54.3%; the slide headline says "more effectively than milk protein" (slide 9). The recording has about 50% ("prevent rather 50% of that loss") [03:56]; the slide figure is exact. Circulating IL-6 was 60% lower than the control (read from the chart) (slide 9).
- Immobilisation trial design: supplement from day -2, leg immobilised to day 7, recovery to day 21, with a D2O tracer, muscle biopsies and 1RM tests (slide 10). Creatine kinase was higher with milk protein than PeptiStrong after recovery, p=0.045 (read from the chart) (slide 10).
- Reference: Weijzen, M.E., Holwerda, A.M., Jetten, G.H., Houben, L.H., Kerr, A., Davis, H., Keogh, B., Khaldi, N., Verdijk, L.B. and van Loon, L.J., 2023. "Vicia Faba peptide network supplementation does not differ from milk protein in modulating changes in muscle size during short-term immobilization and subsequent remobilization, but increases muscle protein synthesis rates during remobilization in healthy young men." The Journal of Nutrition (slide 10). The Netherlands group Otter heard as "Luke Van Noon" [03:56] is Luc J.C. van Loon's.
- The sexes differ between the two sources. Keogh said the first trial was in males only and every trial since includes men and women [03:56]; the slides show both the immobilisation trial and the strength recovery trial were in men (slides 10, 11).
- Strength recovery trial: Keogh said 14 days' supplementation before the damage, done in a Dublin sports clinic in a trained population [03:56]. The slide shows 17 days in all, with the muscle injury on day 14 and peak torque tested again two and three days later (slide 11). The chart marks gaps of 43% at 48 hours and 148% at 72 hours against placebo (read from the chart) (slide 11).
- In the same trial, myostatin rose after the damage with placebo but stayed near baseline with PeptiStrong, and irisin rose right after the damage then returned to normal (read from the chart) (slide 11). Reference: Kerr, Alish, et al. "Improved Strength Recovery and Reduced Fatigue with Suppressed Plasma Myostatin Following Supplementation of a Vicia faba Hydrolysate, in a Healthy Male Population." Nutrients 15.4 (2023): 986 (slide 11).
- Both later trials give the dose as "2.4g PeptiStrong or MCC daily"; the MCC arm is labelled placebo in the charts (read from the slide image) (slides 11, 12).
- Strength and endurance trial: Keogh said it was run with KGK, a CRO in Canada, in gym-familiar but untrained people on a supervised, progressive programme, with DEXA [03:56]. KGK is not named on the slide (slide 12). Reference: Mohan, Niamh Máire, Nora Khaldi, Brian Keogh and Andy Franklyn Miller, "Randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled study to evaluate the effect on human strength and endurance after resistance training and supplementation of Vicia faba protein hydrolysate compared with placebo", BMJ Nutrition, Prevention & Health (slide 12).
- In that trial, repetitions to exhaustion at 80% of 1RM rose by about 4.5 against about 2.3 on placebo at day 56 (read from the chart). FSTL-1 and fractalkine were higher and GDF-15 lower with PeptiStrong (read from the chart) (slide 12). Keogh linked the GDF-15 fall to the training load [03:56].
- IL-6 moved in opposite directions on two slides: lower than the control in mice (slide 9), and a higher fold change than placebo in the strength and endurance trial (read from the chart) (slide 12).
- The creatine slide: creatine monohydrate gives ATP through the phosphocreatine system; PeptiStrong increases muscle protein synthesis through mTOR, "Elevates OxPhos-produced ATP in vitro", acts independently of leucine signalling and "Reduces myostatin production post-exercise" (slide 13).
- Its ATP charts are in vitro and show each ingredient on its own: total ATP production rose from about 700 pmol/min untreated to about 1,000 with 2 mM creatine and about 950 with 50 µg/ml PeptiStrong (read from the chart) (slide 13). A "Combined product" column lists benefits such as "Improves max strength and power output", with no combination data on the slide (slide 13). Keogh argued the creatine case "in theory" [03:56].
- Leucine and HMB: Keogh said a dose of each that does nothing on its own, added to a dose of PeptiStrong that does nothing on its own, boosts the effect of both [03:56]. The slide shows this as mTOR signalling (phospho-S6) in young and aged muscle cells. It adds that leucine transporters SLC7A5 and SLC3A2 are "markedly reduced in aged muscle cells", and that blocking leucine transport suppresses leucine's effect while PeptiStrong's signalling is maintained (slide 14).
- Claims slide. EU: "PeptiStrong is a source of protein", "Protein contributes to a growth in muscle mass", "Protein contributes to the maintenance of muscle". US structure and function claims include "Increases muscle strength recovery rate after exercise" and "Clinically studied to combat muscular fatigue after exercise". Brand callouts include "Patented plant-based peptides" and "AI-discovered ingredient" (slide 15).
- The "clouds" the regulatory manager has been "creatively defining" [13:50] are most likely the slide's "Brand callouts" (my reading) (slide 15).
- Nuritas has a library of more than 9 million peptides [13:50]. The sleep peptide is PeptiSleep, "Natural Plant Peptides For Your Deepest Sleep Yet" (slide 16); Miguel called it non-hormonal, with a peripheral mechanism, formulated by Vivatis and handed out in the sampling bag [13:50]. The chair called it "Pepsi sleep" [16:11].
- Vivatis Pharma's "Day & Night Sticks": a Koncentra "Rise 'n Shine" stick (lemon, 4 g, "With caffeine alternative") and a PeptiSleep "Relax 'n dream" stick (berries, 4 g, "With Melatonin & plant-based peptides"), as written on the pack visuals (read from the slide image) (slide 17). The stick lists melatonin; Miguel's "no hormonal" was said of the peptide [13:50].
- The same slide shows "Peptide Bite", a prototype from Nuritas, Tantus and Vivatis Pharma: a daily tablet with PeptiStrong for "an active, healthy-ageing lifestyle", 20 tablets, one a day, marked "Concept visual · not for sale", with a QR code for samples (read from the slide image) (slide 17). Miguel said Nuritas is developing a product with Tantus and has been active in Poland for six months [13:50].
- The last slide names five Nuritas ingredients: PeptiStrong, PeptiYouth, PeptiSleep, PeptiProtect and PeptiPump. Its product display is an illustration with partly unreadable labels, including tubs labelled creatine and one "Creatine + Peptide" (read from the slide image) (slide 18).

In the tub/research: what does 300 mg mean?
The same number on a label can describe very different doses
Total polyphenols
The whole extract, all compounds added together
A subclass
e.g. anthocyanins
A single compound
e.g. quercetin
Why the numbers don’t travel
1 Method
Quick “total phenolics” tests also react with vitamin C and other
compounds, so can overstate content
2 Source and processing
Variety, harvest and processing change the blend, e.g. cherry
juice vs powder
3 Batch to batch
Content varies between batches, so each batch should be
tested
4 Standardisation
“Standardised to X%” only helps if it says X% of what.
Three questions for any polyphenol ingredient:  Which compound?  How much?  Measured how?
Bowtell & Kelly (2019) Sports Med 49(Suppl 1):S3–S23; Howatson & Clifford (2026) Exp Physiol

Not just
antioxidants
Levels in the blood are far lower
than the body’s own antioxidants,
so polyphenols can’t mop up free
radicals directly.
Instead, they act as signals that
switch on the body’s own
defences.

What good looks like
Better evidence, and better ways to get polyphenols where they need to go
Evidence that moves the field
✓ Standardised extract: which compound, and how much
✓ Total polyphenol dose and dose of primary anothocyanins
✓ Trained athletes and women, not just recreational men
✓ Blood levels measured alongside outcomes
✓ Performance and strength recovery, not just biomarkers
✓ Pre-registered, independent and properly powered
Howatson & Clifford (2026) Exp Physiol; Bowtell & Kelly (2019) Sports Med 49(Suppl 1):S3–S23

Lessons from beyond polyphenols
Two ingredients that built awareness by teaching people how to use them
Dietary nitrate (beetroot)
•  University research from around 2009 showed nitrate
improved endurance efficiency
•  Products standardised to a nitrate dose in a small shot
•  The message became a usage rule: one shot, 2–3 hours before
and/or for 1 week prior to competition.
Lesson: Make the dose and timing the message
Nomio (broccoli sprout extract)
•  Sulforaphane is not a polyphenol, but the same Nrf2 story
•  Built through endurance athletes and influencers explaining
how and when to use it
•  High awareness in its target audience from a smaller evidence
base
Lesson: Let credible users do the teaching
Neither led with mechanism. Both gave people a simple rule and a reason to trust it.
University of Exeter (e.g. Bailey et al., 2009, J Appl Physiol). Nomio cited as a communication example only.

So, is the juice worth the squeeze?
For some polyphenols, yes. Now the industry needs to prove it, and explain it.
Reasonably settled
•  Cherry for recovery: >1000 mg/day,
at least 3 days before and after
•  Blackcurrant for performance: ~300
mg, 1–2 h before endurance or
repeated sprints
Still open
•  What reaches the blood, and what
does the work, does it matter?
•  Optimal dose for each source
•  Women, older athletes and
resistance training trials are needed.
Three asks
1 Standardise and declare
Which compound, and how much.
2 Fund the right trials
Trained athletes, women, blood levels, real
outcomes
3 Communicate dose and timing
Give people a rule they can follow

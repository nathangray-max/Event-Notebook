# A Bigger Sports Nutrition Landscape: Growth, Tensions and What Comes Next

## Summary
Nick Morgan of Nutrition Integrated opened the day with a state of the market. Sports nutrition is obviously going mainstream, he said, but is it growing beyond the motivations that defined it, is it winning consumers while losing its identity, and is the market growing or are its boundaries just moving?

He answered with three rules. Growth does not guarantee value: whey protein prices have never been higher, and brands are going through a structural reset, cutting promotions, shrinking packs, reformulating, launching whey hybrids and trying to sell more higher-margin lines such as creatine and electrolytes. Relevance does not guarantee ownership: creatine is now for everyone, in gummies, chocolate and products for women, and hydration has no agreed formula and new leaders. Opportunity does not guarantee advantage: his company tracks 16,412 product listings in eight categories, and only a few products will succeed, by pulling on format, brand, dose, science and branded ingredients.

His conclusion was that the principles of sports nutrition have gone mainstream, not sports nutrition itself. Brands should think beyond the category, stay true to the industry's DNA, choose where to compete and know their right to win. Everyone has a seat at the table, but not everyone will win.

## Key takeaways
- Starbucks' 15 g protein cold foam is his example of the bigger market: Starbucks say one in seven coffees now provides 15 g of protein. A bigger market is not more of the same market [00:00].
- Brands are not just mitigating whey prices but going through a "structural reset". New products that cut whey reliance and a shift into creatine, energy and fibre are the "top priority for brands" [00:00] (photo 6).
- More "whey hybrids" (sold as whey, blended with milk, soy or yeast to cut cost) launched in the last 12 months than ever; whey milkshakes and whey plus collagen are the next pillars [00:00] (photo 9).
- Plant protein still underdelivers on taste. It should sell itself as a very good protein, not as plant [00:00] (photo 10).
- Gummies are the fastest-growing creatine format by new launches: 8.0% of creatine products, 36.2% of them added in the last six months, with "lots of conjecture about stability" [00:00] (photo 14).
- A quarter of creatine products use a branded ingredient, most of it from one supplier, and he said there is a race to follow; pre-workout is highest at 53% [18:50] (photo 27).

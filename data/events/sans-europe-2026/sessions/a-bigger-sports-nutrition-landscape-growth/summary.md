# A Bigger Sports Nutrition Landscape: Growth, Tensions and What Comes Next

## Summary
Nick Morgan (Nutrition Integrated) opened the day by arguing that the principles of sports nutrition have gone further, rather than sports nutrition itself going mainstream, and put three questions to the room: is the category growing beyond the motivations that defined it, is it winning consumers while losing its identity, and is the market growing or are the boundaries just moving [00:00] (photo 31). His three rules were that growth does not guarantee value, relevance does not guarantee ownership, and opportunity does not guarantee advantage [00:00]. The evidence was Starbucks' 15 g protein cold foam (Starbucks say one in seven coffees now carries 15 g of protein), whey protein at historically high prices (WPI at EUR 30.5 a kilogram and WPC80 at EUR 27.6 by July 2026, about fourfold on 2023, on Vesper's index) and the brand responses to it (fewer promotions, 1 kg packs cut to 900 g at the same price, reformulation, "whey hybrids", whey milkshakes, whey plus collagen at 10 g plus 10 g), creatine moving into gummies, chocolate and products for women, and a hydration market with no agreed electrolyte dose [00:00] (photo 4) (photo 6) (photo 18). He showed the scale of competition from Nutrition Integrated's tracking of 16,412 product listings across the US and Europe: 3,448 creatine products, 2,833 magnesium and 2,648 collagen, with branded ingredients in 53% of pre-workouts, 40% of longevity products and 25% of creatine products [00:00], [18:50] (photo 23) (photo 27). He closed on four principles (think beyond the category, stay true to your DNA, choose where to compete, know your right to win) and on "permission to play" and the right to win: everyone has a seat at the table, but not everyone will win [18:50] (photo 32).

## Key takeaways
- Starbucks' protein cold foam (15 g) started in Q4 2025 in America and is now in the UK, Central Europe, Singapore and India; Starbucks say one in seven coffees currently provides 15 g of protein. "A bigger market does not simply mean more of the same market." [00:00]
- Raw whey prices: on Vesper's EU index, WPI rose from about EUR 11 a kilogram in January 2023 to EUR 30.5 by July 2026 and WPC80 from about EUR 7 to EUR 27.6, while WPC35 moved only from about EUR 3 to EUR 4.3; the slide calls it "the strongest period of price inflation on record", with WPC80 and WPI up about fourfold on 2023 and the sharpest acceleration in H1 2026 [00:00] (photo 4).
- Whey protein prices have never been higher and brands are going through a "structural reset", not mitigation: fewer promotions, pack architecture (1 kg to 900 g at the same price), some reformulation, a rush of new whey launches, and a push to sell higher-margin lines instead. Consumer prices per kilogram for priority brands in Europe rose between October 2025 and October 2026 by 13.6% for clear whey (EUR 60.14 to 68.32), 28.0% for HWP/WPI (47.73 to 61.10), 28.8% for whey blends (37.56 to 48.38), 15.0% for whey hybrids (35.64 to 40.97) and 20.8% for WPC (36.66 to 44.29); he said 15 to 25 percent from the stage. He believes protein will "look quite differently in 18 to 20 months" [00:00] (photo 8).
- His mitigation ladder has six rungs: price increases (widespread), reduced promotion (increasingly common) and pack architecture (widely adopted globally) are tactical; reformulation is strategic and brand specific; new products that cut whey reliance and a portfolio shift into creatine, energy and fibre are the "top priority for brands", with the limitations named as a ground-zero revenue base, brand permission and alienating core consumers [00:00] (photo 6).
- Brands "would love to listen to you about some really good solutions": they want to sell more creatine, electrolytes, pre-workout and higher-value vitamins because the margin structure is better than whey [00:00].
- "Whey hybrids" (presented as whey, blended with milk, soy or yeast to cut cost of goods) launched in greater numbers in the last 12 months than ever; whey milkshakes (whey plus milk) and whey plus collagen (typically 10 g plus 10 g, in shakes and clear drinks) are the next pillars; GLP-1 and longevity proteins, yeast, mycelium and air-derived protein are coming through but are still expensive [00:00]. The slide's examples were Bulk Daily Whey Protein ("best value protein"), Myprotein's Impact Whey Protein Milkshake (20 g protein, 2.6 g sugar, 1.2 g fat per 29 g serving), Clean Simple Eats Protein Powder + Collagen and Planta (20 g protein, 24 vitamins and minerals, 1,000 mg BCAAs) (photo 9).
- Six "pillars of innovation" from the price squeeze: whey choice risks confusing consumers; communications react more than they solve; hybrid powders are gaining consistency (dairy plus plant in overt blends); caseinate is the new milkshake; plant protein still needs a taste breakthrough; and the Myprotein x Nero launch signals a race for the next cafe collaboration [00:00] (photo 12).
- Plant protein is not taking the gap: ranges come in and out because they still underdeliver on taste. The winning message is "we're just a really bloody good protein", not "plant"; dairy plus plant hybrids are appearing for the first time and caseinates are gaining with milk volatility [00:00]. His example was Rocka's vegan range: Pro Protein at 24 g protein, 122 kcal, an 83% fava bean and pea blend, EUR 37.99 per 700 g (EUR 1.65 a serve); Natural Protein at 21 g, 112 kcal, 86.6% soy, fava, almond and pea, EUR 29.99 per 600 g (EUR 1.50 a serve); Protein Shake, "the tastiest vegan protein powder", 84% blend, EUR 37.99 per 900 g (EUR 1.27 a serve), plant moving "from an extended product proposition to a simple product attribute" (photo 10).
- The creatine benchmark is Optimum Nutrition's Micronised Creatine Powder: 3 g creatine monohydrate a serve, GBP 14.00 for the 187 g unflavoured tub (25p a serve, EUR 0.33) and GBP 16.00 for 247 g flavoured (29p a serve), fronted by Lauren James, Chelsea and England footballer (photo 13).
- Creatine is now "for all people and for all occasions"; gummies are the fastest-growing format by new launches in the last six months, off a small base, with "lots of conjecture about stability"; "other" formats (liquids, foods, chocolate) grew faster still off a tiny base, and most brands now have a route into broader retail [00:00], [25:59]. The format split of 2,639 creatine products: powder 70.1% (13.1% added in the last six months), capsule 17.0% (8.7%), gummy 8.0% (36.2%), tablet 4.2% (14.4%), effervescent 0.4% (10.0%), other 0.3% (44.4%); 378 products, 14.3% of the total, were added in six months. The examples were Creatonics' "Creatine+ formulated for women" sachet and Sunday Natural's Nutrachoc creatine chocolate (20 pieces at 1,000 mg creatine monohydrate, sweetened with tagatose) (photo 14) (photo 15).
- Hydration: no agreed electrolyte dose (SiS "all five essential", LMNT 1,000 mg sodium, Precision by sweat rate); Liquid IV is the category leader at £1.50 a stick; effervescents are three times cheaper than a stick pack; naturalness (no colours or preservatives, natural sweeteners and flavours, short ingredient lists) is a prerequisite to growth [00:00]. The shelf prices on the slide: Liquid I.V. GBP 1.50 a sachet (GBP 9.00 for 6; GBP 1.00 on Clubcard), Phizz GBP 0.40 a tablet (GBP 8.00 for 20; GBP 0.28 on Clubcard), Evian 750 ml GBP 1.85 single, Red Bull 250 ml GBP 2.10 single, Lucozade Sport 500 ml GBP 1.65 single (photo 16).
- Declared sodium a serve on selected electrolyte powders runs from 38 mg (ViDrate Boost) through 350 mg (SiS Hydro+), 500 mg (Cadence Daily), 570 mg (Liquid I.V. Multiplier) and 750 mg (Hunter & Gather) to 1,000 mg (LMNT); 69% of powders include four or five electrolytes, and 8% of products claim Himalayan sea salt as a natural sodium source. The slide's heading: "there is no optimal formula" [00:00] (photo 18).
- Hydration powders are positioned three ways: everyday hydration (Humantra, Hunter & Gather, Repowr: routine, natural, sugar-free), superior hydration (SOS "4x faster than water", Ancient + Brave, ARTAH "cellular hydration") and performance hydration (226ERS Hydra Zero on sweat losses, HIGH5 Isotonic carbs plus electrolytes, Precision PH strength options) (photo 22).
- Naturalness in ingredient declarations: 40% state natural colours, 46% use natural flavours and 43% include stevia; Humantra names grape skin as its colour source, Liquid I.V. declares sugar alongside steviol glycosides, ViDrate covers flavour, colour and sweetening in one proposition and Cadence extends the absence message to fillers [00:00] (photo 24).
- Product counts tracked (US and Europe, 16,412 listings across eight spaces): 3,448 creatine (new-product activity +10% over the latest six months), 2,833 magnesium (+15%), 2,648 collagen, 2,153 energy and focus, 1,869 pre-workout, 1,626 sleep and mood, 1,487 electrolyte and 348 longevity (+20% new); he said almost 3,500 creatine, almost 2,800 magnesium and over 2,500 collagen from the stage. "Only a few will be really successful", by pulling on format, brand, dose, science and branded solutions [00:00] (photo 23).
- Branded ingredient penetration (share of tracked products with at least one identified branded ingredient): pre-workout 53% (many use more than one), longevity 40%, energy and focus 31%, sleep and mood 25%, creatine 25%, collagen 25%, electrolyte 17%, magnesium 17%; in creatine most comes from one supplier and "there's a race for those to follow". His slide's nuance: minerals "need to work harder to prove the benefit" [18:50] (photo 27).
- Longevity products take three approaches: alter the ageing process (cellular health, mitochondria, senescence), optimise the ageing experience (muscle and mobility, cognition, energy, sleep) or personalise to a life stage (ageing athletes, menopause, later life); Cellaro's Vitalize AM, Renew PM and Reset monthly range against the 12 hallmarks of ageing was the example [18:50] (photo 29).
- Longevity brands and athlete-backed brands (Sergio Ramos linking sport to longevity through Centenarian: "Real longevity, backed by science. To perform better. To live better.") compete for the same consumer with sports nutrition products framed as "live better today"; the competitive set has never been broader [18:50] (photo 30).
- Brands must think beyond the category (gut, longevity, GLP-1) but stay true to the industry's DNA, choose where to compete and understand the right to win; this will be "the biggest challenge you face for your budgets and strategy in 2027 and beyond" [18:50].
- The four closing principles: think beyond the category (new consumers, needs and occasions); stay true to your DNA (capabilities, credibility, differentiation); choose where to compete ("where you could play from where you should play"); know your right to win (where you can create distinctive value). "Growth creates opportunity. Understanding change creates advantage." [18:50] (photo 32)

## For Vita Actives
Whey margin pressure is the strongest tailwind in the talk for an ingredient supplier: brands are actively looking for higher-margin, non-whey lines and for new protein blends, and he told the room they want to hear solutions [00:00]; his own slide lists a portfolio shift into creatine, energy and fibre as a "top priority for brands" (photo 6). For CreaWin: creatine is his benchmark category, 25% of 3,448 products use a branded ingredient, most of it from one supplier, and he said there is a race to follow [18:50] (photo 27); my reading is that this is exactly the gap a second branded creatine pitches into, with science and story as the differentiators he named. Gummies (8.0% of products, 36.2% added in six months) and food formats are where creatine launches are growing and stability is the open question [00:00] (photo 14); my reading is that a stability story for CreaWin in gummies would land with brands. The ON benchmark sets the consumer price floor at 25p a serve for 3 g (photo 13). For IsoClr: Pea: his point that plant protein must be sold as a very good protein rather than as plant, and that taste is the barrier, fits a clear pea isolate pitched on taste and clarity first (my reading); Rocka's range shows fava and pea blends sold at EUR 1.27 to 1.65 a serve (photo 10). The whey hybrid and whey plus collagen trend shows brands will blend protein sources, including in clear drinks, which opens room for a pea component in clear formats (my reading). For Oxystorm: pre-workout has the highest branded-ingredient penetration, 53%, so it is crowded but receptive to branded actives (photo 27) (my reading). Naturalness and short ingredient lists are a prerequisite across the board [00:00] (photo 24). Relevance high: it is the market-structure and protein-pricing frame for everything else at this event.

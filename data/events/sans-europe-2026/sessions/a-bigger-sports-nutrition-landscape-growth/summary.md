# A Bigger Sports Nutrition Landscape: Growth, Tensions and What Comes Next

## Summary
Nick Morgan (Nutrition Integrated) opened the day by arguing that the principles of sports nutrition have gone further, rather than sports nutrition itself going mainstream, and put three questions to the room: is the category growing beyond the motivations that defined it, is it winning consumers while losing its identity, and is the market growing or are the boundaries just moving [00:00]. His three rules were that growth does not guarantee value, relevance does not guarantee ownership, and opportunity does not guarantee advantage [00:00]. The evidence was Starbucks' 15 g protein cold foam (Starbucks say one in seven coffees now carries 15 g of protein), whey protein at historically high prices and the brand responses to it (fewer promotions, 1 kg packs cut to 900 g at the same price, reformulation, "whey hybrids", whey milkshakes, whey plus collagen at 10 g plus 10 g), creatine moving into gummies, chocolate and products for women, and a hydration market with no agreed electrolyte dose [00:00]. He showed the scale of competition from Nutrition Integrated's tracking: almost 3,500 creatine products, almost 2,800 standalone magnesium products and over 2,500 collagen products, with branded ingredients in over one in two pre-workouts, 40% of longevity products and one in four creatine products [00:00], [18:50]. He closed on "permission to play" and the right to win: everyone has a seat at the table, but not everyone will win [18:50].

## Key takeaways
- Starbucks' protein cold foam (15 g) started in Q4 2025 in America and is now in the UK, Central Europe, Singapore and India; Starbucks say one in seven coffees currently provides 15 g of protein. "A bigger market does not simply mean more of the same market." [00:00]
- Whey protein prices have never been higher and brands are going through a "structural reset", not mitigation: fewer promotions, pack architecture (1 kg to 900 g at the same price), some reformulation, a rush of new whey launches, and a push to sell higher-margin lines instead. Consumer prices for clear whey, WPI, blends and WPC are up by anywhere between 15 and 25 percent. He believes protein will "look quite differently in 18 to 20 months" [00:00].
- Brands "would love to listen to you about some really good solutions": they want to sell more creatine, electrolytes, pre-workout and higher-value vitamins because the margin structure is better than whey [00:00].
- "Whey hybrids" (presented as whey, blended with milk, soy or yeast to cut cost of goods) launched in greater numbers in the last 12 months than ever; whey milkshakes (whey plus milk) and whey plus collagen (typically 10 g plus 10 g, in shakes and clear drinks) are the next pillars; GLP-1 and longevity proteins, yeast, mycelium and air-derived protein are coming through but are still expensive [00:00].
- Plant protein is not taking the gap: ranges come in and out because they still underdeliver on taste. The winning message is "we're just a really bloody good protein", not "plant"; dairy plus plant hybrids are appearing for the first time and caseinates are gaining with milk volatility [00:00].
- Creatine is now "for all people and for all occasions"; gummies are the fastest-growing format by new launches in the last six months, off a small base, with "lots of conjecture about stability"; "other" formats (liquids, foods, chocolate) grew faster still off a tiny base, and most brands now have a route into broader retail [00:00], [25:59].
- Hydration: no agreed electrolyte dose (SiS "all five essential", LMNT 1,000 mg sodium, Precision by sweat rate); Liquid IV is the category leader at £1.50 a stick; effervescents are three times cheaper than a stick pack; naturalness (no colours or preservatives, natural sweeteners and flavours, short ingredient lists) is a prerequisite to growth [00:00].
- Product counts tracked: almost 3,500 creatine, almost 2,800 standalone magnesium, over 2,500 collagen; longevity small but growing [00:00].
- Branded ingredient penetration: over one in two pre-workouts (many use more than one), 40% of longevity products, one in four creatine products, where most comes from one supplier and "there's a race for those to follow" [18:50].
- Longevity brands and athlete-backed brands (Sergio Ramos linking sport to longevity) compete for the same consumer with sports nutrition products framed as "live better today"; the competitive set has never been broader [18:50].
- Brands must think beyond the category (gut, longevity, GLP-1) but stay true to the industry's DNA, choose where to compete and understand the right to win; this will be "the biggest challenge you face for your budgets and strategy in 2027 and beyond" [18:50].

## For Vita Actives
Whey margin pressure is the strongest tailwind in the talk for an ingredient supplier: brands are actively looking for higher-margin, non-whey lines and for new protein blends, and he told the room they want to hear solutions [00:00]. For CreaWin: creatine is his benchmark category, one in four products uses a branded ingredient, most of it from one supplier, and he said there is a race to follow [18:50]; my reading is that this is exactly the gap a second branded creatine pitches into, with science and story as the differentiators he named. Gummies and food formats are where creatine launches are growing and stability is the open question [00:00]; my reading is that a stability story for CreaWin in gummies would land with brands. For IsoClr: Pea: his point that plant protein must be sold as a very good protein rather than as plant, and that taste is the barrier, fits a clear pea isolate pitched on taste and clarity first (my reading). The whey hybrid and whey plus collagen trend shows brands will blend protein sources, including in clear drinks, which opens room for a pea component in clear formats (my reading). For Oxystorm: pre-workout has the highest branded-ingredient penetration, over one in two, so it is crowded but receptive to branded actives (my reading). Naturalness and short ingredient lists are a prerequisite across the board [00:00]. Relevance high: it is the market-structure and protein-pricing frame for everything else at this event.

# Whey & Collagen: The Protein Power Couple

## Summary
Tess Plaquet (technical sport manager, Rousselot) argued that whey prices are here to stay and that the elegant answer is "smarter formulations": replacing part of the whey with collagen peptides rather than raising prices or shrinking packs [00:10]. Her market evidence was whey product launches growing seven and a half percent a year over the last two years, the biggest price growth in 24 to 25 at 8.7, and a tonne of WPC80 going from 5.7k in 2023 to almost 32k in 2026, almost sixfold, with drivers (GLP-1 users, China, active lifestyle consumers, whey being a cheese byproduct, energy, labour, tariffs and climate) that she said will keep prices high [00:10]. Launches combining whey and collagen are growing at almost 17.5 percent a year, often at 10 g plus 10 g, and collagen's supply is spread across bovine, porcine, chicken and fish with far less price volatility [00:10]. Her science was Rousselot's Peptan studies: 22 men on 45 g whey or 25 g whey plus 20 g Peptan for three weeks showed similar recovery and performance; 20 g Peptan with vitamin C for seven days gave 28% less muscle soreness and 13% faster recovery than placebo; 10 g before a 70-minute run held blood endotoxins at the no-exercise level; 8 g a day improved knee discomfort and function in women at three and six months; 15 g a day cut night awakenings from around 30 to 22 and the Peptan group scored 100% on a Stroop test [00:10]. The recording ends mid-sentence as she invites the room to the roundtables.

## Key takeaways
- Whey launches (supplements and beverages) have grown steadily for two years at seven and a half percent a year; the biggest price rise was in 24 to 25 at a growth rate of 8.7 [00:10].
- A tonne of 80% whey protein concentrate was 5.7k in 2023 and is almost 32k in 2026, almost sixfold in three years [00:10].
- She said the drivers mean "those prices will be here to stay": demand from active lifestyle consumers, GLP-1 users advised to take extra protein, and China; whey is a byproduct of cheese and cannot be scaled easily; gas, electricity, labour, filtration costs, tariffs, geopolitics and climate all push the same way [00:10].
- Three brand responses: raise the price, shrinkflation, or smarter formulation with protein blends, which she called the most elegant and an opportunity to stand out on shelf [00:10].
- Launches combining whey and collagen are growing at almost 17.5 percent a year; a 10 g collagen plus 10 g whey product was the example [00:10].
- Collagen's supply is spread across bovine, porcine, chicken and fish and its historical prices show far less fluctuation than whey [00:10].
- Whey is a complete protein rich in BCAAs for muscle building and repair; Peptan is hydrolysed collagen rich in hydroxyproline, glycine and proline, pitched at ligaments, tendons, joints and bone; "these two are no competitors" [00:10].
- 22 men, 45 g whey against 25 g whey plus 20 g Peptan, three weeks of training, jump height and creatine kinase: recovery and performance were similar in both groups [00:10].
- 24 men, 20 g Peptan with vitamin C or placebo for seven days, then 150 jumps: 28% less muscle soreness and 13% faster muscle recovery than placebo [00:10].
- 10 g Peptan for seven days then 45 minutes before a 70-minute run: endotoxins were the same as without exercise, while placebo rose; she said this "could lessen" endotoxins and "could reduce" digestive discomfort [00:10].
- Women with mild knee discomfort, 8 g a day for six months: significant improvement in discomfort and function at three months, further at six [00:10].
- Sleep: 15 g a day, polysomnography on night seven: awakenings fell from around 30 to 22, subjective sleep improved, and the Peptan group scored 100% on a Stroop test against a weaker placebo group [00:10].
- Rousselot offers roundtables on application advantages and help incorporating collagen [00:10].

## For Vita Actives
Rousselot is selling the same brand problem we sell into (whey cost and supply) with a different answer: partial replacement of whey with collagen, backed by a head-to-head showing no loss of recovery or performance at 25 g whey plus 20 g Peptan [00:10]. My reading is that IsoClr: Pea competes for the same "smarter formulation" slot in a brand's whey review, and the pitch should match hers in form: a cost and supply argument first, then evidence that a blend performs. We do not have a head-to-head of this kind for pea; that is the gap (my reading). Her collagen supply story (four animal sources, little volatility) is the kind of supply-security argument a plant protein can make more strongly (my reading). Whey plus collagen clear drinks were named by Nick Morgan earlier; a clear pea plus collagen format is a possible combination rather than a competition (my reading). The health claims here are Rousselot's own studies, with her hedges; none are ours. Relevance high: protein blends and whey pricing.

# Whey & Collagen: The Protein Power Couple

## Summary
Tess Plaquet, technical sport manager at Rousselot, argued that brands should stop asking when whey prices will fall and work around them by blending whey with collagen.

She began with the market. Whey launches keep growing, and she said the forces behind whey prices, from GLP-1 users and China to whey being a cheese by-product and rising energy and labour costs, mean high prices are here to stay. Brands can raise prices, shrink packs or reformulate; she called smarter formulation with protein blends the most elegant answer. Launches combining whey and collagen are growing at 17.44% a year, and collagen comes from four animal sources with far steadier prices.

She then made the case that the two proteins complement rather than compete: whey builds and repairs muscle, while Rousselot's Peptan collagen peptides support tendons, joints and bone. Her evidence was Rousselot's own studies. The central one gave 22 men either 45 g of whey or 25 g of whey plus 20 g of Peptan for three weeks of training, and recovery and performance were similar in both groups. Studies of Peptan on its own reported less muscle soreness, fewer blood endotoxins after a run, better knee comfort and better sleep.

The recording ends as she invites the room to Rousselot's roundtables.

## Key takeaways
- Brands have three responses to whey costs: raise the price, shrinkflation, or smarter formulation with protein blends, which she called the most elegant and a way to stand out on shelf [00:10] (slide 5).
- Launches combining whey and collagen grew at 17.44% a year from 2023 to 2026 to date, led by snacks, cereals and energy bars (39.7%) and nutritional and meal replacement drinks (36.4%); her example, Optimum Nutrition's clear whey isolate with collagen, has 10 g of each [00:10] (slide 6).
- Collagen supply is spread across bovine, porcine, chicken and fish, and its historical prices fluctuate far less than whey's [00:10].
- Whey and collagen are "no competitors": whey for muscle, Peptan (rich in hydroxyproline, glycine and proline) for ligaments, tendons, joints and bone [00:10] (slide 8).
- In 22 men over three weeks of training, 25 g whey plus 20 g Peptan a day gave the same recovery and performance as 45 g whey; the slide says such blends "may provide broader support across the musculoskeletal system" [00:10] (slide 9).
- 2 × 10 g Peptan a day with vitamin C, for a week before 150 drop jumps and two days after, gave 28% less muscle soreness and 13% faster muscle recovery than placebo; the slide marks the soreness result as a likely benefit and the recovery result as significant [00:10] (slide 10).

## Detail
- Her deck arrived after the talk (files/rousselot-whey-collagen-deck.pdf, 23 pages). Slides 17 to 23 come after the closing "Thank you" slide and are not in the recording (see the last bullet).
- Whey launches: food and beverage supplement launches featuring whey grew 7.53% a year from 2023 to 2025 (she said seven and a half percent), from about 18,600 in 2023 to about 19,800 in 2024 and 21,600 in 2025, read from the chart [00:10] (slide 3).
- The "8.7" she gave as the biggest growth yet is, on the slide, the 2024 to 2025 rise of +8.73% in the average price of those launches: US$3.17 per 100 g in 2023, US$3.32 in 2024, US$3.61 in 2025 and US$3.78 in 2026 to date [00:10] (slide 3).
- WPC80 price: she said "in 2023 the price of a ton of whey protein concentrate of 80% was 5.7 k, whereas in 2026 it's almost 32 k. So really, in just three years' time, it's almost a sixfold" [00:10]. The slide gives US$5.7k a tonne in June 2023 and US$31.7k in June 2026; the slide's sources are Mintel and Vesper, and it does not say which market the price is for (slide 3).
- Another speaker's view of the same price: Alan Johnstone (Nutrilution, unfiltered-an-insider-s-view-of) used Vesper's EU price in euros a kilogram: WPC80 about €5.2 in June 2023 and €27.4 in June 2026, read from his chart, and €27.00 in the week of 25 August 2026. Hers is in US dollars a tonne (US$5.70 and US$31.70 a kilogram). The two differ in currency and unit; both show close to a sixfold rise.
- Her drivers, which she said mean "those prices will be here to stay": demand from active lifestyle consumers, GLP-1 users advised to take extra protein, and China; whey is a by-product of cheese and cannot be scaled easily; gas, electricity, labour, filtration costs, tariffs, geopolitics and climate push the same way [00:10]. The slide groups them as supply and demand imbalance, rising production and operational costs, and global economic and environmental factors, citing The Guardian, Rabobank, ScienceInsights and New Hope Network (slide 4).
- Whey and collagen launches by year, read from the chart: about 320 in 2023, 320 in 2024, 420 in 2025 and 520 in 2026 to date. The chart's sources are Mintel, Holland & Barrett, ON, David and Rousselot (slide 6).
- Whey is a complete protein rich in BCAAs, for muscle building and repair. Peptan is hydrolysed collagen, rich in hydroxyproline (unique to animal-derived collagen and, she said, linked to holistic health benefits), glycine and proline [00:10]. The slide adds that whey lacks hydroxyproline and Peptan lacks tryptophan (slide 8).
- The head-to-head (Robberechts et al., 2023, IJSNEM): randomised, double-blind, parallel; 22 healthy, active men; 45 g whey against 25 g whey plus 20 g Peptan a day; three weeks of training; jump height and creatine kinase measured; no difference between groups at the end [00:10] (slide 9).
- Soreness (Clifford et al., 2019): 24 men, 2 × 10 g Peptan or maltodextrin placebo with vitamin C from day -7 to day 2, with doses 40 minutes before and straight after 150 drop jumps on day 0; soreness and muscle power at 48 hours. She described it as seven days of 20 g [00:10] (slide 10).
- Endotoxins (Taylor et al., 2022): randomised, double-blind, cross-over; 20 men; 10 g Peptan or flavoured water for seven days, the last dose 45 minutes before a 70-minute run; endotoxins were the same as without exercise, while on placebo they rose. Her hedge: "...it's really an indication that taking in peptan could lessen the amount of endotoxins in your blood and could reduce your digestive discomfort" [00:10]. The slide: "Peptan could lessen the increase of blood endotoxin levels, a known cause of inflammation" (slide 11).
- Knees (Jiang et al., 2014): randomised, placebo-controlled; women with mild knee discomfort, 8 g a day for six months; joint discomfort (WOMAC) fell 10% at three months and 24% at six, and joint function (Lysholm) rose 4% and 7%, the chart marks each change p < 0.001 [00:10] (slide 12).
- Sleep (Thomas et al., 2023): randomised, double-blind, cross-over; 13 men; 15 g an hour before bed for seven days; polysomnography on night seven. The slide gives a 30% reduction in awakenings; on the chart they fell from about 30 to about 21, read from the chart, where she said "around 30 to 22, something like that". On the Stroop test the Peptan group scored about 1.00 against about 0.97 for control, read from the chart; she said the Peptan group "performed perfectly, 100%" [00:10] (slide 13).
- Rousselot offers roundtables on application advantages and help incorporating collagen [00:10] (slide 15).
- Slides after the close: Nextida GC, which Rousselot says was "specifically developed to target post-meal glucose spikes & boost GLP-1 secretion". Two double-blind, placebo-controlled trials (pilot 2024: 16 people, 5 g or 10 g; confirmation 2026: 30 people, 10 g; each taken 30 minutes before a carbohydrate-rich meal). Citing the 2026 study, for 10 g against placebo the slides give a 37% lower glucose spike, a 47% lower glucose peak, 82% more total GLP-1 exposure, a 53% higher GLP-1 response, a 70% higher GIP response and a lower post-meal insulin response. A 2026 Ipsos consumer study for Rousselot in the US (5 g twice a day for six weeks, N = 181) found 80% felt fuller for longer and 55% reported weight loss; the slide says this "does not constitute a consumer-facing claim" (slide 17) (slide 19) (slide 20) (slide 21) (slide 22) (slide 23).

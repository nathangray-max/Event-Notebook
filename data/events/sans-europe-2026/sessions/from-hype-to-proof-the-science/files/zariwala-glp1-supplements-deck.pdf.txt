Anorectic peptide hormones – GLP-1
GLP-1 – incretin hormone released from
small intestinal and colonic L-cells
following eating
GLP-1 receptors (GLP-1Rs) located in
the brain and peripheral organs (e.g.
pancreas, GI tract, liver)
Binds to the GLP-1Rs enhancing insulin
secretion, inhibiting glucagon release, delaying
gastric emptying, and reducing food intake
Rapidly degraded by the enzyme DPP-4
to its inactive form

--- page 24 ---
24
Contenders - lipid-based compounds
Oleoylethanolamide (OEA) – endogenous
bioactive lipid - small amounts in diet
Agonist of the nuclear receptor PPAR-α involved
in lipid balance
Binds to GPR-119 on intestinal L-cells potentiating
the release of GLP-1
Formulated OEA (TRPTI
 ) shown to enhance
meal-stimulated total GLP-1 and GIP in a dose-
dependent manner
https://doi.org/10.3389/fendo.2026.1879799

--- page 25 ---
25
Contenders - botanicals
o Berberine – does not directly bind to or
activate GLP-1 receptors
Effects mainly mediated by AMPK -
improvements in lipid and glucose
metabolism
Low oral bioavailability and short plasma half
life = high therapeutic dose (e.g. 500mg x 3
split dose)
https://doi.org/10.3389/fendo.2026.1879799
o Cinnamon – mild effect acting as an activator
of the gut TRPA1 receptors triggering
downstream secretion of GLP-1

Contenders – bitter compounds
Certain natural bitter compounds (quercetin,
berberine) may increase GLP-1 secretion
Mechanism - activation of bitter taste receptors
(TAS2Rs) in the gut
Mild to modest effects
https://doi.org/10.3390/ijms27219816

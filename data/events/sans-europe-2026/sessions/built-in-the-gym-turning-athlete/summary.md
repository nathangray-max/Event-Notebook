# Built in the gym: Turning athlete insight into sports nutrition innovation

## Summary
Olivia Haslam (NutraIngredients) talked with Marchon's founder Ollie Marchon and head of product Liam Holmes about what the rise of the "everyday athlete" means for sports nutrition [00:00]. Ollie split these consumers into three groups (inspired by, driven by, live by) and said most of their 8,000 daily app users train at 6:00 in the morning, which changes what they need [00:54]. Liam argued for "situational" formats, such as carbohydrate in a pre-workout for people who cannot eat at 6 a.m. [04:00]. Ollie said awareness of ingredients and supplements is high but understanding lags, so education, honest positioning and efficacious doses matter [05:29] [18:39]. They see creatine as here to stay, moving into cognition, with format and stability as the challenge for ingredient suppliers [08:24]. Their five-year bet is formats that improve compliance [22:11].

## Key takeaways
- The everyday athlete: the running boom, Hyrox ("high rocks" in Otter) and strength training have blurred the line between elite athletes and everyone else [00:54] [03:05].
- Three segments: "inspired by" (habits, feeling better, multivitamin, omega, sleep, food first), driven by ("The driven buy" in Otter; measured progress, nutrient timing before, during and after training) and "live by" (optimisers who plan the whole 24 hours around training) [00:54].
- Timing: most of their 8,000 daily training-app users train at 6:00 in the morning, as do most gym members; they are in a different fed state from people who trained after work before COVID [00:54].
- Situational formats: Marchon put carbohydrate into a pre-workout ("workout fuel") because people training at 5 or 6 a.m. do not want to eat toast or porridge first [04:00].
- Awareness is high, understanding lags: gym-goers talk confidently about products they saw in an ad or from influencers without knowing why, so "education is really critical at this stage" [05:29].
- Creatine needed little brand communication: they rode the wave of big podcast voices on longevity, healthspan and healthy ageing, and of its "decades of of literature"; it builds the daily supplement ritual and mixes into squash, water or porridge [06:53].
- Liam: creatine is "here to stay", the research is moving into cognition and possibly recovery, and for ingredient suppliers the challenge is format: stability in water, in gummies and in other formats [08:24].
- Opportunity areas: gut health, immunity, sleep and recovery, as people train harder, longer and across modalities; gut health to absorb higher carbohydrate loads is "still something that's untapped" [09:36].
- Discipline over hype: Ollie cited a stat of "2005" electrolyte brands launched last year and said marketing electrolytes as fuel in place of carbohydrate is "a bit of a miss"; low-sodium electrolytes to drink more water through the day are fine [11:15] [13:09].
- Liam prefers established ingredients reaching new uses as research catches up (creatine for cognition, curcumin, certain probiotics) over "the new shiny ingredient" [14:05].
- Claims: communicating the benefit is "a regulatory nightmare"; Marchon attaches ingredients to formats already in a routine, such as a creatine plus multivitamin or a "Brain Health Plus multivitamin" [15:23].
- When Marchon surveyed its gym members, the number one struggle was still hitting their daily protein requirement; many marathon runners weeks from the race had not tried a gel [16:50].
- Dose matters: a magnesium product for sleep without the right amount gives no benefit, and disappointed first-time buyers move on to the next trend [18:39].
- Compliance: Amazon now sets more hoops than TikTok Shop; YouTube channels, regulators and third-party lab testing are catching up with poor practice; Science in Sport's chief science officer and advisory boards are the model [20:29] [21:13].
- Five years out: "formats that improve compliance" (gummies, jellies, powders, RTDs) and convenience [22:11].
- Ingredient supply, prices or sourcing were not discussed; the only supplier point was the creatine format challenge [08:24] (my reading).

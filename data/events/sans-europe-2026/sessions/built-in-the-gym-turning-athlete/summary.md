# Built in the gym: Turning athlete insight into sports nutrition innovation

## Summary
Olivia Haslam of NutraIngredients talked with Ollie Marchon, founder of the UK brand Marchon, and Liam Holmes, its head of product, about what the rise of the everyday athlete means for sports nutrition.

Ollie described three kinds of consumer: those inspired by training, who want habits and to feel better; those driven by it, who measure progress and time their nutrients; and those who live by it, planning the whole day around training. Most people on Marchon's training app train at 6 in the morning, which changes what they need. Liam argued for situational formats, such as carbohydrate in a pre-workout for people who cannot eat first.

Ollie said awareness of supplements is high but understanding lags, so education, honest positioning and effective doses matter more than hype. Creatine shows how this works: decades of research and podcast voices did the selling, and it built the daily supplement habit. Liam sees creatine as here to stay and moving into cognition; for ingredient suppliers, the challenge is stability in water, gummies and other formats. Claims are a regulatory nightmare, so Marchon adds ingredients to products already in people's routines.

Asked where the category goes in five years, Liam answered: formats that improve compliance.

## Key takeaways
- Three segments of everyday athlete: "inspired by" (habits, feeling better, food first), "driven by" (measured progress, nutrient timing around training) and "live by" (optimisers who plan the whole 24 hours around training) [00:54].
- Most of their 8,000 daily training-app users train at 6:00 in the morning, so Marchon put carbohydrate into a pre-workout for people who will not eat toast or porridge first [00:54], [04:00].
- Awareness is high but understanding lags: gym-goers talk confidently about products they saw in an ad without knowing why, so "education is really critical at this stage" [05:29].
- Liam: creatine is "here to stay" and the research is moving into cognition; for ingredient suppliers the challenge is format, meaning stability in water, gummies and other formats [08:24].
- When Marchon surveyed its gym members, the number one struggle was still hitting their daily protein requirement [16:50].
- Five years out, Liam's bet is "formats that improve compliance": gummies, jellies, powders, RTDs, and convenience [22:11].

## Detail
- The everyday athlete: the running boom, Hyrox ("high rocks" in Otter) and strength training have blurred the line between elite athletes and everyone else: "We've seen the rise of the every athlete." [00:54], [03:05]
- The segments in more detail: "inspired by" wants multivitamin, omega and sleep support; "driven by" ("The driven buy" in Otter) times nutrients before, during and after training [00:54].
- Early trainers, like most gym members, are in a different fed state from people who trained after work before COVID. Marchon calls its carbohydrate pre-workout "workout fuel" [00:54], [04:00].
- Creatine needed little brand communication: Marchon rode the wave of big podcast voices on longevity, healthspan and healthy ageing, and of creatine's "decades of of literature". It builds the daily supplement ritual and mixes into squash, water or porridge [06:53].
- Liam on creatine: studied on strength, performance and muscle mass, now going into cognition and possibly recovery [08:24].
- Opportunity areas: gut health, immunity, sleep and recovery, as people train harder, longer and across modalities. Gut health to absorb higher carbohydrate loads is "still something that's untapped" [09:36].
- Discipline over hype: Ollie cited "2005" electrolyte brands launched last year (Otter's figure) and said marketing electrolytes as fuel in place of carbohydrate is "a bit of a miss"; low-sodium electrolytes to drink more water through the day are fine [11:15], [13:09].
- Liam prefers established ingredients reaching new uses as research catches up (creatine for cognition, curcumin, certain probiotics) over "the new shiny ingredient" [14:05].
- Claims: Marchon attaches ingredients to formats already in a routine, such as a creatine plus multivitamin or a "Brain Health Plus multivitamin" [15:23].
- Many marathon runners a few weeks from their race had not tried a gel [16:50].
- Dose matters: a magnesium product for sleep without the right amount gives no benefit, and disappointed first-time buyers move on to the next trend [18:39].
- Compliance: Amazon now sets more hoops than TikTok Shop; YouTube channels, regulators and third-party lab testing are catching up with poor practice; Science in Sport's chief science officer and advisory boards are the model [20:29], [21:13].
- Ingredient supply, prices and sourcing were not discussed; the only supplier point was the creatine format challenge [08:24] (my reading).

# Beyond the GLP-1 Boom: Nutrition, Innovation and the Future of Weight Management

## Summary
The day's last panel brought together Nick Morgan (Nutrition Integrated), Brett Hamer (product director, Myprotein) and performance nutritionist Ed Tooley [25:19]. Brett said GLP-1 is "turbocharging" existing trends, with supermarkets now wanting sports nutrition across the store, and that clear protein, and clear protein plus collagen, are selling very well to this consumer [28:46] [35:09] [43:51]. On cost, he said whey doubled in price, pushing brands into milk proteins, whose prices are now rising too, while plant protein has not taken off; Nick put that down to taste [35:09] [37:08]. Nick called creatine "almost like a tier one ingredient" and named low-volume clear protein with fibre, and support for people coming off the drugs, as the gaps [38:42] [45:16]. Ed warned that athletes or consumers who simply eat less risk missing protein, carbohydrate and micronutrients [32:49] [34:13]. The recording ends with the host Nikki's close of the day and next year's dates [57:25].

## Key takeaways
- Nick: GLP-1 users' extra supplement buying (protein, fibre, probiotics, multivitamins) is behaviour change rather than a targeted muscle-loss purchase; protein supply challenges and prices are "indirectly" related to GLP-1, "amongst other things" [26:54].
- Brett: these are "accelerations of existing macro trends"; retailers now want sports nutrition across the supermarket, not only on the health fixture, and licences such as the Müller collaboration ("Mueller" in Otter) have met an "insatiable" appetite [28:46].
- Myprotein builds need-state bundles rather than an overtly labelled GLP-1 range, to avoid alienating other customers; bestsellers are core protein and hydration, and fibre is the interesting one [29:57].
- Nick: few products put GLP-1 overtly on pack; it is "right there in front of us, but it's not there at all" [30:32].
- Ed: GLP-1 use is not open in elite sport yet, athletes know it is on the monitoring list, and personal trainers' clients use it [31:28]; his fear is people eating less and missing key nutrients, with a risk of RED-S ("reds" in Otter) and deficiencies [32:49].
- For low appetite, Ed puts protein first for muscle, then carbohydrate for fuelling and micronutrients to fill gaps [34:13].
- Brett on price: whey at six euros a kilogram goes into everything; at 26 euros a kilogram "your value economics completely collapsed"; customers are more open to milk proteins, which are now "skyrocketing" too; whey stays resilient; clear protein is "performing extremely well" in retail; plant has not exploded; collagen "is really having a moment", especially with the clear customer [35:09].
- Nick: plant protein's barrier is taste; whey is one of the best-tasting proteins, plant has got better but is "never quite as good as we truly expect for the majority of people" [37:08].
- Creatine: Nick said it has become "almost like a tier one ingredient", adopted by women, ageing consumers and weight-management users, who may not know why but get "bang for buck" from several benefits [38:42]; Brett added that retailers now give sports nutrition far more space [39:54].
- Myprotein's AI assistant Fuel Coach: GLP-1 users mostly ask whether a product is safe to take while on the drug, not for GLP-1 alternatives [40:30].
- HMB: asked about rising HMB use for GLP-1 support in Brazil, Nick said there are many HMB launches, HMB pairs well with creatine, and non-protein "muscle blends" are growing from a relatively small base [40:51] [41:07].
- Collagen: aesthetics and "Ozempic face" align collagen with GLP-1 use [41:50]; Ed uses collagen with ageing athletes as well as for injury [43:11]; Brett called clear protein plus collagen "just a sweet spot for this this consumer", with retail launches coming and the US responding [43:51].
- Unmet needs: Brett would add a protein plus fibre product and a more complex fibre blend [44:38]; Nick named low-volume clear protein with fibre (citing Genius protein in America) and products for people cycling on and off [45:16]; Ed said support and education for people buying privately [46:20].
- Nick expects routines or "systems" of products (AM/PM, 1-2-3) with tech and education wrapped round them to be the biggest innovation in 12 to 18 months [47:33].
- Consumers understand less of metabolic-health claims than the industry thinks; anchor them in energy and vitality [49:16] [50:08].
- Marketing: Myprotein is "there to be shot at"; retailer bays like Boots' "GLP-1 friendly" let it be present without GLP-1 on pack [51:57]. Nick: "just win with protein, win with fiber", and hydration [53:16].
- Next disruptor: people coming off the drugs and what training they do (Brett) [55:02]; education between on and off (Ed) [56:00]; AI and faster answers (Nick) [56:07].
- The host's close of the day (Nikki, [57:25]): a recap of SANS Europe, including that "Polyphenols may be a big white space opportunity", and next year's event: a two-day conference followed by the Nutra Healthspan Summit, at the Novotel London West from 7 to 10 December [57:25].

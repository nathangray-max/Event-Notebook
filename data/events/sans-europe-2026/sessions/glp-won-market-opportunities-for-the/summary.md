# GLP-Won - Market Opportunities for the Active Nutrition Sector

## Summary
Morgan Griffiths of Lumina Intelligence argued that GLP-1 drugs, bad news for many food and drink brands, are an opportunity for active nutrition.

He started with scale. In the UK, 6.1% of people are taking a GLP-1 now, and with past users and those considering it the total is about 18.4 million people. Use is similar in Italy, France, Germany and Spain.

He then described the users. They are not the obesity population the drugs were designed for: most are women, Gen X or Millennial and better off. They are already health-minded and willing to pay more for quality. Most expect to spend the same or more on food and drink, and near 60% expect to take more supplements, with creatine higher on the list than he expected.

He saw three openings: side effects, nutrition gaps such as protein intake and muscle loss, and lapsed users, who risk regaining the weight because the drug changed their behaviour without them changing their habits. Huel, Cambridge Nutrition, Portion IQ and Boots are already moving, and Lumina forecasts UK use at around 11% by 2031.

## Key takeaways
- In the UK, 6.1% currently take GLP-1s, 5.7% have taken them and 15.1% are considering them: together about 18.4 million people [00:08].
- Current users are not the obesity population the drugs were designed for: 56% female, 63% Gen X or Millennial, 63% ABC1, and 74% happy to pay more for quality [00:08].
- Two thirds expect to spend the same or more on food and drink, and near 60% are likely to increase their supplement use; creatine was "maybe slightly higher" than expected [00:08].
- The openings are side effects, nutrition gaps (muscle loss, difficulty eating enough protein and nutrients) and lapsed users, with a malnutrition risk for people who eat less without care [00:08].
- He cited a British Medical Association study: people are four times more likely to regain weight lost with GLP-1 than weight lost by traditional methods [00:08].
- Putting GLP-1 on pack is "a kind of a health claim", so retailer support such as Boots' GLP-1 bays matters [00:08].

## Detail
- "So, 6.1% of the UK population are currently taking GLP-1s." The considering figure (15.1%) jumped "by about two thirds, a third to a half" when the pill was introduced [00:08].
- The 18.4 million compared: everyone in the UK born since 2000, the UK's Netflix subscribers, or the 12th largest country in Europe [00:08].
- A summer study found similar current use in Italy, France, Germany and Spain; the US is further ahead [00:08].
- Current users (6.1%) outnumber vegans and vegetarians combined (5.7%), yet food and drink teams invest more in vegan innovation [00:08].
- Health-minded: over 55% already make healthy lifestyle choices and 62% look for food with added benefits [00:08].
- Spend: the most common answer was no change, the net was an increase: "Two thirds of consumers are likely to spend the same or more when on GLP-1 than not." 90 to 95% in the UK buy the drugs privately, at "one to 300 pounds a month" [00:08].
- Supplements: near 90% will use the same or more, with no change included; the list held no surprises apart from creatine, and he saw "a little bit of white space" for active nutrition [00:08].
- Lapsed users: 30% worry about putting weight back on; around 80% think they will keep it off (95% including neutral). Reduced enjoyment of food is another concern [00:08].
- Why weight returns: GLP-1 changes behaviour without the user changing behaviour; when "you turn the tap off", the weight comes back unless habits changed [00:08].
- Market moves: Huel's lighter version, Cambridge Nutrition and Portion IQ ranges, and Boots' GLP-1 bays and shelf clips [00:08].

# GLP-Won - Market Opportunities for the Active Nutrition Sector

## Summary
Morgan Griffiths, Commercial Lead at Lumina Intelligence, argued that GLP-1 drugs, bad news for many food and drink brands, are an opportunity for active nutrition.

He started with scale. In the UK, almost 27% of people, about 18.4 million, are taking, have taken or are considering a GLP-1. A summer survey found similar use in Italy, France, Germany and Spain.

He then described the users. They are not the obesity population the drugs were designed for: most are women, Gen X or Millennial and better off. They are already health-minded and happy to pay more for quality. Most European users spend the same or more on food and drink, and most have increased their supplement use. Proteins lead their supplement list; creatine ranked higher than he expected.

He saw three openings: side effects, access to nutrition (protein, nutrients, muscle loss) and lapsed users. Users are confident of keeping the weight off, but a BMJ study he showed found weight returns almost four times faster after the drugs than after diet and exercise. Huel, Cambridge Nutrition's PortionIQ and Boots are already moving, and Lumina forecasts that UK use will keep growing to 2031.

## Key takeaways
- In the UK, 6.1% currently use GLP-1s, 5.7% have used them and 15.1% are considering them: almost 27% of the population, c.18.4 million people (slide 8) [00:08].
- Current UK users are not the obesity population the drugs were designed for: 56% female, 63% Gen X or Millennial, 63% ABC1 (slide 10), and 74% happy to pay more for quality against a 70% average (slide 11).
- Among European current users (n=352), 57% increased their supplement use, 88% including no change. Proteins lead at 51%; creatine is at 27%, above HMB at 15% (slide 13). He had said "near 60%" and "near 90%", and creatine was "maybe slightly higher" than expected [00:08].
- The most common challenges on the drugs: fear of weight regain 30%, side effects 29%, cost 29%, reduced food enjoyment 27%, maintaining nutrition 22%, muscle loss concerns 22% and difficulty consuming enough protein 19% (slide 14).
- 78% of users are confident of keeping the weight off (94% including neutral), yet the BMJ study on his slide found regain almost four times faster after stopping the drugs than after diet and physical activity changes (slide 15, read from the slide image).
- Putting GLP-1 on pack is "a kind of a health claim", so retailer support such as Boots' GLP-1 bays matters [00:08] (slide 17).

- The US chart (Gallup National Health and Well-Being Index, published 28 October 2025) runs from 26.5 in 2009 to a peak of 39.9 in 2022 and 37.0 in 2025, with 2021 circled beside a Wegovy pen (slide 6) (slide 7, read from the chart). He called it US obesity prevalence and said semaglutide had "a pretty much kind of immediate impact" [00:08]. The slides do not label the measure.
- Sources: the UK figures come from Lumina's GLP-1 Navigator and GLP-1 Out of Home Tracking, 24 weeks ending 5 July 2026 (slide 8). The European figures come from Lumina's Global Longevity Study, Europe GLP-1 current users, n=352 (slide 12). The deck does not name the countries; he said the summer study covered Italy, France, Germany and Spain, with the US further ahead [00:08].
- The 18.4 million equals everyone born since 2000, the UK's Netflix subscribers, or the 12th largest country in Europe, larger than the Netherlands, Belgium, Sweden, Greece, Portugal or Czechia (slide 8). The considering figure jumped "by about two thirds, a third to a half" when the pill was introduced [00:08].
- Current users (6.1%) outnumber vegans and vegetarians combined (5.7%), yet food and drink teams invest more in vegan innovation (slide 9) [00:08].
- Health-minded: 55% make healthy lifestyle choices (51% average) and 62% look for food with added benefits (52% average) (slide 11). He said "over 55%" [00:08].
- Spend on food and drink since starting treatment: decreased significantly 9.1%, decreased somewhat 23.0%, no change 28.1%, increased somewhat 26.1%, increased significantly 13.6%; net increase 39.6%, net decrease 31.1%. 52% said spending on premium and higher quality food had increased (slide 12). He put the net increase at "around 30 40%" and same-or-more at two thirds [00:08]; the bars sum to 67.8% (my sum).

- The side-effects slide he pointed the room to: nausea 27%, constipation 25%, mood changes 22%, fatigue or low energy 22%, diarrhoea 18%, headaches 18%, dizziness 15%, acid reflux or heartburn 14%, bloating or abdominal pain 14% ("boating" on the slide), vomiting 13%, loss of muscle or strength 12%, vision change 7%, other 1% (slide 14) [00:08].

- He saw a malnutrition risk for people who eat less without care, since most buy privately [00:08].
- Keeping weight off: 78% are confident, 94% including neutral (slide 15); he said around 80% and 95% [00:08], so the spoken and slide figures differ slightly. Their methods: exercise 59%, reducing snacks, sugar or alcohol 45%, increasing protein through everyday foods 35%, meal planning or coaching 33%, eating more whole foods 33%, a calorie-restricted diet 30%, tracking calories or macros 29% (slide 15).

He attributed it to the British Medical Association and said people are "four times more likely" to regain [00:08]; the slide says The BMJ and four times faster, so the two differ.

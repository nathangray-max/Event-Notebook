# Psychobiotic B. longum 1714 for mental wellness

## Summary
Louis Koeninger of Novonesis presented the company's psychobiotic strain, Bifidobacterium longum 1714, in the Day 1 gut-brain session. His question: people want better mental wellness but do not know how to get it; can a bacterium help?

He opened with consumer data: 65% of people rank mental wellness as the most important health topic, and 80% say they need to do more for it. Poor sleep and stress are the main obstacles. A psychobiotic, he said, is a probiotic whose benefit is for brain or mental health, and not all probiotics are psychobiotics.

The strain was found at APC in Cork in 2008. He described two routes of action: making tryptophan, and a strain-specific exopolysaccharide that he said shifts tryptophan metabolism towards a neuroprotective pathway. Much of that data is still unpublished.

Two trials carried the talk. In 22 healthy adults, the strain reduced the cortisol response to a cold-water hand test, with lower anxiety and perceived stress. In 89 poor sleepers over eight weeks, it improved sleep quality and daytime function, energy and vitality. An Antarctica trial is still being written up. Every trial was in healthy people.

In the Q&A he said brands cannot claim mental health benefits on pack in Europe; they can add ingredients that carry authorised claims.

## Key takeaways
- Demand: 65% of people consider mental wellness the most important health topic, 80% say they need to do more for their mental well-being, and poor sleep and stress are named as the major obstacles [00:00].
- Acute stress (Allen and colleagues, as Otter hears "Ellen" and "Alan", 2016; 22 healthy adults, repeated-measures, placebo-controlled, cold pressor test): a reduced cortisol peak and a significantly lower total cortisol output against placebo, plus lower state anxiety and perceived stress. He presents this as helping manage the response to everyday stress [00:00].
- Sleep (published 2024 by his account; 89 healthy adults with a sleep quality index above 5, eight weeks): a sleep quality component improved significantly from four weeks, daytime dysfunction at eight weeks, and energy, vitality and social functioning scores rose against placebo [00:00].
- Mechanism as he gave it: the strain produces tryptophan, a precursor of serotonin and melatonin, and a unique exopolysaccharide that he says moves tryptophan metabolism to the neuroprotective pathway; the plasma data on both were "out soon" or in revision, not shown [00:00].
- Claims: asked what brands can claim on pack, "You cannot claim it, not in Europe"; the route is to add ingredients with granted health claims [17:44].
- On clinical relevance in healthy people: "It's not that easy to answer"; he expects stronger effects under more stressful conditions and points to real-world evidence studies with a significant effect [16:23].

## Detail
- Mental wellness is defined from the Global Wellness Institute ("an internal resource" for resilience, growth and flourishing) and the WHO definition of mental health; country quotes from Germany and the US show people describe it the same way [00:00].
- The "vicious circle" consumers know: poor gut health can lead to stress and stress to poor gut health, then anxiety and poor mental wellness; "what people do not know is how to break the vicious circle" [00:00].
- The term psychobiotic is credited to Ted Dinan and John Cryan of Cork (Otter "John crying") [00:00].
- The strain was isolated from a mucosal biopsy of the colon of a 61-year-old; he presents it as a natural resident of the human gut [00:00].
- His claim: it is "the only psychobiotic solution clinically studied" across several aspects of mental wellness; this is the company's claim, not checked [00:00].
- Programme history as he gave it: discovered 2008 at APC, Cork; more than 15 years of research; acute stress trial 2016; a second trial published in 2019 as part of a colleague's PhD (the subject is garbled in Otter); the sleep study last year; a mode-of-action manuscript in revision [00:00].
- Sleep scoring: the component score runs from 0 (best) to 3 (worst); participants started at about 1, "fairly good", and still improved [00:00].
- Antarctica: a randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial in 32 healthy adults at Concordia Station, led by a professor at the University of Tübingen; manuscript in preparation; no results given [00:00].
- Asked about a non-viable (postbiotic) form: not studied clinically, because the mechanism depends on the exopolysaccharide [17:18].
- The chair asked, as a joke, whether it works for jet lag; there was no answer [16:02].
- No slides were filed; the trial figures are as spoken.

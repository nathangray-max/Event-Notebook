Gut microbiota in health and disease
•No ”healthy” microbiota defined, but charaterisedby a high diversity
•Several intestinal and extraintestinal diseases are associated with a
gut microbiota dysbiosis
•Low diversity
•Abundance of microbes which are considered harmful, those are not
necessarily pathogenes
•Irritable bowel syndrome, depression, anxiety disorder, autism,
ADHD, dementia, … are examples of diseases with
neuropsychological components associated to dysbiosis
•The gut microbiotais influencedby severalfactors, e.g. lifestyle,
diet, supplements, medications, …

Key messages from the literature
•Out of all microbiota modulations, probiotics intake is the most
studied in terms of its effects on the gut-brain axis
•Evidence from self-reported rating scales
•Most studies assess effects on behaviour, mood, cognition and brain
function separately vs only few studies assess effects in a combined
neuropsychological approach
Neuropsychology = The study of the relationship
between behaviour, emotion, and cognition on
the one hand, and brain function on the other.

Canwemoreobjectivelymeasurethis?
•Growing use of neuroimaging techniques in an effort to more
objectively assess the impact of probiotics on brain structure and
function
•We provide an exhaustive systematic overview of studies meeting the
following criteria:
•Randomised, placebo-controlled trials
•Participants aged 18 years and older (healthy or of any disease state)
•Oral probiotic interventions
•Outcomes assessed with neuroimaging techniques, including
electrophysiology techniques

Advantagesof fMRI
•Spatial resolution: 2-3 mm
•Measurements over time
•Temporal resolution: a few seconds
•Combined with structural images generated by MRI
•Non-invasive, repeated observations can be done on the same
individual even for non diagnostical purposes in healthy subjects
•As research tool, technology of choice for probing both the structure
and the function of the living human brain

Conclusions
•Probiotic interventions impact on brain health as assessed by
neuroimaging techniques
•Effects on neuroimaging outcomes and clinical symptom ratings
often correlate
•Allows for speculations regarding the biological mechanisms and
clinical relevance of effects
•While consistent with studies correlating brain connectivity and gut
microbiota composition, microbiota modulating interventions may
add towards understanding of directionality
Hutchinson et al. unpublishedDr Julia Rode @ Probiota 2025 in Copenhagen

--- page 23 ---
Take-homemessages
•Neuroimaging for assessment of probiotics is a novel approach with
the majority of studies being published within the last 5 years
•Yet, neuroimaging techniques may be more sensitive to detect subtle
effects of mild and short-term interventions to a greater extent than
symptom scores and cognitive tests
•Importance to develop standardisedprotocols in this relatively new
research field, also when choosing adequate study designs, outcome
measures and analyses

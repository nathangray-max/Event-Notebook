# Bifidobacterium breve MCC1274: The Unique Probiotics in Cognitive Health. Deep dive into Gut-Brain Axis

## Summary
Asuka Tada, commercial manager at MILEI, the European arm of Morinaga Milk Industry, pitched Morinaga's probiotic strain B. breve MCC1274 for memory in older adults. The studies shown are Morinaga's own.

She set the scene with ageing and dementia. People over 60 are expected to more than double between 2019 and 2050, and dementia cases to rise from 46.8 million in 2015 to 131.5 million by 2050. Because amyloid-β builds up about 20 years before Alzheimer's disease, she argued the time to act is mild cognitive impairment, the stage between healthy and dementia.

The gut-brain link comes next. People with Alzheimer's disease have less diverse gut bacteria and fewer bifidobacteria, which is why Morinaga chose a bifidobacterium for cognition.

The evidence is three human studies at 20 billion CFU a day. In an open pilot without placebo, MMSE scores rose into the normal range. In a 16-week placebo-controlled trial of 80 adults with suspected mild impairment, total, memory and visuospatial scores improved. In a 24-week trial in older adults, the deck reports less brain atrophy on MRI than on placebo.

She closed on the strain's credentials: patents, a Japanese functional claim in 2021, US GRAS status in 2022 and a memory supplement already sold in Japan.

## Key takeaways
- Positioning: act at the stage of mild cognitive impairment, where cognition can "recover", "maintain" or "decline", because amyloid-β accumulates about 20 years before Alzheimer's disease (slide 11) (slide 12).
- Open pilot (single arm, no placebo): 24 weeks at 20 billion CFU a day in older people with mild cognitive impairment raised mean MMSE from 24.3 to 27.0; the slide says the strain "improved cognitive function in participants with MCI to be normal score" (Kobayashi et al., 2018) (slide 19, figures read from the chart).
- Trial 1 (randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled; 80 adults aged 50 to 79 with suspected mild impairment; 16 weeks): RBANS total, immediate memory, visuospatial and delayed memory improved against placebo (P<0.0001 each); language (P=0.064) and attention (P=0.740) did not (Xiao et al., 2020) (slide 20) (slide 22, read from the charts).
- Trial 2 (randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled; 24 weeks; MRI analysis 42 on the strain, 47 on placebo): whole-brain atrophy was lower on the strain (P=0.0134), and the orientation score moved better; on the chart the total ADAS-Jcog lines of the two groups overlap (Asaoka et al., 2022) (slide 23) (slide 24, read from the charts).
- Credentials: patents for brain function, Food with Function Claims in Japan (2021), US FDA GRAS for foods (2022), 16 scientific studies as of September 2024 (slide 17) (slide 18).
- The finished product: a Japanese supplement at 20 billion CFU per two capsules, "reported to help maintain memory and spatial awareness" in healthy middle-aged and older people (slide 27).

## For Vita Actives
- Relevance medium: it is a competitor's playbook for the shelf a creatine-for-cognition pitch would go to. Morinaga targets the same healthy-ageing buyer with memory claims built on its own trials.
- My reading: the route they took, MCI-stage trials, standard cognitive batteries (RBANS, MMSE), MRI as a secondary outcome, a Japanese functional claim and GRAS, is a template for what a cognition claim for CreaWin would need to show.

## Detail
- Morinaga history: founded 1917 as Nippon Rennyu, launched Morinaga Dry Milk in 1921, discovered its flagship strain B. longum BB536 in 1969 (slide 2).
- Bifidobacteria levels peak in early life and fall in old age (Arboleya et al., 2016); Morinaga calls strains native to the human gut "Human-Residential Bifidobacteria", with B. breve as an infant type (slide 3) (slide 4).
- MCC1274 was isolated from an infant in 2002 and is aimed mainly at adults; certifications FSSC22000, Halal and Kosher; described as stable in "numerous innovative applications"; patents WO2017209156A1 and WO2021256077A1 (slide 17).
- Population: people over 60 about 1 billion in 2019, about 1.4 billion in 2030 and about 2.1 billion in 2050 (WHO; read from the chart) (slide 6). A UN chart shows Europe and Northern America with the highest old-age ratios (slide 7).
- Consumers: 35% say they suffer general forgetfulness and 31% periods of memory loss at least some of the time (FMCG Gurus) (slide 8).
- Dementia: in Japan, 5.3 million over-65s (16%) in 2015, 8.3 million (23%) in 2030 and 10.2 million (27%) in 2050 (Cabinet Office 2017; read from the chart) (slide 8). Worldwide 46.8 million in 2015, 74.7 million in 2030 and 131.5 million in 2050; Europe 10.5, 13.4 and 18.7 million (World Alzheimer Report 2015) (slide 9).
- Alzheimer's disease accounts for over 60% of dementias (63% on the chart) (slide 10).
- PubMed studies on the "gut brain axis" rose from under 200 a year in 2014 to about 1,700 in 2023 (read from the chart) (slide 13).
- Alzheimer's patients had lower gut diversity and less Bifidobacterium than controls (Vogt et al., 2017); the slide says probiotics, "especially bifidobacteria, may be effective" for cognition (slide 16).
- The deck's numbers for its trials differ between slides: trial 1 is "50 - 79 years old" on slide 20 and "Age 50 to 80" on slide 22; trial 2 is "130 patients aged from 65 to 88" on slide 23 and "Age 65 to 90, 89 people" on slide 24 (the MRI subset, 47 and 42) (slide 20) (slide 22) (slide 23) (slide 24). Trial 2's full analysis set was 55 on the strain and 60 on placebo (slide 23, read from the flow chart).
- Suggested mechanism (Microbiome Science, July 2024): metabolites such as acetic acid and indole-3-lactic acid, lower amyloid-β, less microglial activation and inflammatory cytokines, and more synaptic proteins via the vagus nerve; the slide calls it "suggested" (slide 25, read from the figure).
- Claimed firsts: the only probiotic introduced on the ALZFORUM website, and the "world's first" bifidobacterium shown to maintain memory as a single strain; NutraIngredients-Asia Award 2023 for cognitive function and a 2023 Japanese wellness award (slide 26).
- No slide is marked "Confidential". Every slide carries Morinaga's copyright.

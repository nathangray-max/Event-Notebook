# Gut microbiota & sports

## Summary
Silvia Barbaresi of Minutia.AI spoke on the gut microbiome in sport. She asked whether the gut microbiota is a "grand unified theory" linking the organs, and argued that it shapes training adaptation, performance and injury risk through a gut-muscle axis and a gut-joint axis.

The science section draws on published reviews. Gut microbes and their metabolites are linked to inflammation, energy use and muscle; one bacterium is shown clearing lactate, others are tied to motivation to exercise. Because each gut is different, she says interventions must be personal: "one does not fit all".

Her argument then turns to her company's product. She claims the microbiome is too complex for human experts alone, and presents Minutia Twin, described as the first digital twin of the human gut microbiome. A stool test feeds the model, which simulates diet and probiotic changes and produces a report with dietary advice for athletes.

The evidence is early. A pilot trial in 20 endurance athletes was ongoing, baseline data are said to match the literature, and the closing slides show the model projecting a higher VO2max. Every slide is marked confidential by the company. The talk is from February 2025, so its figures may be dated.

## Key takeaways
- Her thesis: the gut-muscle and gut-joint axes "play a key role" in sports performance and injury prevention, and gut-based interventions "are always customized" (slide 5).
- Mechanisms cited from the literature: Veillonella atypica clears lactate and releases propionate; E. rectale and C. eutactus are linked to dopamine and motivation to exercise; Bacteroides uniformis to energy metabolism and insulin sensitivity (Jin et al., EMBO Molecular Medicine 2024) (slide 8).
- The product: Minutia Twin, presented as the first digital twin of the human gut microbiome, with a sports version (Minutia Performance) to simulate the gut-muscle axis and predict each person's response to diet (slide 10).
- The service: stool metagenomics plus a questionnaire, ideally blood, urine and VO2max, then a report with a gut performance index for endurance and strength and dietary advice (slide 11) (slide 12) (slide 13).
- Evidence so far: a pilot in 20 runners and triathletes (VO2max above 45 ml/kg/min, 10 intervention, 10 control) with the University of Split and cmbio; only baseline results are shown (slide 14) (slide 15) (slide 16).
- The model's own projection, not a measured result: one athlete's VO2max from 44.0 to 53.5 ml/kg/min, or 57.5 in a second scenario that changes F. prausnitzii and butyrate (read from the screen) (slide 18) (slide 22).

## Detail
- The scale of the microbiome: 46 million genes (oral plus gut), more than 2,000 species, more than 100 trillion cells and more than 90% difference between individuals (Tierney et al., Cell Host & Microbe 2019) (slide 3).
- Three offers to sports teams: injury prevention (an inflammatory-state and leaky-gut forecast of likely injuries), a "booster" for consistent performance, and a "talent scouter" with a Performance Quotient estimate from metrics such as VO2max, 1RM and Borg CR10 (slide 4).
- Injury risk: beyond poor diet and high training load, ageing, genetics and antibiotic use are said to raise injury risk; leaky gut with circulating LPS is linked to joint and muscle inflammation; her own article in Sports Injury Bulletin, March 2023 (slide 5).
- Gut-muscle axis: microbial metabolites are said to support muscle through control of inflammation and oxidative stress, glucose and fat oxidation and mitochondrial activity; irisin and IL-6 from contracting muscle stimulate gut release of ghrelin, leptin and GLP-1 (Liu et al. 2023). The figure lists SCFAs and urolithins A and B against muscle mass and function (read from the chart) (slide 6).
- The gut microbiota is described as an "independent organ" linked to heart, brain, muscle, lungs and liver, mainly with aerobic and endurance exercise (slide 7).
- The "elephant paradox": scientists looking at single biomarkers miss the whole picture (slide 9).
- The model captures bacterial abundances, metabolites, demographics and lifestyle, physical performance, diet and physiology; it is described as a probabilistic mechanistic framework with "highly explainable results" (slide 10).
- Example report: gut microbiome performance indices of 2.9 for endurance and 3.5 for strength out of 5, and a nutritional advice page that includes a vitamin B complex (read from the screen) (slide 13).
- Baseline athletes: Prevotella copri was the most abundant species at about 8% (read from the chart); carbohydrate oxidation was the leading enriched function, which she calls higher metabolic flexibility (slide 15) (slide 16).
- Model inputs visible on screen include protein intake, fat intake, dietary fibre, arginine glutamate, arginine intake, citrulline malate intake, Akkermansia, Bifidobacterium, F. prausnitzii, lactate, IL-6 and VO2max (read from the screen) (slide 17) (slide 18).
- Her role: the report cover on slide 12 names her as co-founder and chief scientific officer, MSc biotechnology and PhD sport nutrition (read from the image) (slide 12).

# The Pantryome Paradigm: Framing the Microbial-Human Health Interface

## Summary
Jeremy Burton of the Lawson Research Institute and Western University in London, Canada, set out a new way to think about the gut microbiome. His group runs microbial therapy studies and kept finding molecules from energy pathways in its results.

His idea, the "pantryome", is that gut microbes share the parts they need to make energy outside their cells: B vitamins, haem, vitamin K2 and short-chain fatty acids such as acetate. A healthy gut keeps this shared pantry well stocked, and the host uses it too. He suggests disease may come with a poorly stocked pantry, citing kidney stone patients, whose gut microbes were less diverse than healthy controls'.

Supplementing these molecules directly mostly helps the host, not the microbes, because many are absorbed in the small intestine before reaching the colon. Free acetate is absorbed within 20 minutes. His group therefore focused on getting acetate to the colon. A prostate cancer drug that carries acetate deep into the gut raised Akkermansia about fivefold in patients. They then put apple cider vinegar capsules inside delayed-release capsules.

In a small, early pilot in young adults gaining weight on psychiatric drugs, the delayed-release vinegar changed the gut microbiota, with some markers trending better. He sees delayed release as a way to make probiotics, prebiotics and faecal transplants work better.

## Key takeaways
- The pantryome: microbes build "interconnected and interdependent electron transport chains" that rely on sharing energy components outside the cell, used by microbes and host; "The performance of this environment links to the health of the host" (slide 68).
- Shared nutrients include amino acids, B vitamins, haems and menaquinones (vitamin K2), mapped across the 75 most common gut genera in the PREDICT 1 cohort of 1,098 healthy volunteers (slide 24).
- The delivery problem: many helpful molecules are absorbed in the small intestine, and free acetate reaches the bloodstream within 20 minutes; supplementing the host may "temporarily help the host but not the microbiota" (slide 36) (slide 42).
- Abiraterone acetate, a poorly absorbed prostate cancer drug, carried acetate deeper into the gut: Akkermansia was about 5 times higher in treated patients, and rose over 1,000-fold in a host-free model (slide 46) (slide 47) (slide 49).
- A 500 mg apple cider vinegar capsule held 25 mg acetate, and many vinegar powders tested held no acetate; six capsules match the acetate in the drug's daily dose (slide 57).
- Pilot: people aged 16 to 35 gaining weight on stable psychiatric medication took three delayed-release vinegar capsules twice daily for three months, in an N-of-1 design; results are "Preliminary results only", with weight, lipid and cholesterol markers trending better and the gut microbiota changed (slide 61) (slide 62) (slide 63).

## Detail
- His group's studies in London, Canada cover rheumatoid arthritis, immune therapy, melanoma, pancreatic and renal cancer, NAFLD, HIV, multiple sclerosis, bladder and kidney transplant, cranberry, urinary infections and vaginosis, recurrent C. difficile, atherosclerosis, medication-induced weight gain and anorexia, using faecal transplants, prebiotics, probiotics, phage and fermented food (slide 2).
- "Dysbiosis" has no agreed definition; he lists five in use, and says the microbiome's complexity makes biomarkers hard to find (slide 4). A cited study found the gut microbiota of 1,000 very healthy Chinese people similar to that of healthy young people (slide 5).
- His group's 2021 Cell Reports review posits a well-stocked pantryome in a healthy microbiome and a deficit of shared resources in diseased ones, especially in metabolic disease; dysbiosis "might be more appropriately referred to as bioenergetic failure" (slide 17) (slide 20).
- Lactobacillus crispatus was found to release ATP, which may act on the bladder through purinergic receptors (slide 12) (slide 13).
- Kidney stones affect about 5% of people in Canada, with a lifetime risk of about 10%; half recur within 5 to 7 years; most are calcium oxalate (slide 29). A study of 83 stone patients and 30 healthy controls found lower gut microbial diversity in stone formers (slide 31) (slide 32).
- In the drug study, Akkermansia gains came with more microbial vitamin K2 production; acetate is "likely partially responsible" for the drug's efficacy, the deck says (slide 47) (slide 51).
- Acetate food additives are listed with their FDA and Health Canada status; most are GRAS (slide 54). Compounds screened included calcium, magnesium, manganese, potassium, sodium and zinc acetate, ethyl acetate, triacetin and apple cider vinegar (slide 56).
- Delayed-release capsule study cited: eight people, gamma scintigraphy, capsules opening in the stomach or small intestine (slide 58). A slide imaging an ingested probiotic (E. coli Nissle 1917) in a pig, marked preliminary and unpublished, notes small-intestine transit of about 4 to 6 hours and colon transit of about 24 to 48 hours (slide 41).
- Faecal transplants are costly: screening donors cost $15,180 for one enrolled donor out of 46 prescreened; capsules are now 30 to 40 per stool; he proposes acetate as an add-on to lower doses (slide 65) (slide 66).
- Figures date from February 2025. No slide is marked Confidential.

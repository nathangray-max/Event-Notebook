# The Gut-Mitochondria Connection – A new way to optimize health through the gut

## Summary
Mads Bjørnhof, Chief Commercial Officer of Migalis, a Danish supplement start-up, pitched as one of NutraIngredients' Probiota Pioneers for 2025. Migalis sells a blend of human milk oligosaccharides (HMOs) and pomegranate extract. The idea, which it calls "Mitobiotics", is to improve health through the link between gut bacteria and mitochondria, the parts of the cell that make most of its energy.

The HMOs feed good bacteria. Pomegranate supplies compounds that some gut bacteria turn into urolithin A, and many people lack those bacteria. Urolithin A is said to support the clearing out and renewal of worn mitochondria.

The evidence shown is one chart: urolithin A at about 4.9 mg/L with the blend, against about 1 with pomegranate alone, with no test type given. On stage Bjørnhof said the effect was confirmed in a lab gut simulation and a very small study, with clinical trials ahead. The focus is endurance athletes, for gut trouble, recovery and muscle fatigue; the product has soft-launched in Denmark.

In the panel Bjørnhof said it is not a rival to taking urolithin A directly, and that which gut bacteria do the work is not yet known. Bjørnhof would put new money into research first.

## Key takeaways
- The company's claim: urolithin A is a "metabolite that supports mitochondrial health through mitophagy", and many people lack the gut bacteria to make it; the blend "aims to support gut health and promote the production of urolithin A" (slide 4) (slide 2).
- One chart shows urolithin A at about 0.35 mg/L (blank), 0.27 (HMO), 1.0 (pomegranate) and 4.9 (HMO plus pomegranate), read from the chart; no study design, source or sample is given (slide 4).
- Target areas: brain, longevity and muscle, with endurance sport first: "Athletes struggle with GI challenges, recovery and muscle fatigue" (slide 6).
- Human clinical trials are listed as a next step (slide 8); on stage Bjørnhof said the effect was confirmed "in in vitro gut simulation study as well as in very small" study, as Otter heard it, with "lots of trials ahead of us" [22:40].
- Asked why not supplement urolithin A directly, Bjørnhof said "it's not sort of a competition", the aim being the natural process plus general gut health [40:38].
- Which strain converts pomegranate to urolithin A is not known: "We just know that with HMOs, it works" [57:06].

## Detail
- The four-step model: gut profile optimisation, urolithin A production, mitophagy activation, improved cell function, drawn as more ATP energy in muscle (slide 5).
- The company: a "Danish science-based nutrition and health company" making supplements that "harness the power of the gut microbiome to enhance health and performance" (slide 2). The packs shown read "POMHMO 2'FL" and "POMHMO Granatæble" (pomegranate), read from the photo (slide 2).
- "Mitobiotics: Support gut synergies by influencing the gut bacteria–mitochondria relationships"; the deck gives 100 trillion microbes in the gut, 35 trillion human cells and "quadrillions of mitochondria", assuming 1,000 per cell; target organs drawn are brain, immune, skin, muscle, bone and eye (slide 3).
- The pathway drawn: ellagic acid to urolithins D, C, A, iso-urolithin A and B; mitophagy is defined as "Mitochondrial renewal and biogenesis" (slide 4) (slide 5).
- Brain and longevity are tied to mitochondrial dysfunction "in brain cells" and in "functional performance of tissue and organs" (slide 6).
- Testimonials, the athletes' own words: ultrarunner Danni Møller; extreme hiker Kevin Lyngvold Rubiano; ultrarunner and coach Morten Klingenberg, who says "99% of all long-distance runners have experienced that the gut fails them before the mind or legs"; triathlete Lillan Sindahl Glümer, who reports a calmer gut and no loose stools after taking the product (slide 7). These are testimonials, not study results.

# The Gut-Mitochondria Connection – A new way to optimize health through the gut

## Summary
Mads Bjørnhof, Chief Commercial Officer of Migalis, a Danish supplement start-up, pitched its blend of human milk oligosaccharides (HMOs) and pomegranate extract. The idea, which the company calls "Mitobiotics", is to improve health through the link between gut bacteria and mitochondria, the parts of the cell that make most of its energy.

The argument runs in four steps. The HMOs feed good bacteria and support the gut barrier. Pomegranate supplies punicalagins and ellagic acid, which some gut bacteria turn into urolithin A. Many people lack those bacteria. Urolithin A is said to support mitochondrial health through mitophagy, the clearing out and renewal of worn mitochondria, which the deck links to cell energy.

The evidence shown is one chart: urolithin A at about 4.9 mg/L with the blend, against about 1 with pomegranate alone. The slide does not say what kind of test it was. The deck names brain, longevity and muscle as target areas and leads with endurance sport, backed by four athletes' testimonials about calmer guts on long runs.

Next steps listed are human clinical trials, consumer testing, new formats and new markets.

## Key takeaways
- Migalis sells a proprietary blend of HMOs and pomegranate extract that "aims to support gut health and promote the production of urolithin A" (slide 2).
- The company's claim: urolithin A is a "metabolite that supports mitochondrial health through mitophagy", and many people lack the gut bacteria to make it (slide 4).
- One chart shows urolithin A at about 0.35 mg/L (blank), 0.27 (HMO), 1.0 (pomegranate) and 4.9 (HMO plus pomegranate), read from the chart; no study design, source or sample is given (slide 4).
- The four-step model: gut profile optimisation, urolithin A production, mitophagy activation, improved cell function, drawn as more ATP energy in muscle (slide 5).
- Target areas: brain, longevity and muscle, with endurance sport first: "Athletes struggle with GI challenges, recovery and muscle fatigue" (slide 6).
- Human clinical trials are listed as a next step, so none had been run by February 2025 (slide 8).

## Detail
- The company: a "Danish science-based nutrition and health company" making supplements that "harness the power of the gut microbiome to enhance health and performance" (slide 2). The packs shown read "POMHMO 2'FL" and "POMHMO Granatæble" (pomegranate), read from the photo (slide 2).
- "Mitobiotics: Support gut synergies by influencing the gut bacteria–mitochondria relationships"; the deck gives 100 trillion microbes in the gut, 35 trillion human cells and "quadrillions of mitochondria", assuming 1,000 per cell; target organs drawn are brain, immune, skin, muscle, bone and eye (slide 3).
- The pathway drawn: ellagic acid to urolithins D, C, A, iso-urolithin A and B; mitophagy is defined as "Mitochondrial renewal and biogenesis" (slide 4) (slide 5).
- Brain and longevity are tied to mitochondrial dysfunction "in brain cells" and in "functional performance of tissue and organs" (slide 6).
- Testimonials, the athletes' own words: ultrarunner Danni Møller; extreme hiker Kevin Lyngvold Rubiano; ultrarunner and coach Morten Klingenberg, who says "99% of all long-distance runners have experienced that the gut fails them before the mind or legs"; triathlete Lillan Sindahl Glümer, who reports a calmer gut and no loose stools after taking the product (slide 7). These are testimonials, not study results.
- Status as drawn: studies, IP, user validation, product and commercialisation ticked; next, human clinical trials, target consumer testing, new formats and geographical expansion (slide 8).
- Team: Ina Giversen, PhD, CEO and co-founder (also CEO and co-founder of Nerthus ApS, inventor of Punalpin, a supplement for reduced sperm quality); Leif Helth Jensen, MSc, co-founder (former CEO of Glycom A/S, now dsm-firmenich); Kasper Kemp Hansen, MSc, chairman (Paxstone Capital) (slide 9).

# DynaMAP – Precision metagenomics beyond sequencing

## Summary
Arno Bouwens, R&D Director at Perseus Biomics, one of NutraIngredients' Probiota Pioneers for 2025, presented DynaMAP, the company's way of profiling a microbiome without sequencing. Their problem: benefits are strain-specific and labels must name the strain, but today's methods have low resolution and sensitivity.

DynaMAP labels long strands of native DNA with fluorescent dye at a short sequence motif, stretches and images them, and matches each pattern of dots to a reference database. Because it reads genome structure, Bouwens said it can tell apart strains almost identical in sequence, without PCR amplification.

Their evidence is the company's own preprint. In a standard mock community all strains were found, spikes down to 0.1% were detected, and strains up to 99.9% identical were told apart. On a stool sample, results matched shotgun sequencing closely.

Bouwens closed with the roadmap: a profiling service from the company's lab in Belgium with under five days' turnaround, available now, and a benchtop device from the second quarter of 2025.

In the panel Bouwens said customers had failed to find their own probiotic in samples with amplification-based methods. Their customers are experts, so the hurdle is conservatism, not education; new money would go on marketing.

## Key takeaways
- The pitch: strain specificity drives benefit, and labels must declare genus, species and strain, yet current methods face low resolution, sensitivity issues and noise (slide 2).
- The method reads 100,000+ long native DNA molecules per sample in under 20 minutes; the label pattern on each is a taxonomic barcode "down to strain level" (slide 4). Strains with average nucleotide identity (ANI) above 99.5% were told apart, and up to 99.9% in the mock community (slide 7) (slide 8).
- In the Zymo D6300 mock community it had a 100% recall rate, no significant Gram-positive against Gram-negative bias, a limit of detection better than 0.1%, and no false positives above 0.05% (slide 6).
- On one healthy adult's stool sample, profiles correlated 70 to 80% (log-log) with shotgun metagenomic sequencing, with equivalent reproducibility (slide 9).
- In the panel: customers came to Perseus after failing to detect their probiotic in samples, "most likely because of amplification bias"; shotgun sequencing and DynaMAP found it [54:50].
- Roadmap: a service from an EU-based central lab, under 5 business days, available now; a fully automated benchtop device with a 6-hour turnaround, from Q2 2025 (slide 10).

## Detail
- Slide 2 reproduces a figure from Spacova et al., Frontiers in Microbiology 2023: probiotic foods and probiotic supplements, the claims generally possible (structure-function, nutrient, general or strain-specific health claims and disease risk reduction, requiring pre-approval), and the labelling of genus, species, subspecies and strain (slide 2).
- Five steps and times: high-molecular-weight DNA extraction; fluorescent labelling (about 60 minutes); molecular combing (about 10 minutes); optical mapping (about 20 minutes); matching to a reference database (about 30 minutes) (slide 3).
- Strains can share over 99% ANI yet differ in genome structure; DynaMAP labels a site such as "TCGA" on fragments over 30 kbp and reads native DNA without amplification (slide 5).
- Mock community: eight bacteria at 12.5% each, three easy-to-lyse Gram-negative and five tough-to-lyse Gram-positive; spike-ins at 1%, 0.1% and 0.01%; the 1% and 0.1% spikes were reliably detected by both DynaMAP and sequencing (slide 6).
- Strain test: the reference database held all complete NCBI genomes over 200 kb; for Enterococcus faecalis the true strain was identified and the nearest false positives had ANI over 99.9% (slide 7). The slide's own definition puts the strain threshold at ANI 99.5% to 99.9% (slide 8).
- Stool test: five aliquots of one sample, compared with Kraken classification of sequencing on the same reference database, GTDB taxonomy (slide 9).
- Spoken: the central lab is "based in Belgium", turnaround "less than five days", and the device for use in a customer's own lab is being introduced "this year" [31:35].
- The shotgun comparison used about 10 gigabases of sequencing [43:01].
- Cost: "we were able to reach the same level as 60 this" (Otter; the method compared is unclear), including sample preparation and bioinformatics; a relative abundance profile is delivered [43:21].
- Customers hold their data and can reprocess it, under the FAIR principles [50:35]. What makes a microbiome start-up stand out, Bouwens said, is data quality, comparable across technologies [53:58].
- All performance data are the company's own, from its preprint (bioRxiv, December 2024) (slide 6). Figures and the roadmap date from February 2025; whether the benchtop device launched is not known. No slide is marked Confidential.

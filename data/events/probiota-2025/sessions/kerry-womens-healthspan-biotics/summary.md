# Supporting an improved healthspan in women with Biotics

## Summary
Mónica Olivares, R&D director at Kerry, set out women's health as a market for probiotics and postbiotics. Her case is that research has long used men's bodies as the model, while women now look for solutions to their own needs; the deck cites Kerry's 2024 research that women spend on average nine years in poor health.

Kerry frames women's health by life stage, in three areas: the hormone lifecycle, healthy living and family living. She mapped six of its branded biotics onto them, from a breast-milk probiotic for lactation to a yeast beta-glucan for immunity, and pitched its spore probiotic for protein absorption around menopause.

Two products got evidence. Plenibiotic, a heat-treated postbiotic, reduced water loss through the skin within four weeks. Most of the talk was a trial of HerPreg FERT2, a Lactobacillus salivarius probiotic, in 70 couples with unexplained infertility waiting for IVF: pregnancy success was 52% on the probiotic against 26% on placebo, which she linked to the strain colonising the vagina and raising growth factors. Much of this is labelled preliminary.

Her conclusion: biotics are a huge opportunity in women's health. The one question, on Plenibiotic beyond four weeks, got the answer that the difference held to six weeks. The talk is from February 2025, so its figures may be dated.
## Key takeaways
- Kerry's case for the market (Kerry WOW Research 2024): a woman spends an average of nine years in poor health, and the women's health gap equates to 75 million years of life lost a year (slide 2).
- Demand (same source): 80% of women are concerned about women's health issues such as menopause, menstruation and fertility, and 75% are adopting a long-term approach to health (slide 3).
- Kerry's frame: women's health is "strongly lifestage linked", in three areas (hormone lifecycle, healthy living, family living), with six branded biotics mapped to them (slide 4) (slide 5).
- FERT2 trial (Kerry's own, randomised, placebo-controlled, 70 couples, 3 billion a day for 4 to 9 months before IVF): successful pregnancy 52% on the probiotic (n=27) against 26% on placebo (n=30), which Kerry calls significant; the slide gives no p-value (read from the chart) (slide 12) (slide 15).
- Preliminary results: L. salivarius was found in the vagina in 89.5% of the probiotic group against 7.7% of controls, and vaginal VEGF rose 2.6 times and TGF-beta 1.9 times; higher levels went with pregnancy (slide 18) (slide 19) (slide 20).
- Plenibiotic (Kerry's own studies): in people with high starting water loss, transepidermal water loss was significantly lower after 4 weeks than on placebo (p<0.05) (slide 8).

## Detail
- Other market figures (Kerry WOW Research 2024): more than 300 million women menstruating on any day, 80% with period pain at some point; about 40% of fertility disorders due to ovulatory disorders, 85% of those linked to PCOS; 80% of mothers stop breastfeeding before they want to; one billion women peri- or post-menopausal, 12% of the population (slide 2).
- The product map: BC30 and Plenibiotic for the hormone lifecycle; Wellmune, BC30 and Plenibiotic for healthy living; LC40, BfM26 and HerPreg FERT2 for family living (slide 5).
- LC40 (L. fermentum CECT5716) is from the breast milk of healthy mothers and is described as clinically shown to reduce harmful bacteria that can cause pain during lactation; BfM26 (B. breve CECT7263) is "the first Bifidobacterium isolated from human breastmilk"; Wellmune is described as the first ingredient clinically studied as an oral innate immune trainer (slide 5).
- Plenibiotic: rice-derived, heat-inactivated L. paracasei 327; 50 mg for digestive health, 100 mg for skin; kosher, halal, vegan, non-GMO; 92% of consumers believe an inside-out approach matters for beauty and a third start taking steps for healthy ageing and skin at 30 (FMCG Gurus, Kerry WOW Research) (slide 6).
- Plenibiotic skin results: change in water loss about -1.2 against -0.6 g/hm2 on placebo (read from the chart); less flaky facial skin and less skin cracking against placebo; studies Kumagai et al. 2017 and Saito et al. 2017; the slide lists "Potential Health benefit messaging" such as "supports skin moisture retention" (slide 8) (slide 9).
- FERT2 positioning: 90 mg serving; one in six people experience infertility; one in five infertile couples have unexplained infertility; references "Fernandez et al. In preparation" (slide 10).
- Trial design: Universidad Complutense de Madrid and Hospital Clínico San Carlos (logos); couples on an IVF waiting list of more than four months; probiotic or placebo from day 0 to IVF at months 4 to 6, continued to 12 weeks of pregnancy (slide 12) (slide 13).
- Flow: 285 assessed, 70 randomised, 35 per arm; 8 lost in the probiotic arm and 5 in the placebo arm, leaving 27 and 30 analysed (slide 14).
- Other outcomes: failed IVF 22% against 34%; IVF cancelled for poor embryo quality 18% against 23%, and for other reasons 8% against 17% (probiotic against control, read from the chart) (slide 15).
- Vaginal microbiota: no differences in alpha or beta diversity between groups; Lactobacillus above 95% in most samples; L. salivarius DNA 4.60 against 2.25 log10 copies/mL (slide 16) (slide 17) (slide 18).
- Growth factors at time 2: TGF-beta1 2.33 against 1.25 pg/mL (p=0.002) and VEGF 349 against 110 pg/mL (p=0.018), probiotic against placebo (read from the table) (slide 19).
- Spoken, not on the slide: the spore-forming probiotic (BC30 on slide 5) can "improve the absorption of protein", which she called "especially interesting during menopause" because of muscle mass loss [00:00].
- Spoken: Wellmune in stressed women improved the immune response, reduced upper respiratory infections and "also improve mood"; stressed women have more digestive symptoms than stressed men, and digestive symptoms are common in menopause [00:00].
- Spoken fertility context: one in six people experience infertility (she cited the World Health Organization), and Europe has more than 1.5 million fertility treatments a year [00:00].
- In the talk FERT2 is for couples: "we are addressing both in the COVID women and men" (Otter's words), with benefits also observed in men, not presented [00:00].
- Otter has the trial led by "COVID University of Manchester, the group of Dr Vanier Rodriguez" with a Madrid hospital; the slide logos are Universidad Complutense de Madrid and Hospital Clínico San Carlos, so the university is most likely Complutense (my reading) [00:00] (slide 12).
- She said the lack of a difference in vaginal diversity between groups may be because "the sample size is too small" [00:00].
- Q&A: asked by the chair whether the Plenibiotic effect lasted beyond four weeks, she said the study ran six weeks, the largest differences were at four weeks and the difference held [17:23].
- Markings: slide 12 carries "Kerry Group Confidential" and slide 20 "CONFIDENTIAL AND PROPRIETARY", though the deck was shown at a public conference; slides 17 to 21 are marked "PRELIMINARY RESULTS" (slide 12) (slide 20).

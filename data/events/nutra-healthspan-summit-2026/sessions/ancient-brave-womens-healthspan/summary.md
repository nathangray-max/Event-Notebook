# Living better, not just longer: Shifts for women's healthspan innovation

## Summary
Dr Jenna Macciochi, Director of Science & Innovation at Ancient + Brave, opened the second day's women's health session. Ancient + Brave is a UK wellness brand selling collagen and supplements for women. Her question was how the industry should innovate for women who live longer, but not healthier, lives. Women spend 25% more of their lives in poor health than men.

She set out three shifts. First, from normalised to noticed: women have learned to accept being dismissed, and the industry sells to them with "pink" reformulations, fixed age bands, borrowed evidence and fear. Second, from reproductive hormones to whole-body transitions: hormones act on the heart, bone, muscle, metabolism, sleep and brain, and perimenopause is the least studied stage. Her example was the heart: twice as many UK women die from coronary heart disease as from breast cancer. Third, from borrowed evidence to evidence built with women, since research funding and trials largely leave women out.

Her answer was Ancient + Brave's own model: placebo-controlled trials of its products with universities, blinded consumer studies, and citizen science with customers. It also funds a PhD on collagen in midlife women. She closed on four principles: dose to the evidence, design for transitions rather than age bands, test the finished product with women, and say only what the evidence supports.

## Key takeaways
- Longer lives, not healthier ones: the gap between lifespan and healthy years is 2.4 years wider for women than for men across 183 countries, and women spend 25% more of their lives in poor health (slide 5).
- Women's supplements are under scrutiny for "pink" repackaging, static age bands and borrowed evidence; Which? found many menopause supplements use botanicals at doses too low to match the trials behind them. Fear (ageing as decline, hormones as a problem, hidden threats) is "a common sales tactic" (slide 9) (slide 10).
- Menopause as a whole-body transition: hormones act on brain, heart, bone, muscle, metabolism, sleep, gut, liver, immune system and skin; perimenopause is "Years of fluctuation, and the least studied". In the SWAN cohort cholesterol rose sharply around the final period, beyond what ageing alone explains (slide 12) (slide 13).
- Heart: 23,000 UK women die from coronary heart disease each year, twice as many as from breast cancer. Ancient + Brave, King's College London and Heart Research UK launch a Women's Heart Health Survey in December 2026, the first UK-wide one in 9 years and the first to add menopausal stage (slide 14) (slide 15).
- The evidence gap: under 1% of global research funding went to six female-specific conditions that carry 14% of the women's health burden, and 16.5% of 704 micronutrient studies included only men (slide 18).
- Ancient + Brave's evidence model: clinical trials ("Does it work?"), blinded consumer studies ("How does it feel?") and citizen science with its Inner Circle customers; a four-arm sleep trial of True Nightcap against placebo, with and without a wearable; and a funded Loughborough PhD on collagen and co-supplementation in midlife women (slide 19) (slide 20) (slide 22).

## Detail
- The brand: "a mission driven wellness brand with an award winning range of collagen" and supplements, designed to support women's health; its vision is to be the #1 wellness brand in the world, with new standards "for holistic health, sustainability and female empowerment" (slide 2).
- Research: over £400k over the next four years in independent research with Princeton Consumer Research, UEA, Cardiff and Loughborough universities; logos also show Cardiff Metropolitan and Liverpool Hope. Certification logos: B Corp, 1% for the Planet, The Climate Label, Buy Women Built, Rainforest Concern and ESG Mark. The slide's text layer also carries "£100m" with no label; it is not visible on the rendered slide (slide 3).
- The speaker: 25 years in immunology, human performance and health; former Senior Lecturer in Immunology & Biomedical Science at the University of Sussex; leads product development and clinical research at Ancient + Brave; author of three books, most recently Immune to Age (slide 4).
- Product photos: True Nightcap ("Advanced nutrient nightcap"), True Collagen (bovine collagen) and True Creatine+ ("Advanced creatine formula") on the title slide; True Magnesium+ in a photo (slide 1) (slide 7).
- Sources for the healthspan gap: Garmany and Terzic, JAMA Network Open 2024; World Economic Forum and McKinsey Health Institute 2024 (slide 5).
- What women have learned to accept: 84% said there were times they weren't listened to by healthcare professionals (nearly 100,000 respondents, England; DHSC call for evidence 2021); 56% of UK women feel their pain is ignored or dismissed, and 31% didn't want to "waste" a professional's time (Gender Pain Gap Index, Nurofen and Wellbeing of Women); 1 in 10 women who worked through menopause left a job because of their symptoms (Fawcett Society 2022) (slide 8).
- Menopause: average age 51, usually between 45 and 55; symptoms can start years earlier; more than 34 symptoms. "Women already have the ‘Pink Tax’ and now the hormone tax." Shown on the slide: the paper "What is in the bottle? An analysis of the ingredients in menopause supplements" (Sullivan, Boylan and Harper, 2026), a "Menopause gold rush" headline, and Which?, "Menopause markup", September 2026 (slide 9).
- On fear: "Fear sells answers and certainty when we should start by asking better questions", and without collective action a climate of fear persists "because it is commercially lucrative" (slide 10).
- The four transitions: puberty (bone and body composition build), pregnancy and postpartum, perimenopause, and post-menopause ("A new baseline for heart, bone and metabolism"). SWAN: Matthews et al., Journal of the American College of Cardiology 2009 (slide 13).
- Heart: up to 59% higher risk than men of an initial heart attack misdiagnosis; up to 80% of premature cardiovascular deaths are preventable. Sources include Heart Research UK, the University of Leeds (2016), the World Heart Federation (2025) and the Lancet Women and Cardiovascular Disease Commission (2021) (slide 14).
- The survey is ethically approved and designed to inform policy: when menopause is raised in an NHS Health Check, heart risk should be part of the conversation. The last UK-wide survey was Heart Research UK and Damart, 2017 (slide 15).
- England's Renewed Women's Health Strategy (April 2026) names cardiovascular disease as a priority for women and adds a menopause question to the NHS Health Check; the slide says it does not yet join the menopause question to heart health or plan how heart risk is raised in menopause care (slide 16).
- Evidence gap sources: McKinsey Health Institute 2025 (funding 2019 to 2023, 64 conditions); Science Advances 2021; Scottish Health Survey 2017. Over 80% of the micronutrient studies excluded pregnant or lactating women (slide 18).
- The Inner Circle: customers who opt in to research after three months or more on a product; over 900 survey responses in the past year. "Ingredient studies show what a compound can do. We also need to know what a product does in real lives" (slide 20).
- Citizen science runs with ARIA (myaria.health); its founder Georgina Kyriakoudes is quoted, with three participants' comments, one on the double-blind design (slide 21).
- The PhD: Ancient + Brave's first funded PhD, at Loughborough University with Dr Tom Clifford; independent academic research intended for publication; PhD researcher Chloe Errington (slide 22).
- The deck shows study designs but no results for the sleep trial, the consumer studies or the PhD (slide 19) (slide 20) (slide 22).

# From Data to Decisions: Personalizing Longevity

## Summary
Lauren Nicolle of NutraIngredients+ chaired the last session of the day, framed as AI across the product cycle: discovery with Paul Spagnuolo of ALT, clinical validation with Jeff Chen of Radicle Science, and the consumer with Ohad Manor of Thorne.

Chen described Radicle's decentralised, in-house trials, run at home at a scale site studies cannot reach. On hallucinations, Spagnuolo said they happen all the time and ALT keeps its tool inside the scientific literature. Manor said Taya is a closed system and every message is monitored. Chen said narrow knowledge bases and checking agents cut the risk.

Chen argued that home tests, wearables and phones now let trials collect data sites never could, and that machine learning is needed to read it. He wants biomarkers, wearables and surveys combined rather than one trusted alone. Manor said Taya has limits: complicated cases need a healthcare provider, and shoppers should ask who built a tool and on what data.

All three saw true personalisation as some way off. Spagnuolo pointed to genetics and lifestyle, Chen to precision groups first and trial and error over time, Manor to levels of personalisation. Asked about small studies, Chen called for scale and real-world diversity. Stephen Daniells then closed the day.

## Key takeaways
- Chen said Radicle Science has put about 100,000 people through double-blind, randomised, placebo-controlled trials in about six years, and is running a US-government-funded mental health trial of 6,000 people with one shared placebo and 19 treatment arms [34:11].
- Guardrails against hallucination: ALT keeps AMBROSIA from going outside the literature [36:40]; Thorne monitors and scores every Taya message and sends flagged ones to regulatory and medical [37:43]; Chen said narrow knowledge bases and checking agents cut the risk [39:03].
- Chen: home kits, wearables and phones let decentralised trials collect data sites cannot; agents can automate the "multi-100-step process" of a trial, and machine learning is needed to read the data [39:58]. Biomarkers, wearables and surveys each show one part, so he wants them combined [43:09].
- Small studies: an audience member said many industry studies are pilots of 45-50 or 100 people [58:46]; Spagnuolo said nutraceutical trials run to tens or hundreds, against tens of thousands in pharma [59:13]; Chen said the FDA and NIH are shifting funding toward decentralised approaches [59:31].
- Spagnuolo called for publishing what did not work, since "There's no journal of unpublishable results" [53:38].
- Chen sees a new chance for brands to show consumers, with their own data, that a product delivers its promise [1:02:46].

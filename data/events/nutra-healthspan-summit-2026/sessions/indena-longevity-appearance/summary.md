# The longevity paradigm: Healthy appearance as a reflection of internal vitality

## Summary
Serena Tongiani, Chief Portfolio Officer at Indena, the Italian botanical extracts company, argued that a healthy appearance reflects internal vitality and belongs in the longevity conversation.

She opened on the market. Nutrition Business Journal data show healthy ageing as the fastest-growing supplement category, and skin and beauty ageing among the top five concerns people address with healthy-ageing products. Botanicals can meet that need, she said, but they raise three problems: poor solubility and absorption, active content that varies with the harvest, and pesticide residues, even in organic material.

Indena's answer is a Centella asiatica extract standardised in triterpenes and formulated with sunflower lecithin on its phospholipid platform, sourced from Madagascar and grown in solar-powered greenhouses in Sardinia. In human skin cells it beat a standard extract on oxidative stress and collagen; the deck itself says this is not proof of absorption.

The centrepiece was Indena's own placebo-controlled trial in 52 women aged 45 to 60, with the manuscript submitted. After 84 days wrinkle depth at the crow's feet was 16% lower than at the start, and elasticity, firmness, skin thickness, collagen fibre density and the skin's antioxidant power improved. She closed on quality: there is no quality in a finished product without quality in its ingredients.

## Key takeaways
- Market (Nutrition Business Journal, 2026): healthy ageing is the fastest-growing supplement category and the only one in double-digit growth, at 14.6% in 2025, projected to reach $2.11 billion in 2029; 51% of consumers used dietary supplements to support healthy ageing or longevity in the last 12 months (slide 5).
- Skin and beauty ageing is addressed by 40% of people using healthy-ageing products, fourth after energy and focus (49%), sleep and recovery (47%) and joint and mobility support (45%) (NBJ, January 2026, n=832; read from the chart) (slide 7) (slide 9).
- Botanicals' formulation gap, with Centella as the case: limited solubility and bioavailability, total triterpenes varying from 35.5 to 83.2 mg/g across harvest periods, and pesticide residues in trade samples including organic-certified material (slide 9).
- In human skin cells (fibroblasts) the phospholipid-formulated extract outperformed a standard extract on oxidative stress, collagen release, collagen breakdown (MMP-1) and wound closure (about 40% after 24 hours). The deck marks this "NOT PROOF OF ABSORPTION", and a footnote says clinical benefit has not been established (slide 9) (slide 14).
- Indena's own trial: randomised, double-blind and placebo-controlled, 52 healthy women aged 45 to 60 with dry skin and mild to moderate crow's-feet wrinkles, 150 mg a day for 84 days; manuscript submitted. Primary endpoints: wrinkle depth -11% at 28 days and -16% at 84 days; skin elasticity (R2) +3% and +5% (slide 15) (slide 16) (slide 17).
- Secondary endpoints at 84 days: firmness (F4) -7%, skin thickness +2.2 µm, collagen fibre density +10%, fibre anisotropy -7%, skin antioxidant power (FRAP) +26%, TNF-alpha release -10%; fitted to the testing lab's reference data, wrinkle depth corresponds to 4.9 years younger, with "No variations observed with placebo." (slide 17) (slide 18) (slide 20) (slide 21) (slide 22).

## For Vita Actives
- Relevance medium: botanicals for skin are outside our range, but the trial targets women in midlife and sells "beauty from within" on collagen structure, the space collagen products hold, and the same NBJ ranking puts joint and mobility support (45%) above skin (40%) (slide 7) (slide 15) (slide 20).
- The sources differ on skin: Indena's NBJ survey ranks skin fourth among consumers' healthy-ageing concerns, at 40% (slide 7), while Nick Stene of Euromonitor said on day 1 that skin's premium on the US shelf peaked in 2025 and hair is next (euromonitor-who-is-winning-healthspan). One measures consumer use, the other shelf premium; both are given.
- My reading: most headline percentages match the change from baseline in the extract group's chart values (wrinkle depth 267.9 to 224.8 µm is -16%), not the gap against placebo; on elasticity the placebo group started higher and the two ended at 0.634 and 0.632 (read from the chart) (slide 16) (slide 17). Buyers comparing suppliers' trial figures will meet this; ours should state which comparison they use.
- My reading: the deck pairs a supplier-funded trial with plain caveats ("not proof of absorption", in vitro only) (slide 9) (slide 14). That is the standard CreaWin and IsoClr: Pea evidence has to meet when pitched for women's healthy ageing.

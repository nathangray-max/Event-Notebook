# The Challenge and Opportunity of Our Ageing Population

## Summary
David Weinkove, Professor at Durham University and Emeritus Chair of the British Society for Research on Ageing (BSRA), gave the first talk on the day's agenda. His subject was why slowing ageing matters, and why it is hard to prove.

He opened with a strong claim: our ageing population is a bigger threat than climate change, through the cost of living, pressure on health services and tension in society. A population pyramid of the UK in 2026 set the scene.

He then set prevention against treatment and argued that ageing is malleable. In England, people in the least deprived areas live 70.7 years in good health; in the most deprived, 51.9.

The science has three fundamentals. Ageing speeds up with time, people grow more different from each other, and the body holds its balance less well. That makes trials hard: they need large numbers of healthy but ageing people over several years, and funding and approval are difficult.

His call to action had three parts. Make the case that keeping people healthy for longer is good for everyone. Make trials long enough to measure ageing. Bring industry, charities, government and universities together. The BSRA, with over 300 members from academia and industry, asked the room to talk to it.

## Key takeaways
- The frame: "Our ageing population is a bigger threat than climate change", because of the cost of living, pressure on health services and societal tensions (slide 2).
- Ageing is malleable: in England (ONS, 2018 to 2020, in the Chief Medical Officer's Annual Report 2023), the least deprived 10% of small areas live 70.7 years in good health and 15.6 in poorer health; the most deprived 51.9 and 26.4 (slide 5).
- Three fundamentals of ageing: the rate of ageing accelerates with time, variation between individuals increases, and homeostasis declines (slide 6).
- Trials to slow ageing need large numbers and several years, ideally in people who are healthy but ageing, and funding and approval are challenging (slide 7).
- The call to action: make the case for healthy longevity, make trials long enough to measure ageing, and have industry, charities, government and universities work together (slide 9).

Microbiome-centred treatments: A potential role for bifidobacteria?
• Bifidobacteria respond strongly to specific dietary
carbohydrates and produce acetate and lactate which support
butyrate producers through cross-feeding.
• This makes them useful ecological “amplifiers” of fibre effects.
• B. adolescentis is especially relevant because it is adult-
adapted and often linked to complex carbohydrate metabolism.

Take-home messages
• The gut microbiome is a plausible contributor to healthy ageing, but human causality
remains incompletely established.
• Diet is one of the strongest modifiable determinants of healthspan, but current human
diet–ageing evidence does not prove microbiome mediation.
• Human intervention studies such as NU-AGE and NiMe show that diet can modulate
ageing-relevant microbiome–host interactions.
• Dietary change is powerful but difficult to implement and sustain, especially in older or
institutionalized populations. This highlights the importance of promoting healthy
dietary patterns early in life.
• Complementary microbiome-targeted interventions offer promising opportunities to
improve healthspan-relevant outcomes, but clinical trials are needed to establish their
benefits and determine whether these are mediated by the microbiome.

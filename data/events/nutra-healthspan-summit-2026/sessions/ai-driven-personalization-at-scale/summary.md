# AI-Driven Personalization at Scale: Turning Wellness Complexity into Clear Guidance

## Summary
Ohad Manor, Vice President of Computational Precision Health at Thorne, argued that supplement shoppers suffer from too much information, not too little. His example was Lena, 47, sleeping badly and wondering about perimenopause, faced with AI answers, social media and influencers.

Personalisation is the answer, he said, but also a double-edged sword: people differ, and so do ingredients, claims and formulations. Thorne has over 250 products, so even its own shoppers struggle to choose. He sees AI as the engine that can personalise at scale, provided it is data-driven, dynamic and educational rather than a black box.

Thorne's answer is Taya, an AI adviser launched in July last year. It works only from Thorne's own products, claims, science and articles, without web access. It asks follow-up questions, narrows its recommendations, adds lifestyle advice and shares articles. By Thorne's count it held over 300,000 conversations in its first year. Manor said it ignores bestsellers and promotions.

He closed by urging supplement companies to build similar tools. Otherwise people go to ChatGPT, which does not know a company's products as its own medical and ingredient teams do.

## Key takeaways
- Thorne's own survey, as Manor gave it: roughly 40% of consumers use AI for health questions, and two out of three would use an AI tool they trusted to make decisions [00:23].
- Taya is a closed system: built on foundation models, but without internet access and limited to Thorne's products, ingredients, claims and science and over 1,000 articles [00:23].
- Thorne's first-year figures for Taya, as Manor gave them: over 300,000 conversations, over 650,000 messages, over 1.2 million product and lifestyle recommendations, over 135,000 articles shared, and about 92% thumbs up [00:23].
- In his Lena example, Taya narrowed eight recommendations to two products, explained the ingredients and gave a daily routine with lifestyle tips [00:23].
- Taya recommends almost the whole portfolio, not the same five products; its top topics in year one were heart health and sports nutrition, then cognition and focus, bone and joint, and metabolism [00:23].
- His call to brands: build such tools, because ChatGPT "doesn't know everything about your products the way you know them" [00:23].

## For Vita Actives
- An AI adviser on a brand's site answers only from that brand's claims and science [00:23]. My reading: the evidence and approved wording we give customers for our ingredients may end up in the knowledge base such a tool reads, so it should be clear and claim-safe.
- Shoppers find ingredient forms confusing; Manor named citrate, glycinate, bisglycinate and threonate forms of magnesium [00:23]. My reading: form-specific evidence is what lets a tool like this choose one product over another.
- Relevance medium: consumer-facing AI at a brand; it touches our ingredients only through the claims and evidence brands feed it.

This meeting explores the use of AI to accelerate ingredient discovery, validate clinical trials, and personalize consumer wellness products.

## Consumer Personalization
**Ohad** presented on how **Thorne** uses **Taya**, an AI advisor designed to help consumers navigate a crowded wellness landscape. 
* **Taya** is a closed system trained on **Thorne's** proprietary knowledge base rather than the internet to prevent hallucinations. 
* The tool provides personalized recommendations by analyzing user data such as age, sex, and health challenges like sleep or perimenopause. 
* In its first year (launched July last year), **Taya** facilitated over 300,000 conversations and 650,000 messages, resulting in a 92% positive user rating.
* Discussion focused on the limitations of current text-based interaction: can it integrate wearables and blood markers for more proactive "N of 1" personalization?

## Scientific Discovery
**Paul** explained how his lab used to take ten years and millions of dollars to establish molecular mechanisms; he now utilizes **Ambrosia**, an agentic AI platform that performs iterative research tasks in seconds rather than days. 
* **Ambrosia** was tested via retroactive confirmation using an avocado-derived molecule that successfully identified its target as acyl-CoA dehydrogenase for fatty acid oxidation.
* This technology moves from B2C tools like **Taya** to B2B applications that help companies understand their ingredients better.
* Potential benefits include decreasing time-to-market and increasing success rates for new molecules.

## Clinical Validation
**Jack** described how **Radical Science** uses decentralized, tech-enabled trials through their own vertically integrated platform to generate high-quality evidence via machine learning and agentic workflows. 
* The platform supports massive scale compared to traditional research; currently running a study with 6,000 people involving continuous glucose monitoring and metabolomics.
* Decentralalized trials leverage at-home measurements such as Oura rings or smartphone sensors to collect more diverse real-world data than site-based research allows.
* How can researchers balance competing signals between different modalities like biomarkers versus wearables?

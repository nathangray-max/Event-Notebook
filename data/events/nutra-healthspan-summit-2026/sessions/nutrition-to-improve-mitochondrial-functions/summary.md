# Nutrition to Improve Mitochondrial Functions During Ageing

## Summary
Philipp Gut of Nestlé Research asked how nutrition can keep mitochondria working as we age.

He framed healthy-ageing nutrition across four need states, from prevention and peri-menopause to geriatric care, and three toolkits: nutrients, biotics and bioactives.

Calorie restriction is his reference point. In a 14-year study in dogs, a diet with 25% fewer calories gave a median life of 13 years against 11.2. Unpublished human data link a higher BMI to an older biological age, and weight loss, including on meal replacements, to a younger one. Mitochondria are vulnerable to ageing, and calorie restriction works partly through them.

He then showed four supplements aimed at mitochondria, creatine first, then nicotinamide riboside, urolithin A, and N-acetylcysteine with glycine. Nestlé's own research followed. Nicotinamide and vitamin B6 activated muscle stem cells in a human trial, by no direct mitochondrial route. Two NAD+ precursors raised blood NAD+ within two weeks, partly through the gut microbiome. Thymol, a plant compound, activated mitophagy and slowed functional decline in fast-ageing mice.

His conclusion was measured. Some benefits of calorie restriction can be mimicked with natural molecules, but more human evidence is needed, and biomarkers of ageing can serve as trial endpoints. He closed on Nestlé's new Healthy Longevity Academy for health professionals and a partnership with Nanyang Technological University.

## Key takeaways
- Calorie restriction: in a 14-year lifetime study in dogs, 25% calorie restriction against pair-fed controls gave a median life span of 13 years against 11.2, "+ 15% longer life", with better insulin sensitivity and immune function and later disease onset (Lawler et al., 2008) (slide 4).
- Weight and biological age, unpublished: biological age (PhenoAge) rises with BMI, weight loss in people with obesity lowers it, and meal-replacement regimens lowered it with weight loss, against baseline at weeks 26 and 52 (read from the chart) (slide 5).
- Creatine is the first of four "Examples of supplements to improve mitochondrial functions", with nicotinamide riboside, urolithin A, and N-acetylcysteine and glycine, each shown beside a Solgar or Pure Encapsulations pack (slide 7).
- Muscle: nicotinamide and vitamin B6 activated muscle stem cells and enhanced regeneration in a human RCT, with 29% more activated Pax7+ cells and 37% more regenerating myofibres against placebo; the deck says there is no direct mitochondrial mechanism (slide 8) (slide 9).
- NAD+: two weeks of daily nicotinamide riboside (1 g) or NMN (1 g) raised blood NAD+ against placebo; nicotinamide (0.5 g) did not. The route runs through low absorption and conversion by gut microbes (Christen et al., Nature Metabolism 2026) (slide 10).
- Thymol (20 mg/kg a day) lowered biological age, improved running distance, preserved grip force and raised the mitophagy protein Pink1 in fast-ageing SAMP8 mice; his translational verdict is that more evidence in humans is needed (slide 13) (slide 15).

## Detail
- The PDF has no text layer; the slide text below was read from the slide images. Slide numbers are the PDF's pages and match the numbers printed on the slides.
- Title slide: "Philipp Gut, M.D.", Nestlé Research, November 2026 (slide 1).
- Four need states: preventive nutrition for healthy ageing, peri-menopause related nutrition needs, obesity-related "hidden" malnutrition, and geriatric and acute care nutrition. Subtitle: "Understanding the biology of ageing can benefit health across need states" (slide 2).
- Three toolkits: macro- and micronutrients (ultra-dense protein micelles from dairy; calorie-optimised, high-NRF-index foods and supplements); biotics (synbiotics mediating gut-organ signalling; live bacterial spores as microbiome therapeutics, FMT, pharma); molecular nutrition bioactives (plant-derived mitophagy activators, α-acarbose mimetics and stem cell activators; endogenous metabolites such as co-factors and lipids) (slide 3).
- The dog study's biological-age data (Herzig et al., GeroScience 2024): "Calorie optimization to maintain lean body mass lowers biological age". Mortality hazard ratios: age deviation 1.47 (p=0.0178), restricted diet 0.26 (p=0.0007). Read from the chart (slide 4).
- Weight and biological age, marked "Unpublished data": PhenoAge z-score rises across BMI bands from under 18 to over 40 (p<2e-16); the median difference between biological and chronological age is about 1.7 years younger after weight loss and about 1 year older after weight gain; Optifast differed from the food-based diet at week 52 (**) but not at baseline or week 26. Read from the chart (slide 5).
- Section slide: "Mitochondria are vulnerable to ageing, impacting metabolism and cell signaling" (slide 6).
- Pack shots on slide 7: Solgar Golden Prime Vitality Creatine Complex (stick packs), Solgar Cellular Energy (nicotinamide riboside chloride, Niagen), Pure Encapsulations Renual, and Pure Encapsulations NAC + Glycine powder (slide 7).
- "NAD+ precursors and natural bioactives that target mitochondrial metabolism are promising candidates to slow cellular aging", from internal research: nicotinamide and vitamin B6 for muscle stem cells (Ancel et al., JCI 2024; Hojfeldt et al., 2026); NR and NMN raise NAD+ via the microbiome, and trigonelline is "a novel NAD+ precursor, with potential benefits for muscle strength" (Christen et al., Nature Metabolism 2026; Membrez et al., Nature Metabolism 2024); thymol is a novel autophagy and mitophagy activator, and oleuropein activates mitochondrial energy metabolism through MICU (Civiletto et al., Nature Aging 2025; Gherardi et al., Cell Metabolism 2025) (slide 8).
- Muscle stem cells: a screen of 50,000 nutrients found nicotinamide and pyridoxine stimulate Pax7 and MyoD in muscle cells; in aged muscle, NAM and PN brought Pax7+ stem cells back towards young levels (about 550 against 400 cells/mm², young about 580, read from the chart) (Ancel et al., J Clin Invest 2024); serum NAM and PN decline with human skeletal muscle ageing, shown against appendicular lean mass at 60 and 80 years; the human RCT is Højfeldt et al., Science Advances 2026 (slide 9).
- NAD+ trial groups: placebo (17/16), nicotinamide (17/17), NR (16/16), NMN (15/14). Blood NAD+ rose from about 30 µM to about 75 µM on NR and about 65 µM on NMN (read from the chart). Gut effects include short-chain fatty acid production and conversion of nicotinic acid to NAD+, with a "strong sustained NAD+ increase" in whole blood via the Preiss-Handler pathway (slide 10).
- Polyphenols and other secondary metabolites are "often associated to health benefits in nutrient-dense diets"; in plants they serve pathogen defence, competition, attraction and repulsion, UV, heat and drought protection, and nitrogen storage. Example: resveratrol, quercetin and piceatannol inhibit F1 ATPase (Li et al., Journal of Future Foods 2023; Gledhill, PNAS 2007) (slide 12).
- Thymol mouse study: SAMP8 mice from 8 to 11 months of age; running distance rose over 12 weeks on thymol but not in controls, and was about double control when normalised to baseline; grip force fell in controls but not on thymol (about 0.9 against 0.6 of baseline); Pink1 protein about 1.25 times control. Read from the chart (slide 13).
- Working hypothesis: thymol and carvacrol, plant metabolites with bactericidal effects also used as food preservatives and flavourings, "target mammalian mitochondria through a conserved bactericidal mechanism"; thymol mildly lowers mitochondrial membrane potential and strongly activates mitophagy (mitophagy index about double, read from the chart) (Civiletto et al., Nature Aging 2025) (slide 14).
- Translational considerations: "Understanding the mechanism(s) is key to enable clinical evidence development"; "Biomarkers of ageing can enable discovery, serve as clinical endpoints, and provide frameworks for precision health" (slide 15).
- Nestlé Nutrition Institute's Healthy Longevity Academy, "Now launched", for healthcare professionals only, with five pillars: The Foundations, Fuel and Frame ("Powering strength, metabolic health and cognitive vitality"), Cellular Power, Cellular Renewal, and The Inner Ecosystem (slide 16).
- Nestlé Research teams in Switzerland, Beijing and Singapore; collaborators EPFL, Harvard Medical School, Clock Foundation, Università degli Studi di Milano, University of Copenhagen and Institut NeuroMyoGène.

# Consumer Insights - Straight from the Horse's Mouth

## Summary
Stephen Daniells of NutraIngredients chaired eight members of the public, who gave their first names and ages: Robert (38), Matt (27), Mike (51), Michelle (64), Rahav (25), Donella (51), Nadine (46) and Kane. He asked what they focus on, where they get their information and how they would take a supplement pitch. The audience asked about the long term, AI and trust.

They agreed on most things. All try to exercise and eat well; supplement use runs from none to four. Natural ingredients win attention; pills and synthetic ingredients put several of them off. They want proof before they buy: the science, an effective dose and independent research. Holland & Barrett earns trust by being long established, and most distrust influencers. Nobody raised price.

They differed in their concerns and their sources. The men named heart, colon and mental health; the women named sleep, menopause, hormones and bones. Michelle follows Ayurveda, Donella wants natural and regenerative sourcing, and Kane takes creatine and protein powder for training. Rahav, the youngest, does not mind non-natural products if a brand explains why she needs them today, and wants trials to say whom they did not test. On AI, Mike checks supplements with ChatGPT and Claude and asks for the evidence; Matt scrolls past AI health answers.

## Key takeaways
- Naturalness opens the conversation: Robert listens more the more natural the product and shuts off at pills [23:46], Matt and Mike said much the same [24:01] [24:34], and Robert named naturalness as the main thing to build into a new product [45:44]. Rahav was the exception: she does not mind non-natural products if she is told how they help her [47:02].
- Proof decides: Michelle wants the science and a dose high enough to be effective [25:28] [25:59], Nadine wants to know whether the research was independent or paid for by a company with a vested interest [31:00], Mike wants clinical trials on the brand's site [42:40], and Rahav wants trials to say honestly whom they were not tested on [43:43].
- Trust comes from time and brand: Mike and Kane trust Holland & Barrett because it has been around a long time [24:34] [31:45]; a new brand, Mike said, needs a mix of advertising, proof on its site and a brand story, and cannot be built overnight [42:40] [47:44].
- Influencers are distrusted: Matt avoids health advice from social media [18:23], Mike asks "what's in it for them" [42:40], and Kane said what works for an influencer may not work for him [31:45]. Their sources are friends, Ayurvedic doctors, an Instagram feed and a Netflix documentary on Blue Zones [10:12] [17:45] [16:37] [20:32].
- AI is used with checks: Mike asks ChatGPT and Claude for evidence and checks it on Google [36:58], Rahav uses AI for low-stakes questions only [38:15], Nadine said AI has to be verified [38:53], and Matt ignores AI on health [37:33].
- Collagen needs a reason today: Rahav wants a collagen pitch to explain why she needs it when her great-grandmother did not [26:18], and Donella wants to know what to take with collagen and where it comes from [28:18].

## For Vita Actives
- Relevance high: these are supplement purchase drivers from consumers themselves, with collagen, protein powder and creatine named [05:22] [26:18].
- Origin, naturalness and sustainability came up without prompting [23:46] [28:18] [29:29]. My reading: our ingredients' source, processing and sustainability story should be ready for brand customers' consumer copy, stated only as far as the facts go.
- Dose and independent proof were asked for directly [25:59] [31:00]. My reading: clinically documented ingredients at a stated effective dose give our customers the answer these shoppers asked for.
- Donella asked where collagen comes from, fish or bovine, and what the animals eat [28:18]. My reading: a clear origin and traceability sheet for our collagen would help brands answer that question.
- These are eight people speaking about their own habits: personal experience, not evidence or a market sample (my reading).

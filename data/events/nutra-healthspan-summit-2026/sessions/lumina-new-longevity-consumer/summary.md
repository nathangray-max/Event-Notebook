# Too Young to Age? The New Consumer Rules of Longevity

## Summary
Ewa Hudson, Strategy & Insight Director at ReThink Global, Lumina Intelligence, presented a July 2026 survey of 10,500 consumers in nine countries. Her question: consumers are rewriting the rules of ageing, so is the industry ready to rewrite its own?

Her first point was that the longevity consumer is no longer old. Retired people mostly began thinking seriously about ageing in their 60s; people in full-time work mostly in their 30s. Younger people blame their genes less and put more faith in supplements, and one in four is actively pursuing anti-ageing.

Ageing well, she said, means having the vitality to keep reinventing yourself. Energy, stamina and physical strength is a top-three concern in every age band, and mobility, joint and bone health comes first for women from their mid-thirties. Needs shift with life stage and country, and GLP-1 treatment is moving UK users towards healthier habits.

Trust is the gap: in the UK, not enough proof was the most common hesitation about longevity supplements. Brands such as Niagen, Life Extension and Novos are moving from selling supplements to measuring and managing biological ageing. Her conclusion: applied and communicated science will be the number one trust creator, and the healthspan leaders of the next decade may be platforms that measure, manage and optimise ageing.

## Key takeaways
- Concern about ageing starts younger: retired respondents mostly began thinking seriously about it in their 60s (36%); in the deck's version of the chart, people working full-time mostly began in their 30s (about 30%, read from the chart). She called it "a generational shift" [00:04] (photo 1) (slide 4).
- The younger the respondent, the less they credit genetics and the more they credit supplements: genetics from 39% at 65 plus down to 21% at 18 to 24; supplements from 20% at 25 to 34 down to 12% at 65 plus [00:04] (slide 2) (slide 3).
- One in four is actively pursuing anti-ageing: 16% want to prevent premature ageing and 11% to reverse it, beside 52% who want to age slowly and healthily [00:04] (photo 2) (slide 5).
- Energy, stamina and strength is in the top three concerns of every age band, for men and women; mobility, joint and bone health is in the top three of every band from 35 to 44 up for both, and from 25 to 34 for women (photo 3) (slide 8).
- Trust is the gap. Biggest hesitation about longevity supplements in the UK: not enough proof 22%, cost 21%, don't believe claims 20%, safety concerns 20%. Across the survey, 36% already take supplements to counteract natural ageing [00:04] (slide 17) (slide 18).
- Brands are moving from product to platform: Niagen added telehealth, IV and injections, skincare and a drug pipeline; Life Extension sells ageing blood tests to take before and after supplements; Novos sells an at-home biological-age kit [00:04] (photo 4) (slide 9) (slide 19).

## For Vita Actives
- Energy, stamina and strength is a top-three ageing concern in every age band for both men and women (photo 3), and her list of what products must show starts with "Energy and physical strength" and ends with "Everyday activity and recovery" (slide 8). My reading: this is the concern our sports and active nutrition ingredients (CreaWin, IsoClr: Pea, Oxystorm) sit closest to, so healthy-ageing brands are a natural audience for them, pitched within what each ingredient's own evidence supports. The concerns slide gives an order, not percentages.
- People working full-time mostly start thinking about ageing in their 30s [00:04] (slide 4). My reading: the healthy-ageing buyer and the active adult our customers already sell to are increasingly the same person, which widens where sports ingredients can be pitched.
- 20% name amino acids and proteins among the categories most helpful for extending healthspan, behind vitamins (50%), omega-3 (39%), minerals (25%) and probiotics (22%) (slide 17). Muscle appears in her life-stage messages (UK women in their late 40s, US men in their late 30s), but 5% or fewer choose muscle mass or strength as their single most important goal (slide 10) (slide 11) (slide 13) (slide 14). My reading: muscle is a supporting healthy-ageing message, not the lead; energy, strength and everyday activity are the better door for CreaWin and IsoClr: Pea.
- Not enough proof is the top UK hesitation, at 22% (slide 18), and brands now test before and after supplements (photo 4). Science published behind the scenes "will feed all the AI tools" consumers ask for advice [00:04]. My reading: brands will ask suppliers for clinical evidence and outcomes a consumer can measure; our evidence should be ready, public and easy for those tools to read.
- Relevance high: consumer data on ageing, with energy and strength as the leading concern and proteins named by one in five.

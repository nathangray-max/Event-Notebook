# Too Young to Age? The New Consumer Rules of Longevity

## Summary
Ewa Hudson of Lumina Intelligence presented consumer research on ageing from a survey of over 10,000 people in nine countries. Her question: consumers are rewriting the rules of ageing, so is the industry ready to rewrite its own?

Her first point was that the longevity consumer is no longer old. Retired people mostly began thinking seriously about ageing in their 60s; people in full-time work mostly in their 30s. Younger people blame their genes less and put more faith in supplements, and one in four is actively pursuing anti-ageing.

Ageing well, she said, means having the vitality to keep reinventing yourself. She named energy, stamina and physical strength as the top concern across age brackets, and mobility, joint and bone health as first for women. Needs shift with life stage and country, and GLP-1 treatment is moving users towards healthier habits.

Trust is the gap: the first answer on hesitation about longevity supplements was not enough proof. Some brands are moving from selling supplements to measuring biological ageing, as Life Extension does with blood tests taken before and after supplements. Her conclusion: applied and communicated science will be the number one trust creator, and the leaders of the next decade may be platforms that measure, manage and optimise ageing.

## Key takeaways
- Concern about ageing starts younger: retired respondents mostly began thinking seriously about it in their 60s (36% on the Retired bar), people in full-time work mostly in their 30s; she called it "a generational shift" [00:04] (photo 1).
- The younger the respondent, the less they credit genetics and the more they credit supplements: genetics from 39% at 65 plus down to 21% in the youngest group; supplements from 20% at 25 to 34 down to 12% at 65 plus [00:04].
- "[1 in] 4 is actively pursuing [anti]-ageing": 16% want to prevent premature ageing and 11% to reverse it, beside 52% who want to age slowly and healthily [00:04] (photo 2).
- Energy, stamina and strength is in the top three concerns of every age band shown, for men and women; mobility, joint and bone health is in the top three of every band from 35 to 44 up for both, and from 25 to 34 for women (photo 3).
- Trust is the gap: the first answer to "What's your biggest hesitation about taking longevity supplements?" was not enough proof; 36% already take supplements to counteract natural ageing [00:04].
- Brands are moving from selling supplements to managing biological ageing: Life Extension sells ageing blood tests to take before and after supplements, and she gave Novos as "Another similar example" of a move "from supplement brand to health intelligence company" [00:04] (photo 4).

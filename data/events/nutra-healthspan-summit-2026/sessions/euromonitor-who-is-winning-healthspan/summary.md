# Fewer products, more proof: who is winning the healthspan economy?

## Summary
Nick Stene, Senior Global Insight Manager for consumer health at Euromonitor International, opened the Healthspan Summit. His question was where the money in healthspan is going and which messages earn a premium.

He started with the market. Consumer health grows about 5%, only 1.5% without inflation, and this is the first year more is spent on prevention than on treatment. Medicines sold over the counter and sports protein are both under profit pressure, so money, including billions in deal finance, is moving to premium dietary supplements.

Within healthspan, products that name a specific need or life stage earn a premium, while broad wording appears on cheap and expensive products alike. Ingredients follow a life cycle: proof brings a premium, popularity brings scale, then a price war. Magnesium forms have gone that way and NAD+ is close, with postbiotics leading what queues behind it. IM8, one product meant to replace a shelf of supplements, was his example of a brand winning on proof and trust.

His conclusion: lead with proof, know where each ingredient is in its life cycle, segment and price for specific groups such as women entering perimenopause, and design proof for shoppers, healthcare professionals and the AI agents that will increasingly shop for them.

## Key takeaways
- Milestone: "the first year in which we have spent more physically on prevention than we have on treatment", and the gap between supplements and OTC will widen [00:20] (photo 2).
- Money is moving to premium dietary supplements, through line extensions, brand actions and M&A; he sees "about eight to $9 billion in motion", some from venture capital and private equity outside health [00:20].
- Healthspan as a funnel: the edge (SKU names directly on age and life stage, including mitochondrial) is niche, growing and earns a premium; broader healthspan wording appears on cheap and expensive products alike; protection messages (immune, antioxidant) cover about half the shelf [00:20].
- Ingredient life cycle: proof of benefit, then premium, then popularity, then price war. Magnesium forms "turned into footballs" on price, while L-threonate, the cognitive form, still holds a premium; "popularity is the death knell on premium" [00:20] (photo 3).
- NAD+ is losing its premium as it goes mainstream (US price index 1.84 in 2024, 1.43 in 2026 to date); postbiotics lead what queues behind it, with share up 48% and a price index of 2.83 [00:20] (photo 4).
- IM8 is the fastest year-one growth he has seen: one product of 90 ingredients positioned to replace 16 supplements, with year-two revenue guidance of USD 220 to 230 million [00:20] (photo 7).

## Detail
- The speaker was introduced as Nick Steen; the title slide gives Nick Stene, Senior Global Insight Manager, Consumer Health, Euromonitor International [00:00] (photo 1).
- Market: he gave consumer health as "373 billion", growing about 5%, or 1.5 without inflation, half a point down; treatment has gone negative in real terms [00:20]. The slide gives USD 373 billion, +5% in current terms (3.5% price rises) and +1.5% without inflation ("demand stagnation") (photo 2).
- Growth by category, 2025 then 2026: vitamins and dietary supplements 5.9% and 5.6%; OTC 3.4% and 3.2%; sports nutrition 13.3% and 12.5%; weight management and wellbeing 0.2% and 0.3%. Shares of the market: vitamins and supplements 42%, OTC 42%, sports nutrition 11%, weight management and wellbeing 5%. The slide's line: "2026 is the year when we started spending more on prevention than we do on treatment" (photo 2).
- OTC is hit hardest by tariffs, by dependence on active pharmaceutical ingredients from India and China, and, next, by European wastewater rules [00:20].
- Sports protein, as he said it: he has shelf data showing a 20% plus pass-through in physical pricing, 30 to 40% overall once pack shrinkage and other absorption moves are counted. On premium whey supply: "So the protein one probably starts to get solved in supply because it's scarcity driven around premium way around 2027 back end, 2028 earliest", with "years of trauma" left [00:20].
- Growth is stronger in shops than online, where trust is leaking; demographics explain about 30 to 40% of growth (people entering a new life stage, not the size of the ageing population), brand push about another third, and employers' attitudes to time off work more [00:20].
- On the US shelf, longevity and healthy ageing grow fastest and earn a strong premium; menopause is losing its premium while 40-plus and perimenopause still earn one; prostate has lost its premium; skin peaked in 2025 and hair is next; immune has peaked but remains a workhorse [00:20].
- Europe follows the same pattern, but mitochondrial messaging is already stronger than in the US, partly because of what the regulatory framework allows, and healthy-ageing messaging is "a tougher sell", some of it only online [00:20].
- Magnesium forms in Europe, January to August 2026 against 2025, read approximately from the chart (price index against matched peers, change in share of products): carbonate about 1.4 and 0%; oxide about 0.75 and 3%; citrate about 0.95 and 13%; bisglycinate about 0.65 and 42%; taurate about 0.55 and 43%; malate about 0.55 and 48%; L-threonate (cognitive) about 1.17 and 49%. The slide's line: "Popularity is a consistent death [k]nell for premium [achieve]ment." (photo 3). Otter heard L-threonate as "L3 and eight" [00:20].
- NAD+ escaped niche last year and grew three times on the US shelf in 18 months; stacks with spermidine and resveratrol won back some premium in 2026, but it is near price war [00:20]. The slide's NAD+ family price index (NAD+, NADH, NR, NMN, NMNH): US 1.84 in 2024, 1.28 in 2025, 1.43 in 2026 to date; Europe 1.68, 1.50 and 1.32 (photo 4).
- Queuing behind NAD+, US 2026 (share growth, price index): postbiotics +48%, 2.83; urolithin A +17%, 1.37; TMG +19%, 1.23; fisetin +22%, 1.14; spermidine +45%, 1.13. The slide's line: "What's next? Because nothing stays shiny and new forev[er]" (photo 4).
- Postbiotics are growing fast, have "really strong connections into GLP-1" and earn one of the strongest premiums; he is watching them most closely. In his words: "But postbiotics is at this moment in time kicking ass" [00:20].
- Life stage, US products per 10,000 supplement products, 2024, 2025 and 2026 to date: testosterone 171, 182, 163; prostate 70, 85, 98; menopause 46, 64, 73; andropause 0, 1, 2. The slides: perimenopause is picking up fast while menopause slows; andropause is "near non-existent on the shelf"; testosterone is more than twice the size of menopause on the shelf, stalled in the US, still rising in Europe and getting cheaper, with fading spikes in tongkat ali, tribulus and shilajit; prostate support now rises fast (photo 5) (photo 6).
- Men do not buy into an andropause label; they respond to testosterone, performance and prostate messages [00:20] (photo 6).
- IM8, as he said it: over 100 million in year one, on target for 220 to 230 in year two, 400 million projected for year three, and a billion raised on a profitable subscription model [00:20]. The slide: year-two revenue guidance USD 220 to 230 million and 2027 full-year guidance over USD 400 million; US online sales peaked in Q4 2025 and eased slightly in Q1 and Q2 2026; the US is by far its largest market, ahead of the UK and Canada (photo 7).
- Why IM8 wins, from the slide: one product replaces a shelf (90 ingredients, positioned to replace 16 other supplements); celebrity equity and a science council, driving word of mouth; subscription economics a lender can fund, with USD 1.44 of declared gross profit per USD 1 of acquisition; and an AI-native acquisition engine across 43 markets. "IM8 nailed "trust" in all its flavours" (photo 7).
- He named IM8 and Ancient + Brave as brands nailing proof [00:20]. Nathan's note: IM8 "is winning a lot in the market, alongside Ancient+Brave" (notes).
- Positioning that facilitates "someone's perfect life" lasts longer than solving a problem, which ends when it is fixed or fails; collagen can be sold either way [00:20]. The slide sets "something wrong [with] you our product can fix" (joints, wrinkles, ageing, collagen deficiency) against "Aspire to a life free of imperfection" (vitality, performance, radiance, wellbeing): "you never stop taking something that is part of who you want to be" (photo 8).
- His takeaways: lead with proof; know the ingredient's life stage and the pipeline behind it; segment and price, with women heading into perimenopause badly served; and design proof for three gatekeepers: shoppers, healthcare professionals and agentic commerce [00:20].

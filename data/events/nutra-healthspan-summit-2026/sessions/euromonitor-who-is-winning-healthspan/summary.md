# Euromonitor: who is winning in the healthspan economy

## Summary
Nick Stene (as titled; introduced as Nick Steen), senior global insight manager for consumer health at Euromonitor, opened the Healthspan Summit on where the money in healthspan is going and which messages earn a premium [00:00] [00:20]. He said consumer health is growing about 5%, 1.5% without inflation, and this is the first year more is spent on prevention than on treatment [00:20]. OTC and sports protein both face profit pressure (tariffs and supply; premium whey costs), so money and $8 to $9 billion of deal finance are moving to premium dietary supplements [00:20]. Specific healthspan naming earns a premium while broad messaging is diluted, and ingredients lose their premium as they become popular, as magnesium forms have and NAD+ is starting to; postbiotics are his one to watch [00:20]. His takeaways: proof, knowing each ingredient's life stage, segmenting (especially perimenopause), and designing proof for shoppers, healthcare professionals and AI shopping agents [00:20].

## Key takeaways
- Market: consumer health totals "373 billion" as he scopes it (currency not stated), growing about 5%, or 1.5 without inflation, half a point down; treatment has gone negative in real terms [00:20].
- Milestone: "the first year in which we have spent more physically on prevention than we have on treatment", and the gap between supplements and OTC will widen [00:20].
- OTC is hit hardest by tariffs, by dependence on active pharmaceutical ingredients from India and China, and, next, by European wastewater rules [00:20].
- Sports protein: he has shelf data showing a 20% plus pass-through in physical pricing, 30 to 40% overall once pack shrinkage and other absorption moves are counted; the premium whey supply problem "probably starts to get solved" around the back end of 2027, 2028 at the earliest, with "years of trauma" left [00:20].
- Money is moving to premium dietary supplements, through line extensions, brand actions and M&A; he sees "about eight to $9 billion in motion", some from venture capital and private equity outside health [00:20].
- Healthspan as a funnel: the edge (SKU names directly on age and life stage, including mitochondrial) is niche, growing and earns a premium; broader healthspan wording appears on cheap and expensive products alike; protection messages (immune, antioxidant) cover about half the shelf [00:20].
- Growth is stronger in shops than online, where trust is leaking; demographics explain about 30 to 40% of growth (people entering a new life stage, not the size of the ageing population), brand push about another third, and employers' attitudes to time off work more [00:20].
- On the US shelf, longevity and healthy ageing grow fastest and earn a strong premium; menopause is losing its premium while 40-plus and perimenopause still earn one; prostate has lost its premium; skin peaked in 2025 and hair is next; immune has peaked but remains a workhorse [00:20].
- Europe follows the same pattern, but mitochondrial messaging is already stronger than in the US, partly because of what the regulatory framework allows, and healthy-ageing messaging is "a tougher sell", some of it only online [00:20].
- Ingredient life cycle: proof brings a premium, popularity brings scale, then price war; magnesium forms "turned into footballs" on price; the form Otter heard as "L3 and eight" still holds a premium on cognition; "popularity is the death knell on premium" [00:20].
- NAD+ escaped niche last year and grew three times on the US shelf in 18 months; stacks with spermidine and resveratrol won back some premium in 2026, but it is near price war [00:20].
- Postbiotics are growing fast, have "really strong connections into GLP-1" and earn one of the strongest premiums; he is watching them most closely [00:20].
- Men do not buy into an andropause label; they respond to testosterone, performance and prostate messages [00:20].
- A brand Otter heard as "I am eight" broke 100 million in year one, is on target for 220 to 230 in year two, projects 400 million in year three and raised a billion in funding on a profitable subscription model; he named it and "ancient and brave" as brands nailing proof [00:20].
- Positioning that facilitates "someone's perfect life" lasts longer than solving a problem, which ends when it is fixed or fails; collagen can be sold either way [00:20].
- Takeaways: lead with proof; know the ingredient's life stage and the pipeline behind it; segment and price, with women heading into perimenopause badly served; and design proof for three gatekeepers: shoppers, healthcare professionals and agentic commerce [00:20].

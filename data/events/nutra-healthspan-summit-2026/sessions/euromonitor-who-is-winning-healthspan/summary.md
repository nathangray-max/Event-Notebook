# Euromonitor: who is winning in the healthspan economy

## Summary
Nick Stene, senior global insight manager for consumer health at Euromonitor, opened the Healthspan Summit. His question was where the money in healthspan is going and which messages earn a premium.

He started with the market. Consumer health grows about 5%, only 1.5% without inflation, and this is the first year more is spent on prevention than on treatment. Medicines sold over the counter and sports protein are both under profit pressure, so money, including billions in deal finance, is moving to premium dietary supplements.

Within healthspan, products that name a specific need or life stage earn a premium, while broad wording appears on cheap and expensive products alike. Ingredients follow a life cycle: proof brings a premium, popularity brings scale, then a price war. Magnesium forms have gone that way and NAD+ is close. Postbiotics are the one he is watching most closely.

His conclusion: lead with proof, know where each ingredient is in its life cycle, segment and price for specific groups such as women entering perimenopause, and design proof for three gatekeepers: shoppers, healthcare professionals and the AI agents that will increasingly shop for them.

## Key takeaways
- Milestone: "the first year in which we have spent more physically on prevention than we have on treatment", and the gap between supplements and OTC will widen [00:20].
- Money is moving to premium dietary supplements, through line extensions, brand actions and M&A; he sees "about eight to $9 billion in motion", some from venture capital and private equity outside health [00:20].
- Healthspan as a funnel: the edge (SKU names directly on age and life stage, including mitochondrial) is niche, growing and earns a premium; broader healthspan wording appears on cheap and expensive products alike; protection messages (immune, antioxidant) cover about half the shelf [00:20].
- Ingredient life cycle: proof brings a premium, popularity brings scale, then price war; magnesium forms "turned into footballs" on price; "popularity is the death knell on premium" [00:20].
- Postbiotics are growing fast, have "really strong connections into GLP-1" and earn one of the strongest premiums; he is watching them most closely [00:20].
- His takeaways: lead with proof; know the ingredient's life stage and the pipeline behind it; segment and price, with women heading into perimenopause badly served; and design proof for three gatekeepers: shoppers, healthcare professionals and agentic commerce [00:20].

# Artificial Intelligence to Rapidly Develop, Improve and Expand Use of Natural Ingredients

## Summary
Paul Spagnuolo, a food science professor at the University of Guelph and co-founder and Chief Scientific Officer of Applied Laboratory Technologies (ALT), declared his conflict of interest and explained why he built an AI tool for ingredient discovery.

For 20 years his lab has applied pharmaceutical drug discovery to food molecules, taking two products, one from avocado, from discovery to clinical trial and market. That path is long, costly and mostly fails, and the claim at the end covers only the indication studied.

AMBROSIA, an agentic AI platform built with a computational biologist, aims to shorten the discovery and validation stages. As a retroactive test they gave it the avocado molecule. He said it reached in one day the target and use his lab had taken 10 years and millions of dollars to find. ALT's slide claims the stages shrink from years to days and from millions to thousands.

He said researchers still have to validate in the lab. Finzelberg was the first partner: in less than a week AMBROSIA pointed to how its extract works, which matched Finzelberg's own research. ALT now works business to business, helping companies understand their ingredients and find new markets for them.

## Key takeaways
- ALT's slide claims AMBROSIA cuts discovery and validation from "5-10 years" and millions of dollars to "5-10 days" and thousands (photo 1); Spagnuolo said it took AMBROSIA one day to reach what took his lab 10 years [20:14].
- In ALT's retroactive test on avocatin B, AMBROSIA analysed 1,284 crystal structures and found 361 potential gene targets, 99 at high confidence, with lipid metabolism and PPAR signalling among the primary pathways (photo 2); Spagnuolo said it pointed to acyl-CoA dehydrogenase and fatty acid oxidation [20:14].
- A claim covers only what was studied: a phase two result on cardiometabolic function "that's our claim", not inflammation or longevity [20:14]. ALT pitches AMBROSIA as a way to find new markets for existing ingredients (photo 3) [30:28].
- AI does not replace the lab: "you still need to flip the switch", with researchers doing the validation [20:14].
- Finzelberg, the first partner, received an answer on its extract in less than a week that matched its own research [30:28]; ALT's slide quotes Finzelberg on Menofelis, its Siberian rhubarb extract for menopause, and AMBROSIA's "multi-target profiles" (photo 4).

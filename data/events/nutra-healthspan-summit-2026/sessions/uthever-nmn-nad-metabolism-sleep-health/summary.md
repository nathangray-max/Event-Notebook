# Mitochondrial Function and Ageing / Advancing Human Clinical Research: UTHEVER NMN from NAD+ Metabolism to Sleep Health

## Summary
Two talks shared this slot, on one EffePharm deck. Professor Colin Selman, a biogerontologist at the University of Glasgow who lists EffePharm beside his university, opened on the science. Mitochondria work less well as animals age, and that decline is one of the hallmarks of ageing. His question was whether it can be slowed.

His answers came from animals. Turning down nutrient sensing, eating fewer calories and giving NMN, a precursor of NAD+, all preserved mitochondrial function in mice. Long-term NMN reduced frailty, and in a preprint it extended the lifespan of female mice. None of his evidence was in people.

JianJun Yu then presented UTHEVER, EffePharm's branded NMN. A trial published in 2022 is the brand's base. The new work is a small sleep trial of EffePharm's own, not yet published: 34 adults who stay up late or sleep poorly took NMN or placebo for eight weeks. Sleep quality, insomnia and fatigue scores ended lower than on placebo. Each result is labelled as within the NMN group or against placebo, and not as a treatment claim.

The talk closed on regulation. EFSA published a positive safety opinion in May 2026, and the deck expects EU novel-food approval in November 2026. EffePharm says that opens pharmacy, beauty and mainstream retail across the EU.

## Key takeaways
- Selman's case, all from worms and mice: mitochondrial function falls with age, and reduced nutrient sensing, a 30% calorie cut and NMN each preserved it in mice; NMN made mitochondria more efficient without adding more of them (slide 5) (slide 7) (slide 8) (slide 9).
- Long-term NMN (about 550 mg/kg a day in drinking water from 13 months of age) is shown reducing frailty in both sexes and extending female lifespan in mice; the source is a 2024 bioRxiv preprint, and the frailty chart is labelled schematic, its values illustrative (slide 10).
- UTHEVER's published trial (Frontiers in Aging, 2022): 66 adults aged 40 to 65 randomised, 62 analysed, 300 mg a day for 60 days; the slide reports a 38% increment in NAD+ levels and 6.5% gains in walking endurance and quality of life, read from the slide image, without saying whether against placebo (slide 13).
- The sleep trial (EffePharm data on file): 34 adults aged 30 to 60, 600 mg a day or placebo for eight weeks. At day 56 the NMN group scored lower than placebo on sleep quality (PSQI, p<0.05), insomnia severity (ISI, p<0.01) and fatigue (FS-14, p<0.05); within the group the scores fell 36.61%, 48.43% and 48.15% from baseline (slide 14) (slide 15) (slide 16) (slide 17).
- Deep sleep rose 27.65 minutes (38.88%) against the NMN group's own baseline (p<0.05); no placebo comparison is shown (slide 18).
- EU novel food: filed August 2023, public consultation completed February 2025, positive EFSA safety assessment published May 2026, approval "expected" in November 2026; the US FDA sent an NDI no-objection letter in November 2025, and the UK application is in progress (slide 20) (slide 21).

## Detail
- Selman: Professor of Biogerontology and Head of the School of Molecular Biosciences, University of Glasgow (since 2013); the slide gives his affiliation as "University of Glasgow; EffePharm". He sits on the editorial boards of Aging Cell and Experimental Gerontology (slide 2).
- Living longer brings age-related disease: cardiovascular, neurodegenerative and liver disease, macular degeneration, sarcopenia, osteoporosis and osteoarthritis, cancer, type 2 diabetes and obesity, and immunosenescence (slide 3).
- Mitochondrial dysfunction is one of the hallmarks of ageing (Lopez-Otin et al., 2013 and 2023), shown across humans, mice, flies and worms (slide 4).
- With age, oxygen consumption falls from about 6.9 at 4 months to 5.7 at 12 months and 4.3 at 21 months (pmol O2 per second per citrate synthase), and ATP content and ATP production fall to roughly 60% and 40% of the younger level, all read from the charts; the species is not named on the slide (Drew et al. 2003; Hempenstall et al. 2012, 2026; Morsci et al. 2016) (slide 5).
- Two routes to preserve mitochondrial function: genetic and dietary (slide 6).
- Nutrient sensing: the insulin, mTORC1 and S6K1 pathway, with IRS and S6K1 marked. The mouse panel lists preserved cognition, immune function, glucose tolerance and cardiac output, less age-related fat, and fewer cataracts, cases of idiopathic dermatitis and osteoporosis (Selman et al. 2008, 2009, 2012; Borland et al. 2024) (slide 7).
- Dietary restriction (30% fewer calories) raised median lifespan in mice from about 22 to about 33 months, "~45–50%", read from the chart. In male mice it raised muscle mitochondrial respiration at 1, 9 and 18 months without adding mitochondrial content (Hempenstall et al. 2012, 2026; Mulvey et al. 2017) (slide 8).
- NMN: male mice given 100 or 300 mg/kg a day in drinking water from 5 to 17 months of age had higher muscle mitochondrial respiration and gene expression with no change in mitochondrial content (Mills et al., Cell Metabolism 2016) (slide 9).
- The summit's partner slide sits between the two talks; EffePharm is a Diamond Partner (slide 11).
- Sleep trial design: randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled; assessments at day 0, 28, 56 and 84, the last four weeks after stopping. The deck shows no day 84 results (slide 14).
- Fatigue (FS-14): 38.52% down at day 28 (−3.06 points) and 48.15% at day 56 (−3.82 points), p<0.001 within the group (slide 15).
- Sleep quality (PSQI): −3.94 points at day 56, p<0.0001 within the group; no day 28 figure is given (slide 16).
- Insomnia severity (ISI): 46.19% down at day 28 (−6.06 points) and 48.43% at day 56 (−6.35 points), p<0.0001 within the group (slide 17).
- Deep sleep: how it was measured is not stated; the slide says a longer duration "suggests more restorative sleep" (slide 18).
- The outcomes slide shows the 36.61% fall in PSQI score as "+36%" overall sleep quality and rounds deep sleep to 28 minutes; its "No Daytime Drowsiness" tile has no figure and no slide behind it in the deck (slide 19).
- The regulatory slide's footer reads "UK Longevity Summit 2026", so it was made for another event. It calls UTHEVER first to file, first through public consultation and first to EFSA final assessment, and says final Union listing is "a procedural next step" (slide 20).
- Elsewhere: Brazil approved at up to 95 mg a day (through partner distributors); Australia, Thailand and India applications in progress; NMN "can be sold normally" in Mexico, Japan and Hong Kong; finished-product declarations in Canada, Malaysia, the Philippines and Vietnam (slide 21).
- Claims on the closing slide: "World's 1st" clinically-proven NMN brand with 3 clinical studies ongoing; 4 quality certifications (Kosher, Halal, ISO 9001, cGMP); No. 1 trademarked NMN ingredient brand in the USA by 2024 B2B sales volume (Euromonitor, research completed December 2025) (slide 22).

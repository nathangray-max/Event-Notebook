# Making Smart Choices About Longevity Supplements

## Summary
Sophie Chabloz of AVEA, a Swiss longevity supplement brand, explained what longevity supplements are and how their customers choose them.

Chabloz set the biohackers aside. Bryan Johnson's 105 pills a day and Ray Kurzweil's 200 are not their market: their customers want to feel well for as long as possible, not to live to 150. No supplement has been proven to extend human lifespan, Chabloz said, but evidence for some molecules in healthy ageing grows every week. Several, such as NAD+, CoQ10 and collagen, decline with age and are hard to get from diet alone.

Their customers are mostly 45 to 65, and 70% are women. They are well read and demanding. They ask for third-party purity tests, read trials and check claims on PubMed and AI tools. They do not want tests or ingredient lists; they want their own problems solved. So AVEA tests every batch in a Swiss lab, uses evidence-based doses, runs its own studies and consumer tests, offers a broad range by need, and educates heavily.

Their closing advice was to cover the basics first: vitamin D3 and K2, omega-3, magnesium and methylated B vitamins. Without them, Chabloz said, customers will not feel the full effect of more advanced longevity supplements.

## Key takeaways
- Longevity means healthspan: "Our customers are not interested in living until 150 maybe not even until 100 what they're interested in is feeling great for as long as possible." No supplement has been proven to extend human lifespan [00:01].
- Their customers are mostly 45 to 65 and 70% are women; Chabloz started AVEA because the longevity space felt "very performance, very male, driven" [00:01].
- Quality is the first filter. AVEA tests each batch of each ingredient in a third-party Swiss lab for heavy metals and purity, and recently rejected a supplier's batch for mercury and went out of stock rather than use it [00:01] (slide 7).
- Customers expect science: evidence-based ingredients and doses from suppliers who ran trials, then tests of the finished formula. AVEA showed a skin study in 58 people (40 women, 18 men) aged 35 to 68, with texture, hydration and elasticity improving over 12 weeks [00:01] (slide 8).
- Customers buy outcomes, not tests or ingredients: most do not want a test, so a broad range by need (skin, blood glucose, joints, gut) matters more than personalisation, which Chabloz said is "not ready yet" [00:01] (slide 9).
- Cover the basics first: vitamin D3 and K2, omega-3, magnesium and methylated B vitamins (especially if vegan) (slide 10) [00:01].

## Detail
- Slide 4 chart, titled as NAD+, CoQ10 and collagen naturally decreasing with age, from 100% at 20: NAD+ 50% at about 52 and 10% at 80; CoQ10 68% at about 40 and 43% at 80; collagen 85% at about 28 and 20% at 80, read from the chart; no source is given (slide 4).
- Slide 3 shows paper titles on spermidine and autophagy, AKG, glycine, NAD+ precursors, fisetin as a senotherapeutic, and an oral collagen peptide skin trial (slide 3). Slide 5 maps AVEA's Longevity Bundle onto the hallmarks of ageing (slide 5).
- Chabloz described longevity supplements as "cellular fine tuning" aimed at the hallmarks of ageing, such as DNA damage, senescent cells, impaired autophagy and nutrient sensing; not a fix for an average deficiency [00:01].
- NMN quality: the slide's headline says most NMN products tested by ChromaDex had virtually none of the active ingredient (slide 7). Chabloz also cited an NUS Singapore study and said "70 or 80%" of NMN products tested did not contain the claimed amount, adding that Chabloz could not remember the exact figure [00:01].
- Chabloz warned that protein powder, collagen and mushroom extracts can carry heavy metals, which may matter over years of daily use [00:01].
- The skin study (slide 8): average texture readings rose from about 60% to about 65%, corneometer hydration from about 36 to about 48, and cutometer elasticity from about 67 to about 70 over 12 weeks, read from the charts; the Visia evenness score (Ebenheit on the image) went from 47% to 73% in one subject after 3 months. No control group is shown. Chabloz said it should be published soon, and that AVEA patented one ingredient in the combination after cell, worm and mouse work [00:01].
- AVEA also works with contract research organisations, case studies, wearables and at-home tests to judge finished products [00:01].
- The range (slide 9): energy and focus (cellular energy via NAD+ increase), skin health, glucose control, vitamins and minerals, cellular renewal (autophagy and senescent cells), joint care, gut balance, and biological-age and epigenetic testing.
- The brand is about four years old (July 2025) with products on sale for three years; four in-house customer care staff, a fifth with a nutrition background about to join; consultations often go back to basics such as vitamin D and magnesium status [00:01].
- Supplements sit on top of a pyramid of mindset and rest, nutrition and movement (slide 11). Chabloz recommends Examine and Consensus to customers for evidence (slide 12) [00:01].
- Recap: specific longevity molecules, reliable supplements, evidence-based formulas, a routine tailored to needs, and the base of D3, K2, omega-3, magnesium and zinc (slide 13).

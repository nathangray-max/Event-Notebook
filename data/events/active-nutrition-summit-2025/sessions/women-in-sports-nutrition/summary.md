# Women in Sports Nutrition

## Summary
A panel in the second day's Female Athlete session on nutrition for active women. The recording starts after the introductions; matched to the programme, the panel was Steffen Oesser (CRI), on collagen peptide studies; James Morton (SiS); the sports dietitian Renee McGregor; and the doctor Nicky Keay (UCL). The chair is not named.

The panel agreed that the old rigid rules for each day of the menstrual cycle have no evidence behind them. Recent studies, Morton said, largely show no difference in substrate use across the cycle, so the advice is back to fundamentals: fuel consistently, treat each woman as an individual, and coach rather than instruct. McGregor's main message was to stop demonising carbohydrates; Keay called a regular period "a free monthly medical check" and warned that missing ovulation can hide behind normal-looking cycles.

Oesser said research in women is growing and described studies of specific collagen peptides on tendons and ligaments, body composition, endurance in women and bone density in women, with results Oesser called outstanding and very promising.

Asked for one nutrient, Keay chose vitamin D across the lifespan, Morton iron for athletes, and McGregor none: needs come first.

## Key takeaways
- Recent studies largely show no difference in substrate metabolism across the menstrual cycle, Morton said; day-by-day cycle rules have no evidence, so fuel consistently [01:22] [13:50].
- Oesser described studies of specific collagen peptides on ligaments and tendons (with a men-and-women analysis), on body composition alongside training, and on endurance in women only, with an "extra benefit" over placebo when combined with training [09:55].
- Oesser also described a published study on bone density in women: one controlled year, then open-label follow-up to four years, where loss stopped and density rose; a second study with two controlled years is "very promising", final results awaited [22:45].
- Keay: in the luteal phase progesterone slightly raises metabolic rate, so women may need a little more food; in a year-long study of dancers, many with periods were not ovulating or made too little progesterone [14:59] [20:48].
- One nutrient each: vitamin D throughout the lifespan (Keay), iron especially for endurance athletes (Morton), none without a needs analysis (McGregor) [25:05] [26:44] [27:46].

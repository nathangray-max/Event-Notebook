# Women in Sports Nutrition

## Summary
A panel on the second day on nutrition for active women and female athletes. The recording starts after the introductions, so only first names are known: Stefan, who spoke on collagen peptide studies he worked on; James, who had given a talk earlier; a sports dietitian; and a medical doctor. The chair is not named.

The panel agreed that the old rigid rules for each day of the menstrual cycle have no evidence behind them. Recent studies, James said, largely show no difference in substrate use across the cycle, so the advice is back to fundamentals: fuel consistently, treat each woman as an individual, and coach rather than instruct. The dietitian's main message was to stop demonising carbohydrates; the doctor called a regular period "a free monthly medical check" and warned that missing ovulation can hide behind normal-looking cycles.

Stefan said research in women is growing and described studies of specific collagen peptides on tendons and ligaments, body composition, endurance in women and bone density in women, with results he called outstanding and very promising.

Asked for one nutrient, the doctor chose vitamin D across the lifespan, James iron for athletes, and the dietitian none: needs come first.

## Key takeaways
- Recent studies largely show no difference in substrate metabolism across the menstrual cycle, James said; day-by-day cycle rules have no evidence, so fuel consistently [01:22] [13:50].
- Stefan described studies of specific collagen peptides on ligaments and tendons (with a men-and-women analysis), on body composition alongside training, and on endurance in women only, with an "extra benefit" over placebo when combined with training [09:55].
- He also described a published study on bone density in women: one controlled year, then open-label follow-up to four years, where loss stopped and density rose; a second study with two controlled years is "very promising", final results awaited [22:45].
- The doctor: in the luteal phase progesterone slightly raises metabolic rate, so women may need a little more food; in a year-long study of dancers, many with periods were not ovulating or made too little progesterone [14:59] [20:48].
- One nutrient each: vitamin D throughout the lifespan (the doctor), iron especially for endurance athletes (James), none without a needs analysis (the dietitian) [25:05] [26:44] [27:46].

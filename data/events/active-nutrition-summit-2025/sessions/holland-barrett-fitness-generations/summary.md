# From Boomers to Zoomers: Fuelling fitness across generations

## Summary
Dr Mark Evans, a Senior Scientist at Holland & Barrett and a sports nutritionist by training, followed the Euromonitor overview with a retailer's view: who buys active nutrition at each age, and how to formulate and talk to each group.

Evans argued that active nutrition is now for everyone, not only athletes, and that one message does not fit all ages. Boomers want mobility and ageing well, Gen X longevity and joints, Millennials convenience and recovery, and Gen Z energy and focus. Supplement use is high in every group, but Gen Z finds products on TikTok and distrusts polished brand content.

Protein was their first example: one nutrient, told differently at each life stage, from growth to muscle repair to sarcopenia and bone health. Creatine was their second. Evans called the evidence for cognition in healthy people very preliminary, noted there is no EFSA claim for it, and showed how brands stack creatine with ingredients that do carry claims so that packs can suggest the link without stating it.

Their close: communication should be planned from the start of product development, and the science team should set efficacious doses.

## Key takeaways
- Hero ingredients shift by generation: Boomers protein, calcium, vitamin D, probiotics and omega-3; Gen X protein, collagen, omega-3 and curcumin; Millennials protein, adaptogens, magnesium, curcumin and creatine; Gen Z creatine, caffeine, electrolytes and ashwagandha (slide 11).
- Supplement use is high across ages: 79% of Boomers, 78% of Gen X and 81% of Millennials (CRN 2023 survey), and 70% of Gen Z in the US and UK call TikTok their most valuable source of food recommendations (Statista 2024) (slide 9) (slide 10).
- On creatine and cognition, Evans said there is "very preliminary evidence" in healthy people and that the strongest evidence is in cognitively impaired people; their slide says there are no EFSA-approved claims for creatine and cognition [00:00] (slide 13).
- Their stacking table: creatine 3 to 5 g/day (no EFSA claim); L-theanine 200 to 250 mg/day with 150 mg caffeine; caffeine over 75 mg a serving; B vitamins at 20% RI; Rhodiola rosea 576 mg/day for mental energy and attention (no EFSA claim); pantothenic acid at 15% NRV for normal mental performance (slide 14).
- Protein is one nutrient with many stories: growth for children, muscle repair for adults, convenient high protein, mobility, sarcopenia and bone health for older adults [00:00].
- Plan communication from the start: "start with the end in mind" (slide 16) [00:00].

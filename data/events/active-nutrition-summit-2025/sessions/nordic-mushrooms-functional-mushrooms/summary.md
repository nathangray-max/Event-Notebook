# Functional Mushrooms

## Summary
Eric Puro, co-founder and CEO of Nordic Mushrooms, which makes mushroom extracts in Finland, made the case for functional mushrooms in brain health and then warned that much of the market does not contain what its labels say.

He opened with growth: his slides put the global functional mushroom market on course for $65B by 2030. His lead example was lion's mane for the brain: he presented nerve growth factor and BDNF as the mechanism, and his company's human trial in healthy adults, which his slide says showed better working memory, complex attention and reaction time, with effects building week on week where caffeine's fade.

Then the problem. In an Italian study, 13 of 19 mushroom supplements did not contain the listed mushroom; in a US study, 74% of reishi supplements did not contain the listed ingredients. He named poor-quality material from China as the industry's biggest risk, and argued that the usual quality markers, polysaccharides and beta-glucans, are not what delivers the benefits. Each species should be specified on its own compounds: hericenones and hericenes for lion's mane, cordycepin for cordyceps, ganoderic and lucidenic acids for reishi, inotodiol for chaga.

He closed with his NordRelease extraction, which his slide credits with 92% bioavailability, and trials under way on lion's mane and chaga.

## Key takeaways
- Market: from $25B to $65B "in ten years" on one slide, and $31B growing to $65B by 2030 at 11% CAGR on another, both credited to Horizon Grand View Research; growth of 31% in 2023, credited to the American Botanical Council (slide 3) (slide 4) (slide 5).
- His company's lion's mane trial: healthy adults, four weeks, 50 participants, lion's mane extract against caffeine and placebo (La Monica et al. 2023); his slide says lion's mane "improves cognitive performance, enhancing working memory, complex attention, and reaction time" (slide 11) (slide 12).
- Quality failures: 13 of 19 Italian mushroom supplements did not contain the listed mushroom; 74% of US reishi supplements did not contain the listed ingredients, 58% showed no reishi triterpenes, and a key reishi polysaccharide was found in only 26.3% (slide 16) (slide 18).
- "The biggest industry risk is poor quality material from China." (slide 19)
- Polysaccharides and beta-glucans are, he said, not responsible for the benefits people want; quality should be specified on each species' own compounds (slide 21) (slide 22) (slide 23).
- Extraction, as his slide puts it: water 5% bioavailability, dual alcohol-and-water extraction 50% and flagged as "Novel non EU approved extraction technology", NordRelease 92% (slide 25).

## Detail
- Slide numbers are the PDF's pages (28). The title slide reads "Honest and potent mushroom extracts from Finland"; several slides are headed "KÄÄPÄ Mushrooms", and his contact and the NordRelease news link are at Kääpä Biotech (slide 1) (slide 9) (slide 25) (slide 28).

Slide 4 carries both a Grand View label and the American Botanical Council Market Report Issue 141 as sources for the 31% figure (slide 4).
- A large market means a large body of research: "1586 results for Reishi alone" (slide 6).
- Lion's mane, as his slides put it: nerve growth factor stimulation, BDNF, "reduction of amyloid beta plaque build-up", neuroprotective and neurocognitive benefits, gastroprotective effects; primary compounds hericenones and hericenes. The problem slide reads "Loss of NGF", and a footnote says depression correlates with significantly lower NGF levels (Chen et al. 2015) (slide 7) (slide 9) (slide 10).
- The paper shown is La Monica et al., "Acute Effects of Naturally Occurring Guayusa Tea and Nordic Lion's Mane Extracts on Cognitive Performance", Nutrients 2023; 15(24): 5018. The slide calls it the "first human clinical research with healthy adults" (slide 11).
- His slide's further points on the trial: "Results steadily growing weekly", a "stacking effect" of neuroregeneration in which "the impact on mood and cognition effectively gets better every time", and caffeine's effect diminishing over time "because the body builds tolerance" (slide 12).
- Myelin: quoted findings that H. erinaceus extract and its compounds HeA and HeS "display promising functional effects and promote OL maturation", and a regulatory effect on myelin genesis in vitro, under the heading "Potential for myelin regeneration" (slide 13).
- The Italian study is Risoli et al., "Mushroom-Based Supplements in Italy: Let's Open Pandora's Box" (PubMed 36771482): most facilities lacked internationally recognised GMP and some products had contaminants above legal limits (slide 15) (slide 16).
- The US study is Wu et al., "Evaluation on Quality Consistency of Ganoderma Lucidum (Reishi) Dietary Supplements Collected in the United States", Scientific Reports 2017; it concluded quality consistency "was poor" (slide 17) (slide 18).
- Quality markers he showed as current: beta-glucans (a Nammex white paper), polysaccharides (Chinese products); his alternative is "bioactive compounds (beyond beta glucans)" (slide 22).
- Compounds and benefits by species: chaga, inotodiol, for longevity and antioxidants; reishi, ganoderic and lucidenic acids, for calmness and sleep; lion's mane, hericenones and hericenes, for brain health and focus; cordyceps, cordycepin and adenosine, for energy and endurance (slide 23).
- History: Ötzi the Iceman (3230 BC) carried two polypores, one probably used medicinally; mushrooms in Han Dynasty medicine (202 to 220 AD); NordRelease introduced in 2019, with a linked news item titled as an EU approval of Kääpä Biotech's NordRelease extract powders (slide 25).
- Trials under way: lion's mane and neurogenesis over 3 months; chaga and stem cell regeneration in healthy ageing; chaga and mitochondrial function and longevity (slide 27).

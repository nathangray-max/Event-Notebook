# Branding that moves the right consumer in Active Nutrition

## Summary
Marit Veenstra, a brand and innovation strategist at HMT, the Healthy Marketing Team, argued that active nutrition brands lose sales to confusion, not to weak science. Shoppers face too many options and unclear benefits, and many walk away without buying. Most launches fail, she said, because no one understood, trusted or felt anything for them.

Her answer is HMT's FourFactors: a consumer must see who needs the product, accept the ingredient, understand the benefit and trust the brand. The factors sit on an adoption curve that runs from technology and lifestyle buyers to the early mass market and the mass market. Niche trends start with low awareness; embedded trends are familiar to everyone.

She then split active consumers into four mindsets: professional athletes driven by need and proof, fitness influencers driven by beliefs and novelty, recreational sportspeople who want easy solutions, and "daily strollers" who want what is normal and familiar. Each needs its own message and its own kind of proof. Her examples were Upfront's protein matcha, sold in Dutch-language packs, and Heinz's lycopene drink.

Her conclusion: know where to play and how to win, and translate the message as a product moves from medical to trend to mass market.

## Key takeaways
- Shoppers are confused: 73% feel overwhelmed by too many choices, 72% are confused by how benefits are explained, and 74% have walked away without buying (Consumer Pulse Survey 2024, Waitrose 2024) (slide 6).
- 80% of new launches fail within 12 months, she said, not for weak science but because no one understood, trusted or felt anything for them (slide 8).
- The FourFactors test for a concept: who needs it, is the ingredient accepted, is the benefit understood, is the brand trusted (slide 11) (slide 34).
- There is not one active consumer but four mindsets, Need, Want, Solution and Normal, each with its own message and trust strategy (slide 19) (slide 35).
- The professional athlete wants scientific proof, health claims and rational branding; the daily stroller wants taste, no change in habit and a mass-price brand (slide 20) (slide 23).
- Crossing from medical to trend to mass market needs different messages, benefits and proof at each step (slide 35).

- Trends: niche trends enter with low awareness, emerging trends head towards the early mass market, embedded trends are familiar to all in the mass market (slide 16). The sticky notes on her title image read "individual health", "protein shift" and "gut health" (slide 13).
- Fitness influencers want products that fit them, match their values, are trendy, inspire them and make them feel special; recreational sportspeople want familiar, convenient, tasty products that fit their routines (slide 21) (slide 22).

- Her six closing takeouts: confused consumers walk away; most innovations fail due to irrelevance; trends do not matter until they matter to your consumer; four mindsets; technology without storytelling stays invisible; crossing the chasm requires translation (slide 35).

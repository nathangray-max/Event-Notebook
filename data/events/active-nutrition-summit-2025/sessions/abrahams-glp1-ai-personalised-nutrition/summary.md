# GLP-1, AI and Data for Consumer Health & Personalised Nutrition

## Summary
Mariette Abrahams, founder and CEO of Qina, a data and consultancy business for personalised nutrition, described how health, food, pharma and technology are converging, and what that means for brands.

Their first trend was discovery. Consumers now move between Google, ChatGPT and reviews before they buy; "Healthy Living" rose from 75th to 10th among uses of generative AI in a year; and Shopify's link with ChatGPT lets people buy inside the chat. Wearables, AI coaches and gyms are becoming places where health advice happens, and pharma, Bayer in particular, is moving into prevention.

Their second was GLP-1. Prescriptions to adults rose 586.7% over five years, and Abrahams said Europe is two to three years behind the US. One study found GLP-1 users short of vitamin K, magnesium, choline, iron, protein and fibre, though Abrahams stressed it was small and there is much to learn. Consumers will want help while on treatment, coming off it, or instead of it.

Their advice to brands: formulate for nutrient density, smaller appetites and changing body composition; find digital partners; show clinical proof through digital experiences; and lean into AI, because a world without websites and apps may be coming.

## Key takeaways
- The number of adults prescribed a GLP-1 rose 586.7% over five years; Abrahams said about 70% are in the US and that some experts put Europe two to three years behind: "So the wave is forthcoming." [00:07] (slide 19).
- In a study of people on GLP-1 treatment, intake was low in vitamin K, magnesium, choline and iron, and low in protein and fibre; Abrahams called it one study of 60 people and said "we still have an awful lot to learn" [00:07] (slide 20).
- Consumers will look for solutions while on treatment, instead of it, or when coming off it, with concerns over muscle mass, lean mass and nutrient deficiency [00:07].
- AI is changing discovery: on Harvard Business Review's list, healthy living rose from number 75 to number 10 among generative AI uses in a year, and Shopify's ChatGPT link lets consumers buy without visiting a website [00:07] (slide 10) (slide 12).
- On Qina's tracking of features in personalised health solutions, the slide shows food logging (+20%), biomarker testing (+11%), habit tracking, and AI coaches and assistants (54%); Abrahams spoke of a rapid increase in AI coaches [00:07] (slide 14).
- Their four opportunities for brands: formulate better (nutrient density, smaller appetites, body composition, holistic health), partner better, create digital experiences that show clinical proof, and lean into AI (slide 25) (slide 26) (slide 27) (slide 28).

## Detail
- Qina combines "proprietary data, digital tools and domain expertise to help companies innovate faster and smarter" (slide 2).
- On their hype-cycle slide the technologies are agentic AI, generative AI, computer vision, natural language processing and machine learning; the trends are longevity, food as medicine, active nutrition, women's health, transparency, gut health and GLP-1 (slide 5). Abrahams said agentic AI is at the peak and GLP-1 has dipped as the drugs became widely available [00:07].
- Investment is flowing to DIY biomarker kits (vitamin D levels, for example), access to nutrition experts, smart eating and body-scan devices [00:07]. Notable 2025 investments on the slide include Bioniq, Superpower, Allora, Alba, Welltheory, Hormona, Nourish, Culina, Moom, Biohm, Epicore Biolinq, Aeon, Prenuvo and Habitual (slide 6).
- McKinsey 2025 segments: max optimizers 25% of consumers and 40% of wellness spend; confident enthusiasts 11% and 15%; health traditionalists 20% and 13%; health strugglers 24% and 20% (slide 7).
- Abrahams said younger consumers use ChatGPT as an assistant ("Shall I take my shape before exercise?"), older ones in place of Google; on Qina's data the number of solutions using generative AI had tripled by April 2025 [00:07] (slide 11).
- Partnerships: Dexcom and "aura" (as heard; glucose with sleep data), and a wearable company that pivoted into nutrition [00:07] (slide 15). Gyms are becoming places of health, with doctors, nutritionists and prescriptions inside [00:07].
- Bayer: bought a personalised supplement company for "two 50 million" and later sold it, launched a longevity offer (a genetic test, an app and a non-personalised supplement), bought an inflammatory bowel disease app, and bought top-selling Amazon supplements, which include botanicals [00:07] (slide 17).
- Levels of GLP-1 personalisation on their slide: GLP-1 companion products (food and ingredients), medical food, "phygital" products with generative or agentic AI, and digital plus biological, with digital twin technology (slide 21). Examples Abrahams gave: GLP-1-friendly labelled products, a doctor-prescribed medical food, a high-protein Abbott medical nutrition product, an agentic AI chatbot for GLP-1 users, a UK start-up selling prescription, drug and companion products together, and DNA plus microbiome tests to predict GLP-1 response [00:07].
- Abrahams said a body heard as "the American lifestyle Association" published guidance on behavioural, psychological, exercise and nutrition support for GLP-1 users, and that GLP-1 users adopt wearables and exercise more [00:07].
- Transparency: consumers scan everything, supplements and shakes included, with ingredient apps [00:07] (slide 22). Competition is shifting: consumers want simplicity and transparency, expect personalisation and demand proof of efficacy (slide 23).
- Looking ahead: virtual healthcare marketplaces where consumers upload their own data and ask what to buy, and tech companies' smart glasses pointing to "a world of no websites and no apps" [00:07] (slide 31).

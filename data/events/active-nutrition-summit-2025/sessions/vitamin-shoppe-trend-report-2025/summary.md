# The Vitamin Shoppe Health & Wellness Trend Report 2025

## Summary
The Vitamin Shoppe, a US supplement retailer with over 635 stores, published its fourth annual trend report in 2025. Nathan filed it with this summit; whether it was presented there is not known. Its figures are the report's own: the retailer's sales and site searches, SPINS industry data, and a survey of 2,000 US adults by Talker Research in May 2025.

The report finds a proactive consumer: 69% of Americans take supplements, and 35% have used AI tools for their health. It names five trends. Protein is moving from powders into drinks, bars and snacks. NAD+ has gone mainstream for longevity. Peptides are the next frontier in sports nutrition. Formats are moving beyond pills and powders, to liquids, gummies, chews, beadlets and topicals. And shilajit is finding a new generation of buyers.

Creatine runs through two of the trends: the category has grown over 320% at the retailer since 2019, and searches for creatine gummies are up over 1,300% this year.

It closes on trust: TikTok drives curiosity, but friends, news and doctors still build credibility, and only 11% of people find influencers very trustworthy.

## Key takeaways
- Protein formats: RTD protein drinks up 20% and bars and snacks up 28% year to date, while powders fell 12%; 66% of survey respondents are trying to add protein (page 3).
- NAD+: site searches up nearly 500%; the cellular health and healthy ageing category grew 91% in the first quarter, with NAD+ 90% of that growth (page 5).
- Peptides: searches up over 550% in 2025, and 41% of consumers are more aware of peptides than five years ago; creatine with PeptiStrong is a top seller (page 7) (page 8).
- Creatine formats: the category is up over 320% since 2019 at the retailer; "creatine gummies" searches are up over 1,300%; SPINS shows liquids, chewables and gummies growing faster than powders in creatine (page 9).
- Trust: doctors (59%), family (40%) and friends (28%) are trusted most; only 11% rate influencers very trustworthy and 22% not trustworthy at all (page 12).

## Detail
- The original is filed unchanged: files/the-vitamin-shoppe-health-wellness-trend-report-2025.pdf. Page numbers here are the file's pages; most hold a two-page printed spread.
- Sources: data from 635+ stores and vitaminshoppe.com, industry data from SPINS, and the survey: 2,000 US adults, 16 to 22 May 2025, random double-opt-in, margin of error ±2.2% at 95% confidence (page 2).
- Survey headlines: 56% are likely to try new trends; 31% feel healthier than five years ago, 35% less healthy and 30% no different; 35% have used AI for their health and 27% would consider it; AI uses include research (31%), meal planning (25%), weight-loss programmes (24%) and supplement use (21%) (page 2).
- Protein: SPINS shows the protein supplement and meal replacement category up 9.1% in 2024; "protein" searches up 81% (page 3). Clear protein RTDs are a breakout: Clean Simple Eats (20 g whey isolate a can) and RYSE (22 g a bottle) (page 3). Barebells is up 97% and the top bar of 30 brands; the David bar (28 g protein, 150 calories, 0 g sugar) is second within months; Built "Puff", with whey and collagen, reached fifth (page 3) (page 4). Protein snacks: Legendary Foods, Quest, Vaughn's (page 4).
- NAD+: the report says NAD+ levels fall by up to 50% between 40 and 60 (page 5). 75% of respondents are concerned about ageing; supplements are the top longevity activity (43%), ahead of seeing a doctor (40%) and exercise (39%) (page 6). Life Extension, with Niagen NR, grew 127% in the first quarter; Tru Niagen and Codeage liposomal NAD+ are also named (page 6).
- Peptides: 16% are a lot and 25% somewhat more aware; 30% do not know what peptides are (page 8). AN Performance Creatine + Peptide (PeptiStrong, by Nuritas) is described by the report as "clinically proven" for strength, endurance and recovery; Nuritas says three human trials show muscle benefits (page 8). PeptiStrong also appears in Force Factor's Anabolic Muscle Builder and Whole Health Rx's GLP-1 Support Protein (page 8).
- Coming peptide launches: MuscleTech creatine and DL185 dileucine products, said to give "159% greater activation of muscle protein synthesis"; BodyTech Elite products with DL185 and myHMB, VasoDrive AP, and PeptiStrong with MalTor (page 8).
- Formats: liquid vitamins up 50% year to date, MaryRuth's up 90%, New Chapter entering liquids, "liquid multivitamin" searches up 31% (page 9). Ashwagandha (KSM-66) honey sticks and a shilajit syrup (page 9). ANIMAL creatine chews up over 100%; Force Factor, Legion, Codeage and BodyTech have launched creatine chews and gummies (page 9). BodyTech Elite launched the first creatine beadlet, with Specnova's NovaQSpheres (page 10). Topicals: castor oil roll-on, magnesium spray and magnesium tape (page 10).
- Format preferences: 65% of supplement users take pills or capsules; 34% say more format variety would make supplements easier to take, and 20% that it would help consistency (page 10). The other figures on the format chart cannot be paired with labels from the text (page 9).
- Shilajit: sales up over 40% in the first quarter, searches up 52%; BetterAlt took 25% of shilajit sales within months, combining it with sea moss, ashwagandha and tongkat ali; Codeage's GLP-Harmony Companion+ includes it (page 11). Top wellness concerns: energy (49%), immunity (38%), cognitive function (37%), bone health (35%) (page 11).
- How people discover trends: friends 30%, news media 29%, other social media 28%, family 23%, adverts 21%, brands 16%, TikTok 15% (page 12).

# From Insight to Innovation: Mapping Trends in Functional Health Ingredients

## Summary
Harini Venkataraman, Associate Research Director at Lux Research, mapped where functional health ingredients are going. Lux is a research and advisory firm that tracks innovation signals such as patents and investment.

She opened with three areas gaining momentum: phytochemicals, the gut-brain axis and small "shot" beverages. The space is crowded. Different ingredients can deliver the same benefit, so choosing between them is hard for brands and consumers, and ingredients with several benefits have an edge.

She then took three consumer areas. Performance is mainstream: consumers agree it means building, sustaining and recovering muscle, and Lux expects strong growth over the next one to two years. Protein is table stakes, and precision fermentation may diversify protein sources over about five years, with more than 30 startups working on dairy proteins. Beauty from within is moving towards mainstream acceptance. Immune health is still at early consensus, because consumers see it as personal.

Her closing advice was to back ingredients with science and clinical evidence, solve formulation and flavour problems, partner with established ingredient players, and check what consumers mean by "performance" or "beauty" before building a product for it.

## Key takeaways
- Three momentum areas: rising phytochemicals, growing interest in the gut-brain axis, and small-volume "shot" beverages (slide 5) [02:19].
- Connected impacts: different ingredients can confer the same benefit, which makes ingredient selection complex; ingredients addressing several benefits "can provide an edge" (slide 7) (slide 33).
- Performance is in mainstream consensus, with strong growth expected over the next 1 to 2 years. Consumers see it as building, sustaining and recovering muscle; the ingredients they name are protein, creatine, beta-alanine, curcumin and CBD (slide 14) (slide 15).
- Precision fermentation: more than 30 startups focus on dairy proteins; Perfect Day is the most mature, followed by ReMilk and Formo; DSM and Fonterra's Vivici targets beta-lactoglobulin, including for sports nutrition. She doubted it would hit the price now; she saw it as a path over "the next, let's say, five years or so" (slide 16) (slide 17) [02:19].
- Beauty from within is moving from early consensus to mainstream acceptance; immune health stays in early consensus with steady growth expected, because consumers see it as personal (slide 20) (slide 26).
- Her takeaways: back ingredients with clinical evidence that overcome formulation and flavour hurdles, collaborate with established ingredient players, and check how consumers define the category (slide 33).

## Detail
- Lux: founded in 2004, research hubs in Boston, Toronto, Amsterdam, Singapore and Tokyo, more than 3,000 syndicated reports a year (slide 2). She said she leads consumer product innovation research and previously worked at DSM [00:00] [02:19].
- The bioactive "boom" (slide 6): phytochemicals and plant extracts, antioxidants (including CoQ10 and glutathione), functional proteins and peptides (collagen, casein, elastin, plant proteins, amino acids), functional sugars and HMOs, prebiotics, probiotics, vitamins and minerals, lipids (omega-3, -6, -9, DHA, phytosterols).
- Phytochemicals: extraction and processing innovations aim to improve yield, purity and bioavailability of flavonoids, terpenoids and carotenoids for metabolic health (slide 8). She called getting them out in high quantities, formulating them and achieving bioavailability the biggest challenge [02:19].
- Other technology areas she named: microbiome modulators, including fibres effective at lower doses; fermentation-derived ingredients (lipids, proteins), if EU regulation and scale allow; AI for microbiome profiling, where she said earlier movers such as Day Two were not successful; encapsulation and new processing for bioavailability [02:19].
- Lux's consumer method maps conversations on a maturity curve from ideas to early consensus, mainstream acceptance and established ideas; performance shows strong consensus [02:19] (slide 14).
- Consumer voices on performance: recovery to sustain gains, and muscle and strength from training and high protein intake (slide 13). She said protein is "probably less than $10 per kilogram" [02:19].
- Beauty from within: consumers want natural ingredients that work with the body; named ingredients are adaptogens, botanical extracts, spices (cumin, cinnamon, turmeric), collagen and ceramides, and omega-3 (slide 19) (slide 21).
- Functional mushrooms: most developers focus on lion's mane and reishi, almost all target more than two types, mainly fruiting bodies; beverages are a key application (slide 22).
- The Every Company and Landish: a protein and adaptogen coffee premix with fermentation-derived ovalbumin, 8 g protein a serving, sold direct to consumer at $49; Lux's take is that it should strengthen its B2B strategy (slide 23). The spoken price is unclear in the recording [21:14].
- Immune health consumer ingredients: probiotics, prebiotics, vitamins A, C and D, zinc and enzymes (slide 27). She sees potential in enzymes, and said large corporates, IFF among them, are moving into postbiotics because they ease formulation, while consumers struggle to tell them apart [21:14].
- Prebiotics: immune-modulating, naturally sweet prebiotics are moving beyond gut health; Lux advises upcycled soluble fibre with several benefits (slide 28). Example: NutriLeads' BeniCaros, a carrot-derived polysaccharide described as clinically backed for immune health, distributed exclusively in the US and Canada (slide 29).
- Outlook: discovering and scaling a novel ingredient is still risky and takes years (slide 31). She expects metabolic and digestive health to evolve further with GLP-1 [23:04].
- Marking: every slide carries "Lux Proprietary and Confidential". This is the company's own marking on a talk given at a public conference (slide 1).

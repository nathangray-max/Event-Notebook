# Nutrigenomics, AI & Next-Gen Supplements

## Summary
Marimiina Pykälistö, a certified nutritionist, presented nutritional genomics as the basis for a new generation of supplements. This summary comes from their deck alone; there is no recording.

Their starting point is that genes do not explain health or illness on their own: humans share 99.9% of their DNA, and lifestyle, environment and nutrition shape how genes are expressed. Pykälistö separates epigenetics, nutrigenomics (how nutrition affects gene expression) and nutrigenetics (how nutrition affects people with different genetic variations).

Food, in their framing, is information, not only calories. Cofactors are the building blocks of epigenetic change, epinutrients modulate epigenetic marks, and transcription factors control how DNA is read. Plant compounds act as epinutrients, with effects Pykälistö describes as balancing and short-lived, and they cannot replace cofactors. Common genetic variations change how much of each a person needs.

Their new generation supplements combine all three: cofactors, epinutrients and transcription-factor modulators. Their examples for metabolic health pair minerals and CoQ10 with botanicals such as berberine and cinnamon, and curcumin, vitamin D and resveratrol. Pykälistö ends on new tools for sequencing, nutrient-gene analysis and phenotyping as the future of healthcare.

## Key takeaways
- Genes alone do not explain health: humans share 99.9% of DNA, and lifestyle, environment and nutrition shape gene expression ("Genes load the gun, lifestyle pulls the trigger") (slide 2).
- Nutrigenomics studies the effect of nutrition on gene expression; nutrigenetics how nutrition affects different genetic variations (slide 3).
- Food as information: cofactors, epinutrients and transcription factors work together, and genetic variations change how the body benefits from nutrients (slide 5) (slide 8).
- Plant compounds as epinutrients are "Multifactorial and polygenetic", "Balancing rather than one-directional" and "More short-lived than long-term", and cannot replace cofactors (slide 7).
- Their metabolic example combines cofactors (chrome, zinc, ALA, CoQ10, L-carnitine), epinutrients (berberine, acacia fiber, cinnamon) and transcription factors (curcumin, vitamin D, resveratrol), sourced to the Institute for Functional Medicine (slide 10).

## Detail
- Epigenetics was "born" in 2003, when "the scientific world was shocked" that genes do not explain illness or health, sourced to Natural Therapies Clinic, 2.8.2024 (slide 2).
- DNA methylation, histone acetylation and non-coding RNA are the best-known epigenetic processes; cofactors, often enzymes, enable them, and epinutrients influence them (University of Utah) (slide 6).
- Individual variations (SNPs) affect enzyme function and change the quantity and quality of epinutrients and cofactors needed (slide 8).
- Slide 9, headed "Ex. 1 Immunity Booster" with text about metabolic health: cofactors Sod, manganese, copper, zinc, iron and sulphur; epinutrients rosemary, beetroot, pomegranate; transcription factors fatty acids and sulforaphane (source 10.1631/jzus.B1900614) (slide 9).
- Today's tools: genetic sequencing, nutrient and gene analysis, and phenotype analysis (slide 11); the final content slide is titled "The future of healthcare" (slide 12).
- The deck names no product, brand or study results of their own, and does not mention AI despite the billed title.

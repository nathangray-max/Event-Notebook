Creatine deficit in elderly
 Reduced endogenous synthesis
Decline in AGAT and GAMT activity leading to lower endogenous creatine production
 Decreased dietary intake
Elderly often consume less dietary creatine, particularly if they follow low-protein or plant-
based diets. This reduces exogenous creatine availability.
 Muscle creatine decline
Age-related loss of skeletal muscle mass (sarcopenia) is linked to lower total body creatine
stores. Aging muscle may also have reduced creatine uptake due to changes in the expression or
function of the creatine transporter (SLC6A8).
 Potential impairment in creatine transport
Limited evidence suggests that the efficiency of creatine transport into tissues may decline with
age, although this is still under investigation.
 Altered energy demands and utilization
Because creatine is central to cellular energy buffering via the phosphocreatine system,
impairments in creatine metabolism may contribute to reduced energy availability, muscle
weakness, and fatigue in older adults.
Front Physiol. 2024 Dec 3;15:1496544
J Clin Med. 2019 Apr 11;8(4):488.
Subcell Biochem. 2018;90:145-168.

Fortifying food with creatine?
 UN climate-change report has called for a transformation of the human diet
 Reducing meat production and consumption
 Eating less meat could lead to a low intake of several meat-specific nutrients (creatine)
 Adding creatine to various meat-free foods could be a feasible public health intervention
 Prevent a large-scale creatine malnutrition
 Reducing meat consumption and promoting greenhouse-gas mitigation
 Convenient in some populations (elderly)
 Creatine can be produced industrially by several environmentally friendly processes
 Creatine could be efficiently added to foods commonly consumed by the general
 Market-driven fortification = voluntarily enrich convenient foods with creatine
 Second step = mass fortification mandated and regulated by the government sector
 The favorable safety and the bioavailability of dietary creatine support this approach
 Establishing an appropriate level of fortification

Take home message
 Creatine appears to be a semi-essential nutrient for humans
 Many populations, including elderly, suffer from low dietary intake of creatine
 Solid links between benefits of dietary creatine and health from the first round of population studies
 Evidence of supplemental creatine benefits from interventional studies (muscle, bone, brain etc)
 Relatively safe although more long-term studies in elderly are needed
 Creatine is a promising food component for public health nutrition for healthy aging
 From creatine-rich foods and dietary supplements to foods fortified with creatine

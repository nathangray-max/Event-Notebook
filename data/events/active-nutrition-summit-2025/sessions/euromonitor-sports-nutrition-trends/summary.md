# Sports nutrition market: consumer trends and innovation in Europe

## Summary
Aishwarya Rajpara, a Beauty and Health Consultant at Euromonitor International, opened the first day with the state of the European sports nutrition market, drawn from Euromonitor's market data, its 2025 consumer survey and its tracker of new product launches.

The global market reached USD 30.6 billion in 2024, and Europe holds 19% of it. Protein powder is still the largest category in Europe, but ready-to-drink protein grew fastest. Consumers now link health to mental wellbeing and stress, and European women report more stress than men and are turning to strength training.

Launches tell the same story. Low sugar is the feature consumers want most, convenience is their top priority, and clean-label and "free from" claims grew fastest. Sports supplements were the fastest-growing launch area, led by electrolytes and creatine, while milk protein supplement launches fell. Creatine use rose in every age group.

She closed on three trends: convenience, transparency and a holistic approach that reaches beyond protein, with women as the clearest group to grow. In questions she said consumers expect sustainability as standard rather than pay a premium for it.

## Key takeaways
- The global sports nutrition market was USD 30.6 billion in 2024; Europe holds 19% of sales, over 80% of that from Western Europe, and Europe has the second-highest two-year growth after Latin America (slide 9).
- In Europe, protein powder is 43% of sports nutrition sales; protein bars grew 11% a year from 2022 to 2024 against an 8% global average, and RTD shows the highest growth (slide 12).
- Sports supplement launches rose 33%, with the highest increase in electrolytes and creatine, while milk protein supplement launches fell 21% (slide 17).
- The slide says creatine is "Building momentum in post-workout routine" (slide 18). She said its use rose in all age groups, and that "new studies that claim" effects beyond muscle, on cognitive function and memory, are one reason [00:00].
- Low sugar is the product feature most wanted, convenience the top priority and clean label still in demand; word of mouth and web browsing drive awareness more than experts or celebrities (slide 14).
- European women report 21% higher stress than men, physical activity is the first way consumers tackle stress, and more women do strength training (slide 11) [00:00].

- Desired features, in the chart's order: low in sugar, ready-to-drink, bar format, clean ingredient list, portable, powder, electrolytes, caffeine, free from animal products, gel (slide 14).
- Launches: over 8,000 new and sub-brand launches in sports nutrition in Europe in 2024; clean label claims grew over 100%; health claims and flavour lead innovation (slide 15).
- Claims: sustained focus on high-protein claims, fortification rising, "no added sugar" claims up 32%, gluten-free and lactose-free gaining; natural claims drive growth, with organic and sustainable sourcing growing steadily (slide 16).

- Consumption reasons: performance is the key reason; beyond protein, the focus is hydration and recovery speed (slide 18). She said strength and muscle tone remain the first reason and hydration is rising [00:00].
- Her three closing trends: convenience (accessibility, flexible packaging), transparency (nutrition information, ingredient awareness, sustainability impact) and a holistic approach (broader consumption, beyond protein) (slide 20).

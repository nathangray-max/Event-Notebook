# Active Nutrition Summit 2025: Key Insights & Industry Trends

## Summary
This is a secondary synthesis of the whole summit, not a talk. It is a 20-page written report of unknown authorship and date that says it "synthesizes all presentations". Its figures are the report's own, as it states them, not the speakers' figures as given in the room.

It groups the summit into themes: market dynamics and consumer trends, gut health and the microbiome, weight management and GLP-1, female athlete nutrition, personalisation and AI, longevity and "ageless" consumption, mind health, and regulation and trust. It closes with white spaces, investment areas and an outlook.

It opens with a global market of USD 30.6 billion in 2024, Europe at 19%, and growth projected from USD 28.4 billion to USD 37.8 billion by 2028; the two figures do not agree and no source is given. It then summarises most named talks, from Paul Cotter's athlete microbiome research to Luca Bucchini's regulatory update.

It is useful as a map of the programme and of talks with no session of their own. Some attributions are loose: buyer figures from Biogena's talk also appear under the Avea talk, and some sections reword the hosts' closing recap.

## Key takeaways
- By generation, the report names creatine as a hero ingredient for Millennials and Generation Z, with protein for all four generations (page 2).
- Euromonitor data as the report gives it: new product launches up 33%, electrolytes and creatine leading, milk protein supplements down 21%, "no added sugar" claims up 32% (page 2) (page 3).
- Rousselot's Nextida GC collagen peptides, which the report says boost GLP-1 secretion, with a 42% reduction in glucose spike taken before a meal (page 6).
- Creatine as a semi-essential nutrient for the general population, with food fortification as a public health route (Ostojic, as the report puts it) (page 11).
- Misinformation figures attributed to Alice Millbank: only 2.1% of social media nutrition content aligned with public health guidelines; 59% of the accounts studied had no formal health qualification (page 14) (page 15).
